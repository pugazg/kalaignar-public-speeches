#### Little Stories! Monkey Show!

Little stories—not one, not two, but three little stories I am going to tell you, comrades! I am going to tell them.

Yes. I am going to tell little stories precisely in order to explain the chaotic condition of Achariyar's rule, which passes the time telling little stories, talking in tales over and over.

To give explanations about Kulluka Bhattar, who speaks in little stories, I summon little stories themselves to my aid. Listen, comrades, listen! Listen closely to the little stories.

A first-rate gambler! Yes, he is indeed a first-rate gambler. Gambling is his work, his life, the very aim of his existence.

He starts gambling in the morning. Not even concern for his morning duties arises in him. He gambles and gambles, spending the time gambling.

### PDF page 9 — printed page 8

He keeps gambling without knowing that time is passing, without any concern for the changes of time—morning, noon, evening, night.

He is a lazy fellow, a gambler, without the slightest concern for food or thought of sleep.

He forcibly drags in all who come and go to gamble and picks quarrels with them. He wrangles.

Apart from gambling, he has no connection or conversation with others in any other work.

His gambling mania grew to such an extent that the townspeople would be frightened on seeing him.

Suddenly one day, at a gathering where the townspeople had assembled together, he spoke about himself and introduced himself. How did he introduce himself?

“I am a gambler! A first-rate gambler!” A lazy fellow! Not in that manner! Then how? As what?

“People of the town, you know me, very, very well. I am not now going to tell you anything about me that you do not know.

“I have spent my life in a very upright manner. I am a man who has never failed in integrity.

### PDF page 10 — printed page 9

“That I am renowned for briskness is also not something unknown to you.

“Therefore, in this situation, you must give me a title as proof that you have confidence in me.”

He asked them to call him from that day onward with the epithet ‘worthy man’.

Will the townspeople agree to this? Surely they will not, is what must arise in all your minds.

But it is here, friends, that a strange, amusing event takes place.

‘All right, worthy sir,’ the townspeople agreed, and from that day they called that first-rate gambler a worthy man. This is the first little story.

You may glare at me and say, ‘What, man, you are telling as a story an event that cannot happen and cannot be believed!’

I am not spinning a tale. Events like this do indeed happen. Here, today, in this country—that too I am going to explain after telling the other two little stories.

### PDF page 11 — printed page 10

A man lived in the country bearing the distinction ‘highway robber’. He was very skilled at highway robbery.

That highway robber would stop everything from loaded carts travelling alone to empty carts and snatch whatever he could from the travellers.

He would stop motor cars carrying travellers. From a *thali* to a tin cup, whatever he got, he would not hesitate to snatch it.

He had become a notorious, fearsome highway robber. Mercilessly, he would threaten maidens.

Gentlemen, men of wealth, poor or rich—whoever it was, that highway robber would never fail to show his handiwork.

Suddenly, one day, he—the highway robber—appeared in the town.

He gathered the townspeople together and asked them to give him the title ‘man of integrity’ and to express confidence in him. The townspeople too said, ‘All right.’

Do not become exasperated and say, ‘Are you mocking us, Karunanidhi? A story indeed—a senseless story. Must we believe this too?’

### PDF page 12 — printed page 11

You yourselves will agree that such events commonly occur in the country. Wait a little. Let me tell the third little story too.

A prostitute—a well-known prostitute, one who forcibly drew young men to herself and spent her time as a prostitute. Such a woman lived in a city.

Though she was over thirty, she would present herself as a young woman. She was adept at adorning herself to such a degree that others would think her charming.

She would cover the wrinkles sunken into her face with powder and ‘snow’. She would comb her hair and adorn it with flowers; wear graceful clothes and ornaments in many different ways; and, swaying, showing off and preening, beckon passers-by with glances and hand gestures—that hand-signalling woman, that bangle-handed woman, that enchantress, that prostitute.

She, who was regarded and spoken of in the town with contempt and reproach as a prostitute, one day convened the town council and asked—and obtained—that she be joyfully bestowed the name ‘Queen of Chastity’.

Yes; it was upon the prostitute that the honourable epithet ‘Queen of Chastity’ was conferred by the people of that city.
