# Azhvars Research Centre Function

Karunanidhi opens by responding to criticism that he has attended a function of the Azhvars Research Centre. He says this is already his second visit, so explanations that might have been necessary on the first occasion need not be repeated. He describes his own way of deciding whether to attend functions: for some he seeks, in conscience, the “permission” of Periyar, for some of Anna, and for this one of Bharathidasan.

He then quotes Bharathidasan's praise of Ramanuja for making what had been treated as the preserve of a few available to the Tamil country more broadly. Karunanidhi says that this verse gives him sufficient sanction to participate in a function honouring Ramanuja.

To explain his position, he recalls having attended Kamban festivals despite earlier Dravidian-movement criticism of the *Ramayana*. He remembers Anna's literary debates with R. P. Sethupillai and Pasumalai Navalar Somasundara Bharathiar and says Anna could reject aspects of Kamban's story while still admiring Kamban's Tamil, poetic beauty and literary achievement. In Ramanuja's case, Karunanidhi says, he can accept not only literary value but also the social ideas associated with him.

Karunanidhi refers to the twelve Azhvars' songs, the *Nalayira Divya Prabandham*, and its description as the “Dravida Veda.” He credits Ramanuja with helping establish its place in Vaishnavite temples and then turns to Ramanuja's egalitarian social role.

The speech highlights Ramanuja's association with temple entry at Tirunarayanapuram, today's Melak Kottai, saying he removed barriers against Adi Dravidars entering the temple roughly 850 years before Gandhi's temple-entry campaign. Karunanidhi presents this as evidence that Ramanuja combined spirituality with reformist social ideas.

He also tells the story of Urangavallidasar, described as a disciple from the hunter community. Karunanidhi uses the episode to stress Ramanuja's rejection of caste pride: a person should be free of pride in education, wealth and caste, and Ramanuja is portrayed as deliberately challenging those who retained caste arrogance.

Another extended episode concerns Tirukkottiyur Nambi. After Ramanuja is said to have made eighteen journeys to receive a sacred mantra under a vow of secrecy, he publicly proclaims it from the temple tower. When challenged, he answers that he is willing to go to hell himself if sharing the mantra enables many others to attain salvation. Karunanidhi treats the story as an example of self-sacrifice for the benefit of the many.

This leads to a personal anecdote from Karunanidhi's film *Thirumbi Paar*. A censor objected to a line about leaders who climb temple towers to proclaim principles, assuming it mocked Ramanuja. Karunanidhi says that he had not written it with Ramanuja in mind; the censorship dispute itself prompted him to read more about Ramanuja.

The speech then turns to the awards presented at the function. Karunanidhi praises writer Saavi for humour, literary work and participation in the freedom struggle; Justice M. M. Ismail for his close association with Kamban studies and Tamil literature; V. C. Kulandaisamy as a scholar of such breadth that he could be called a university in himself; and poet Soundara Kailasam for her Tamil poetry and warm personal nature despite differences in religious and political outlook.

A major closing section marks R. M. Veerappan's seventy-second birthday. Karunanidhi recalls the 1972 split involving M. G. Ramachandran and says Veerappan had tried, even emotionally, to prevent the separation. He says that although Veerappan later stood apart from him, he respected that decision because Veerappan remained loyal to MGR. Karunanidhi describes Veerappan as MGR's true and steadfast heir in commitment, rather than identifying inheritance with family relationship.

Karunanidhi then distinguishes two questions: whether one should go to a temple is a matter of ideology, while whether Tamil should be used for worship inside an existing temple is a matter of language and rights. He says Periyar too supported Tamil archana, and argues that even spirituality can be welcomed when it strengthens Tamil language and Tamil feeling.

He concludes by saying that a society seeking unity should make constructive use of whatever tools can help it: Ramanuja's spirituality, Anna's intellectual movement, Periyar's Self-Respect Movement, Gandhi's non-violence and the Buddha's equality. He praises Veerappan's work through the Azhvars Research Centre and, in a closing birthday joke, says that “72 is being greeted by 74,” before wishing him a life of more than 120 years like Ramanuja.
