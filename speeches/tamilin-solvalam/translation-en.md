Karunanidhi addresses students of the Puducherry Law College and begins with their institutional demands, including the need for a proper site and building. He then turns to the larger responsibility of law students, arguing that legal education should prepare them not merely to appear before courts but to argue for the future of the country and for people divided by caste power, religious conflict, money and social inequality.

The central subject is the vocabulary available to Tamil in modern fields, especially law. Karunanidhi observes that English distinguishes terms such as “law,” “bill,” “act,” “legislative assembly,” “legislative council,” “legal adviser” and “constitution,” while ordinary Tamil usage repeatedly falls back on the single word `சட்டம்` together with descriptive compounds.

His point is not that Tamil lacks lexical richness. He explicitly notes the language's ancient literary and grammatical wealth, even giving the multiple words `ஆ`, `பசு` and `கோ` for the cow. The problem, he says, is that modern scientific and legal domains have expanded faster than the language's specialised vocabulary.

Accordingly, governments alone cannot solve the problem. Scholars, lawyers and other experts must contribute new, usable technical terms, while governments help bring those terms into practice.

He closes by linking this work of lexical growth to the historic Tamil academies associated with the Pandya country and asks the students and their leaders to add to that tradition by strengthening Tamil for contemporary life.

## Separate source closing note

Speech delivered at a Puducherry Law College function on 5 October 1980.
