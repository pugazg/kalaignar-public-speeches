## PDF 181 / printed p.180

### 24

## Moonlit Courtyard

For two days, as though under the spell of the song “Mayakkam... Mayakkam...,” all of us have been immersed in a Tamil enchantment. Now that the festival is ending, there is real sadness in leaving this shore.

This coast, once associated with separation and loss, has for two days heard ministers, scholars, poets and artists speak about Tamil society, Tamil identity and the future of the Tamil world.

The sea-lost Kaveripoompattinam is appearing again as a new Poompuhar. Whether or not those now in office remain, this vision should endure as long as Tamil and Tamil feeling endure.

## PDF 182 / printed p.181

Speakers and poets have portrayed the beauty of *Silappathikaram*. Work on the new Poompuhar and the epic's art gallery began in 1970 and passed through uncertainty before reaching this full moon festival in 1973.

I say we will continue such festivals in the years ahead. Critics ask why a Poompuhar festival should be held while the State has many problems. Our answer is that reviving the cultural memory of a city destroyed by the sea is itself an important public task, and we have pursued it with determination.

## PDF 183 / printed p.182

Many religious festivals take place without drawing political criticism. This festival attracts attention because it is a Tamil historical festival—not a puranic or devotional celebration, but one remembering those who ruled ancient Tamilakam.

We honour Karikala, Nedunchezhiyan and Senguttuvan so that we first understand who we are. A speaker observed that Ilango's distinctiveness lay in making people from an ordinary merchant family—Kovalan and Kannagi—the centre of his epic rather than merely celebrating kings.

A government that speaks for ordinary people naturally finds meaning in such a festival.

## PDF 184 / printed p.183

More than a million people have taken part over these days, and the gathering has remained peaceful. I thank and congratulate all who made that possible.

The new turn now visible in Tamil history gives us renewed enthusiasm.
