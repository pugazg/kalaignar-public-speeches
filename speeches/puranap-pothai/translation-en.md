#### Puranic Intoxication!

While passing time by telling people to keep singing bhajans and *Bhaja Govindam*, Achariyar, the Chief Minister who came to rule the country, abolished the rationing system, placing the burden on Bhagavan! We have seen what benefit, what use, came of that.

At one time he says, ‘Rationing is being abolished; Bhagavan said so, and I have abolished it.’

Achariyar also says, ‘If the abolition of rationing fails, I shall leave my post.’

In one way or another, Achariyar goes on telling little stories as support—stories about Bhagavan and stories that praise and glorify Bhagavan—under the name of sacred tales, in the manner of aunt-and-grandmother stories, as epics, pictures, stories of the Lord, devotional songs and in many, many other forms; from childhood onward he invokes devotion, recitation, fear of hell and salvation and many such reasons, and brings in the Puranas and Itihasas as support.

### PDF page 81 — printed page 80

The Purana—a kind of intoxication! It stands as a cause of the people's delusion, of mental intoxication!

Puranic intoxication, handed down from generation to generation and instilled as habit and custom, has soaked into the people's minds and thoughts!

That intoxication, soaked in and embedded, is again being fed among the people through repeated speech and propaganda by Achariyar—the man who came to rule the country!

At the same time, heads of monasteries too have turned their attention to this work—to the work of inducing Puranic intoxication.

The Purana has begun to take on new life and induce intoxication, creating ways, means and opportunities for a few people to manipulate others however they please while those people struggle trapped in that intoxication.

The Purana—a devotional story—the divine play of Parandhaman: read it and you gain merit; sin disappears; even if you do not read it, merely stand beside someone reading it and listen, and your sin will be removed and you will attain liberation in the next world. With such a preface it is first given to the people; it is preached.

Without speaking back, without raising a question mark, listen. Without examining it, act according to it! Doubt it and trouble will come—place the burden on Bhagavan—

### PDF page 82 — printed page 81

—you will receive the benefit—turn the Purana's pages and see, listen—how many devotees have had their sins removed and attained merit! The people are made to swoon through coaxing words.

A great effort must be made for this intoxication to clear—to leave, to leave the people.

For this, let us too wander for a while among the Puranas—come!

At the very moment I invite you to wander among the Puranas, I am also obliged to give a warning.

I invite you to wander among the Puranas,

not to praise the Puranas. Nor to abuse them; not to follow them blindly; nor to mock them however one pleases! To understand the Puranas!

The many things called Puranas move through this country as manuscripts, poems, stories, epics and pictures, present in the people's sight, thought, vision and imagination!

Periya Puranam, Vinayaka Puranam, Garuda Puranam, Kanda Puranam, Harishchandra Puranam, Arunachala—

### PDF page 83 — printed page 82

—Puranam, and whatever else there may be: for however many incarnations of God there are, and for every sacred place, there are not one but many, many Puranas, sometimes even contradicting one another!

In the Garuda Puranam are described the cruelties that humans must undergo in hell for particular wrongs, and which gifts, given to whom, will enable one to live that beautiful otherworldly life, a heavenly life!

However much one says about the Periya Puranam, dawn will never come!

We read of the devotee Iyarpakai Nayanar who offered even his wife as a gift to Maheswaran, and of the Compassionate One who asked for such a gift of a wife!

We see Siruthondar, immersed in devotional intoxication, who—when one in Shiva's guise asked for his own child Seeralan, the speaking golden portrait, the fruit-sweet child he had begotten—cut the boy Seeralan into pieces, cooked him and served him!

Reading and rereading this, singing and singing, people fall into ecstasy, their bodies thrill, tremble, sweat, every limb swelling with rapture, and thinking again and again of Aalavaayappan's test of grace, they rejoice and dance—

### PDF page 84 — printed page 83

—and we see such people in the state of devotees! Pitiful!

From the granary stored away for the Lord, during famine the people—the Lord's own children of grace, tormented by hunger—took rice and ate it, intending to replace it in prosperous days. Unable to tolerate hearing this, and because they had eaten Shiva's property, relatives, kinsmen, wife and children—why, even a tender child—were cut down with the sword, displaying the extreme limit of devotion (intoxication), and in the end the murderous, cannabis-like Kodpuli Nayanar received the grace of the Lord of Kailasa: can we forget him!

Can we, with our intelligence and inquiry, refrain from weighing the service to Shiva of Nayanars such as Kannappar, who escaped after plucking out his eye, Karaikkal Ammaiyar, whose body wasted away and form collapsed into that of a spirit, Appar who underwent severe suffering, and Sundarar, Sambandar and Manikkavasagar, who obtained Shiva's grace with relative ease!

In the Vinayaka Puranam, countless games and amusements about the births of the Lord!

And in the Kanda Puranam, the incarnation of the six-faced one, and for that the arrow of Manmatha that Manmatha shot at Shiva—

### PDF page 85 — printed page 84

—and Manmatha's tragic end by being burned, the story of the destruction of the asuras, and how many, how many other events are there to be seen.

Why should we not ask who still needs the story of Harishchandra, who, because someone deliberately and deceitfully took away his property and tried to make him out to be a liar, bound himself without sense or justice and was ruined?

Arunachala Puranam! At Arunachalam, called Tiruvannamalai, Mahavishnu, the god who protects, and Brahma, the god of creation, are said to have taken the forms of a boar and a swan to find the feet and head of Hara and thereby settle the futile dispute over who was greater.

A quarrel and dispute between gods assigned the work of creation and preservation! What wonder! What spectacle! All over which of the two was greater!

To determine victory and defeat in this bizarre contest, Shiva is said to have stood in cosmic form at Tiruvannamalai!

In this contest Brahma, taking the form of a swan and flying upward to see the head, is said to have taken as witness a screw-pine flower that was falling down from Shiva's sacred matted hair and falsely—

### PDF page 86 — printed page 85

—boasted, ‘I saw Shiva's sacred crown; I defeated Vishnu!’ Then the deception came to light and he is said to have incurred the anger and curse of the Lord of Kailasa.

Because such divine plays supposedly took place there, Tiruvannamalai is said to have become a holy place.

In just the same way, a Purana says that for some reason a frightened donkey chased a grass-carrying outcaste woman, the woman ran round and round a temple carrying the grass, and both the donkey that chased after her and the frightened woman who ran were granted the kingdom of salvation.

There is also, in the Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam, a story in which liberation is granted to a son who killed his father and had intercourse with his mother!

In the Purana we can see Tirumazhisai Azhvars, praised as holy men, who even through theft are said to have worshipped without diminishing their devotion to Bhagavan!

I read another strange and amusing incident in a Purana.

Obscenity has been poured upon the Lord in it to such an extent that one ought to bow one's head in shame!

### PDF page 87 — printed page 86

A storehouse of obscenity: bizarre bodily relations that do not accord with reason, inquiry or humanity; excited revelry; spectacles of entertainment! Are these not what most things called Puranas in this country contain? What else!

For anything at all, for every little incident, is there not a Purana, an old story, in this country? It is embedded in the people's hearts and lies there intoxicated!

Why is the crow black? There is a Purana for it! Why does the elephant have a trunk? A god-story for that! Why is sea water salty? A Purana! Why are there three lines on a squirrel's back? A story for that too, linked with Rama!

‘The seed of the aivelangai fruit is shaped like a linga! Don't you know?’ they begin, and then begin the story of Bhasmasura!

‘The seed of the aivelangai fruit has the form of a Shivalinga. Do you know why?’ This has spread among the people, especially among the large mass of ordinary folk! Merely thinking about it makes both the blindness attributed to God and the incidents said to have been done by gods in their capacity as gods seem most strange and even amusing—

### PDF page 88 — printed page 87

—indeed!

Listen to that amusing story. That Purana!

There was, it is said, an asura named Bhasmasura. According to the dictionary of the Puranas and sacred texts, an asura means not merely one who does not practice charity, compassion, good thought or good conduct, but one who does not even know how to do such things!

Such a cruel one. A wicked man who carried on his daily life and conduct amid dreadful thoughts is said to have performed penance directed toward Paramashiva.

Penance! Not ordinary penance! He is said to have performed such severe penance that he cooled the heart of Sarveswaran and brought the Lord of Kailasa to a state in which he appeared before him and granted him a vision.

Does not the fact that the Compassionate One appeared before that demon mean, does it not explain, that Bhasmasura's penance possessed such purity that one ought to bow before it?

In any case, through the greatness and excellence of Bhasmasura's penance, the God difficult to behold, Kailainathan, Shiva Peruman, appeared before him.

### PDF page 89 — printed page 88

Looking at Bhasmasura, he said, ‘My son. We are pleased with your penance. Ask what boon you want; it is granted.’

At once Bhasmasura asked, ‘Lord, whoever's head I place my hand upon must instantly turn to ash, must burn up,’ and Shiva bestowed the boon saying, ‘Granted. Granted.’

The asura rejoiced greatly. The very next moment, saying, ‘I shall first test the greatness of your boon by placing my hand upon your own head,’ he is said to have run toward Shiva to place his hand on Shiva's head and burn him to ash.

What did the all-powerful Lord do? He did not stand and resist. He did not stop to ask, ‘Why this outrage?’ He is said to have taken to his heels, thinking only that if his head escaped, that was enough!

The asura chases. He chases Shiva! Chasing and chasing, he runs after Shiva without letting up, trying to catch him.

Shiva runs in every direction until exhausted. He can no longer run. But the pursuer will not give up hunting and chasing him! If he stops, he will become fire, burn and turn to ash, if that hand lands upon his head—

### PDF page 90 — printed page 89

—such is the worry! What is Shivalinganathan to do?

He turned and looked. Nearby he saw an aivelangai creeper heavy with fruit.

He is said to have run and shrunk himself into an aivelangai fruit, hiding inside it to escape the asura and preserve his own life! Not only did Shiva hide: he thought of Mahavishnu, the preserving god, and immediately Vishnu, in order to resolve Shiva's difficult situation, is said to have taken the form of Mohini and appeared before Bhasmasura, who was roaming about searching for Shiva.

Mohini! To enchant the asura, bring him to ruin and keep Shiva alive, Vishnu assumed Mohini's form. He became a woman, Mohini.

The moment Bhasmasura saw Mohini, he became infatuated. Mohini too pretended to consent. When the asura approached, she moved away and sent him off saying, ‘Go bathe and come back!’

The asura went to bathe. Before this, Vishnu as Mohini, by his power, had made every river, well, pond and pool everywhere dry up without water.

The asura who had gone to bathe returned saying there was no water.

### PDF page 91 — printed page 90

Mohini sent him back saying, ‘At least sprinkle a little wet mud on your head and come.’

The asura went back and, as Mohini wished, saw a little muddy, slushy water in a pond.

The moment he saw it, intoxicated by his desire for the maiden, forgetting what he was doing and forgetting its consequence, he scooped up the muddy water and rubbed it on his head.

By the power of the boon he had obtained from Shiva through unrelenting penance, the moment Bhasmasura's own hand touched his head he burned and became ash.

The asura was finished! The great sinner died; through Mahavishnu's magic, through the intoxication of seeing Mohini's form!

Vishnu caused Shiva to come out of the aivelangai fruit, telling him that the demon had been destroyed!

The Purana goes on to say that Shiva, coming out, saw Vishnu's Mohini form, became infatuated and had intercourse with Mohini, who was Vishnu.

From the time Shiva ran and hid, people believe that the seed of the aivelangai fruit has the form of a Shivalinga—

### PDF page 92 — printed page 91

—and over this they encounter and hear that particular god-story, that Purana, in Puranic books, Puranic discourses, drama, cinema and everything else, and do not forget it; they are made unable to forget it.

Could one whose power of penance was enough to satisfy Shiva's sacred heart suddenly become evil and dare to kill the very god who had granted him the boon?

Let that pass. Could Shiva—Sarveswaran, the all-powerful one, the one with the power to grant boons—not correct the evil nature of an evil man?

Why should a god who had the power to hide inside the fruit of a small creeper run fearfully in every direction merely to avoid being caught by the asura!

Is this not an insult to the very power of Shiva who burned the three cities!

Above all, according to the sacred Purana, after seeing Vishnu in female form, Shiva—who had fled in fear and hidden and then emerged—became infatuated, blinded by lust, and had intercourse with him! Is such conduct fitting for a god?

Is it not uncivilized that, upon seeing Vishnu—a male, a god like himself—turned into a woman to save him, he lost his senses and—

### PDF page 93 — printed page 92

—performed the monkey-dance of lust? Is that not contrary to the dignity of a god!

And the one born from these two males—that is, Shiva and Vishnu who had assumed Mohini form—also becomes a god! So proclaims the Purana!

Is that all? Why does a woman have no moustache, no beard? Since when has she had none? There is a Purana for this too.

This is what I meant when I said that obscenity has been poured upon the Lord to such an extent that one must bow one's head in shame! Why, not only upon the gods above gods, but even upon sages who had renounced everything.

Mandavya was a sage. And a sage means one who has renounced, does it not!

He too is said to have wished to marry and lead household life. Searching throughout the world and failing to find a woman pleasing to his heart, he decided that his own widowed sister at home was suitable and made her his wife.

To the sage who made his sister, his widowed sister, his wife, a girl child was born, grew up, and became a maiden of marriageable age!

### PDF page 94 — printed page 93

When the girl had grown up, the sage and his wife set out on a journey around the world.

Before leaving, they wondered: with whom could they leave their daughter so that it would be good, beneficial! Who would let her remain with her chastity unspoiled and without blemish?

Shiva, Vishnu, Brahma, Vinayaka, Muruga, Devendra, the thirty-three crore gods and the rest—none was suitable, they are said to have thought. Each had committed wrongs in matters involving women; they could not be trusted with the young woman, so they rejected them all.

They may have remembered that Shiva had Uma as one half of his body and Ganga upon his head, and at the same time did not leave Vishnu alone when Vishnu was in Mohini form!

Perhaps they could not forget Vishnu's many incarnations, especially the revelries carried on in the Krishna incarnation.

More than this, the Puranic incident in which Vishnu took the form of the husband of Brindai, the wife of a sage, violated her chastity, she died because of it, and Vishnu, his lust still not cooled, rolled in the heap of ashes from her burned corpse, and finally only after wearing as a garland the tulasi plant that sprouted from those ashes—

### PDF page 95 — printed page 94

—returned to his senses, may have warned them.

Likewise, perhaps thinking of Indra's violation of Ahalya, of Muruga who, though with Deivayanai, went in search of Valli, and of the gods above gods who consorted with temple women, Mandavya and the woman who had been born as his sister and then became his wife seem to have set them aside, deciding that none of these was suitable to safeguard their daughter.

Finally, deciding that Yama Dharma alone was suitable, they are said to have entrusted their daughter to Yama Dharmaraja before leaving on their world journey, asking him to guard her chastity and return her when they came back.

How they forgot that Yama Dharma too, according to the Bharata, had intercourse with Kunti Devi and fathered Bhima!

They themselves were people in which a sister married her elder brother and an elder brother married his sister, were they not!

Yama Dharma, fearing that if the maiden entrusted to him remained outside, somehow someone might ruin and corrupt the young woman, swallowed her through his mouth and kept her safely in his stomach—

### PDF page 96 — printed page 95

—without anyone knowing, without her being seen by anyone!

As the days passed, desire increasingly overcame Yama Dharma. One night he took the maiden out of his stomach and made love to her! The sage's daughter too agreed, and they are said to have joined together and rejoiced.

By day in his stomach and by night joining Yama Dharma in bed and engaging in sexual play, the sage's daughter passed her time.

One day, in a grove, Yama Dharma took the sage's daughter out, left her in a pavilion and went to bathe.

The young woman was alone in the pavilion. The fullness of her youth and the beauty of her body, blooming through the new play of a new woman, caught the eyes and mind of Agni Bhagavan as he passed that way and inflamed him with lust.

Agni Bhagavan sought the help of his friend Vayu Bhagavan, who had come with him, and Vayu Bhagavan is said to have blown a spring breeze through that grove.

The breeze blew over decorative flowers, and with the fragrance of those flowers it is said to have placed enchantment in the young woman's mind, excitement in her body and intoxication in her senses.

### PDF page 97 — printed page 96

Seizing this moment, Agni approached her; at his touch she weakened; Agni embraced her; she clasped him still tighter and showered him with kisses, and sexual play began, it is said. The two, lost to themselves, heard Yama Dharma approaching and were startled. The sage's daughter immediately, just as Yama had done, put Agni Bhagavan into her mouth, swallowed him, and kept him safely in her stomach.

As usual Yama Dharma sent the young woman into his stomach, went home, took her out at night, carried on the usual play, and at dawn sent her back into his stomach as usual.

By day, the enchanting maiden inside Yama's stomach could not remain idle!

She took Agni out of her own stomach and, turning Yama's very stomach into a field of passion, is said to have enjoyed intercourse with Agni.

By day, pleasure with Agni. At night, joining Yama Dharma. Thus she moved through a world of pleasure.

Because Agni was confined in the beautiful woman's lower belly, everyone in the three worlds is said to have suffered endless troubles without fire.

### PDF page 98 — printed page 97

The gods of the three worlds and all the sages tried to find Agni. No one could bring Agni back! How could they?

Vayu Bhagavan saw what had happened and immediately arranged a feast for everyone, including Yama. Two extra leaves were placed beside Yama Dharma. When Yama asked, ‘For whom?’, Vayu said, ‘If you let out the woman in your stomach, it will be right,’ and Yama, frightened, let the woman out.

Vayu Bhagavan said, ‘Woman, send out the Agni in your stomach,’ and Agni, frightened, is said to have come out.

As Agni came screaming and frightened from the woman's stomach, that maiden's moustache and beard are said to have been completely burned away by Agni's flame.

From that day onward, a Puranic story goes among the people saying that moustache and beard disappeared entirely from the female sex! What an amusement!

How much obscenity there is in this Puranic story, how much meaningless obscene conduct attributed to those called gods.

### PDF page 99 — printed page 98

All this is read with devotion as divine play.

Panchali in the Bharata is placed among the ranks of chaste women. Is it fitting? Proper? Appropriate? Think!

A goddess to five, an imperishable chaste woman, a woman who belongs to five men, the wife of five husbands—how can she be a chaste woman?

Only five? It is said that as a sixth, that imperishable chaste woman Panchali had given her heart to Karna as well!

In the mango-fruit section of the Bharata, following the way shown by Kannan to make a mango that had fallen from the tree attach itself back to the tree, when the Pandavas and Draupadi disclosed the secrets of their minds, did Panchali not reveal, ‘I have a love-infatuation for Karna’? How can Panchali then be accepted as the queen of chastity? It cannot be!

From those things said to be Puranas—sacred stories, pure texts that show the path to devotion and liberation—if merely speaking of a few, including the story of Mandavya and the Bharata's Panchali, held up as an example of women's chastity, appears this shameful—

### PDF page 100 — printed page 99

—why should we not ask: what benefit, what benefit at all, do people gain from reading or hearing these?

By blindly reading and listening to Puranas, believing the extraordinary events described in them, the wonders beyond human power and the obscene incidents to be the Lord, incarnation, the way of grace, divine blessing, people become intoxicated in that same state, lose mental strength and, instead of advancing through their own power, their own labour and their own thought, immerse themselves only in prayer, devotion and recitation in the hope of raising their present condition, their condition of poverty—and become lazy!

Not merely lazy: spending the money in their hands and their time—why, even their thought—on worship of God, pilgrimage and puja, they carry, though unable to carry, the burden of a life without substance, thinking of the fate that carries them along, and lead a life crying, ‘Death, come! Death, come!’ Should this condition not be changed?

Should not the life in which people, as people, keep thinking of something, longing, waiting and waiting only to be disappointed, be changed and enriched?

### PDF page 101 — printed page 100

All this must be done. Anyone with the courage to deny it may say so directly! They will not come.

Therefore, Dravidian youths, young people filled with attachment to your people, it is you I ask: if the country is to be rescued, first remove the poisonous ideas from the minds of its people.

Puranic intoxication! It must be abolished through the teaching of new knowledge, through new-age ideas blossoming and flourishing among the people!

Let the drumbeat of uprising resound, so that the rationalist force—the camp—may cooperate with the DMK and see a Dravidian land from which the intoxication has been cleared away and removed.
