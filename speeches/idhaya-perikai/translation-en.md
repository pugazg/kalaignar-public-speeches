### PDF page 4 — printed page 3

#### A Small Drop, a Great Flood.

A storm of anguish arose. It devastated the flower garden called affection that had existed between us and our leader. The burden of condemning our leader fell upon our hearts. Because of the marriage episode which he calls very, very ordinary, our bewilderment and tears became futile, and in our condition there came about a “regrettable change.”

In the very first issue of the *Maalai Mani* newspaper, Anna wrote the editorial in these words:

**A regrettable change has occurred.**

That sentence must be considered with care.

Even with thousands upon thousands of younger brothers who have studied history—who bear the marks of daily sacrifice in international politics—who, through speech, writing and acting, work with enough ability to turn everyone from great scholars to ordinary people toward their point of view; even with them, working so that opponents are stunned, enemies enraged, doubters delighted, and those who cursed become unsettled; even with tens of thousands of genuine workers who toil without rest like the great waves of the sea—

### PDF page 5 — printed page 4

—even with all of them at hand, if scholar Anna, who possesses the maturity of mind and strength of will to conduct a great political movement, had to write in anguish of a “regrettable change,” how can that situation possibly be described?

When gold is being brought out of a mine and a rock suddenly rolls down, the effort to escape that rock and the effort we made to save our principle were one and the same.

How we trembled! How utterly we wilted! How many tears we shed! The sound of our choking sobs shook the whole Tamil land! For a few days we became people consumed by anxiety—statues that did not know what to do.

When we remember those few days—alas—the prayer that leaps in our hearts is surely: may those days never return.

On one side was the thunderbolt that we were separating. Yet after the separation, greater than the sorrow that we had separated was the realization that we who had parted had now become people charged with a great future; that we had become bullocks yoked to the noble chariot called public service. How were we going to achieve our ideals?

### PDF page 6 — printed page 5

Could we do it?.... Driven by such feelings of responsibility, we stood bewildered. That condition itself was terribly frightening.

More frightening still were the weapons of abuse hurled at us—the shafts of ridicule, the arrows of mockery, the darts of slander.

“Can these people run a movement!”

“These things are short-lived.”

“Will cultivation entrusted to a little child ever make it home?”

Such taunts danced about.

The doubt, “Can these people run a movement?” changed, and the answer arrived: these are precisely the people who can.

The prophecy “short-lived” disappeared, and the pet name “immortals” was bestowed upon us.

The proverbs about “a child’s farming” were put to shame. We can now see young bulls roaring proudly: Behold the yield the little children have produced—see the harvest—see the stacks piled high!

The talk that it would all end in the selfish interests of a few was shattered. Front-line fighters, proclaiming that every breath is for the country, now advance with graceful gait like brothers of a battalion—

### PDF page 7 — printed page 6

—the fruit of our effort has become such a camp.

No one else discovered and gave us the new name “Tear-Drops.” We gave ourselves that sorrowful name. There is no disgrace in a poor person calling himself poor.

There is nothing laughable in one who toils calling himself a labourer. We who shed tears and cried aloud do not lose our honour by calling ourselves Tear-Drops. On the contrary, we take pride—we swell with dignity—we walk with a proud stride. Why?

What riddled the Tsar’s rule—and the trickster Rasputin—with holes like a sieve? A teardrop.

What struck the napes of the devilish aristocrats of France? A teardrop.

What made the “Chinese island” become Red China? A teardrop.

> **Translator/source note:** The frozen Tamil literally reads `சீனத் தீவு` (“Chinese island”). The unusual source wording is represented rather than silently corrected.

A teardrop has brought down dictators. In many places a teardrop has summoned drops of blood. Those who treated tears with contempt ended up giving their heads freedom from their necks.

‘The tear shed by the poor is a sharp sword.’

### PDF page 8 — printed page 7

‘The tear shed by an idealist..........?’

The answer is not a question mark. Here stands our growing strength; this is the true power contained in the idealist’s tear.

If we have grown, has it been in an ordinary way? Without discipline, like the swelling of a wild river? With alphabet primers whose very organization is unknown?......No, no........

With clear plans—in all four quarters of Dravidam—with capable people carrying out the work, we have flourished.

Having held Kazhagam conferences district after district and, now, having entered upon preparations for the State Conference, when we pause and think back over the past two years, how much fulfilment wells up within us! We become embodiments of exhilaration. Our hearts leap with the joy of how honourably we Tear-Drops have performed our duty.

Every northern minister who came and went was shaken at the sight of the black-flag battalion gathered like a sea.

Even though horses’ hooves pressed into the bodies of our fighters for the cause—even though the sound of firing arose at Kundrathur—even though Naranamangalam happened—even though at Theni a “divine sport” was enacted—
