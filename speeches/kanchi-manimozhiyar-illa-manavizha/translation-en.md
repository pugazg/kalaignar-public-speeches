### Kanchi Manimozhiyar House Wedding

With all our good wishes, the wedding ceremony has been completed here between Ilavarasu — the beloved son of Natarajan, the younger brother of our Professor Ilanchezhiyan, and the grandson of Kanchi Manimozhiyar, who continues to live in all our hearts — and Kalaiyarasi, the daughter of our dear friend, engineer Thirunavukkarasu. Seeing all of you gathered in this hall in such numbers for this wedding makes clear how much love and attachment all of you have for Manimozhiyar.

Younger brother Murasoli Maran mentioned here that Manimozhiyar built up the party in Chennai district. Not only at the district level, but also at the state level, for the DMK

## PDF 248 / printed p.247

to grow, gain strength and spread widely everywhere, one of those who stood by Perarignar Anna was the elder Kanchi Manimozhiyar.

While speaking, younger brother Maran said, “Professor Ilanchezhiyan was one who refused even an office that was offered to him.” In the same way, his father Manimozhiyar too was one who rendered rare service with enthusiasm and conviction, saying that this movement was not for office and that he would work to develop the movement further.

Before it was announced who the candidates would be and where they would contest in the 1967 election, all the activists in that area urged Arignar Anna to make Kanchi Manimozhiyar the Assembly candidate for Saidapet constituency. Without my knowledge, Anna called Kanchi Manimozhiyar and asked, “This time I am thinking of fielding Karunanidhi in Saidapet constituency. What do you say?” Manimozhiyar immediately told Anna that he would not file a nomination. Not only did he say that; after my name was announced, even I may not have met the people of Saidapet constituency as many times as he did. He met the voters more often than I did and

## PDF 249 / printed p.248

worked tirelessly for my victory that day, and I will never forget that it was Kanchi Manimozhiyar who plucked that fruit of victory and gave it to me.

Every morning, when I came to his house to take him with me to meet the voters, he would already have prepared himself and be ready to leave before all of us. From morning until evening we would go through many areas, house to house, asking for votes. My legs would begin to ache.

At six in the evening I would look at him and say, “Manimozhiyar, this is enough for today. We can see the remaining places tomorrow.” He would reply, “No, no. What is this? You are younger than I am. How can you get tired like this? Come, let us ask for votes in two more streets,” and he would insist on taking me along. Only after we had finished asking for votes at every one of those houses would he be at peace.

Not only that; he would note down with great precision which places we had to visit the next day to ask for votes, which leading people in which areas we had to meet, and whom we had to approach to obtain their support. In that way, he guided me through that election.

## PDF 250 / printed p.249

Many party activists were responsible for Anna winning as a Member of Parliament in South Chennai and, likewise, for my winning as a Member of the Legislative Assembly in Saidapet constituency. Even so, the credit for guiding that effort — the credit for working with the resolve, “I do not want office; I will seat you on the throne” — belongs to Kanchi Manimozhiyar.

Therefore, the message I give at this wedding is that all friends in the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, and all those in its various organisations, should follow that spirit of sacrifice shown by Kanchi Manimozhiyar.

He was not a man who hated office altogether. He had not renounced it. Nor had he said that one must become an ascetic in relation to it. But whenever the leadership said, “No, step aside a little and make way for this person,” he made way and became a guide for others. That is why, even though many years have passed since his death, we still speak of that distinction. To say that this itself was Kanchi Manimozhiyar would be no exaggeration.

The journal `Porvaal` (“Battle Sword”), run by his beloved son, our Ilanchezhiyan, truly shone like a battle sword in the hands of the self-respect warriors of Tamil Nadu, the Dravidian

## PDF 251 / printed p.250

movement's warriors, and especially all the comrades of the DMK. We all know this very well. The book written by Ilanchezhiyan under the title `Thamizhan Thodutha Por` (“The War Waged by the Tamil”) still remains a treasury that can stand as a strong support to all of us in opposing Hindi domination in Tamil Nadu. Such a Tamil family — a family that lived for Tamil, that continues to live for Tamil, and that will live for Tamil in the future. Taking part in a wedding function within such a family is something that brings pride to all of us.

Our friend Suthanandam referred to this as a reform marriage, yet said, “I pray to Murugan that this bride and groom may live well,” and blessed them. Even if a reform marriage is conducted by the DMK, we will not say that there is no place there for believers.

What we want in a reform marriage is that our language must be given respect there and reason must be given respect there. At the same time, we will not say that those who differ from our views, or who hold other views, have no place here.

Whether the marriages in our homes are reform marriages, Self-Respect marriages, or marriages conducted in the Tamil manner, in every marriage we must ensure that our

## PDF 252 / printed p.251

sense of self-respect does not wither away. That alone is what we desire, nothing else.

I want to say one thing. When the Minister for People's Welfare, Arcot Veerasamy, briefly offered his greetings, he said — as he usually does, and as he does at all weddings — that they should have only a limited number of children, giving the kind of blessing appropriate to his People's Welfare portfolio. Maran also pointed out that this is a second-generation marriage.

Even though the first-generation marriage took place as a Self-Respect marriage, it was not an inter-caste marriage. It was a marriage within caste. Now the second generation too is taking place in the same way. Therefore I ask that, at least in the third generation, the children born to our Ilavarasu and Kalaiyarasi should introduce inter-caste marriage in their homes. (Applause.)

For one who is a self-respecter, a rationalist, one who grew in the footsteps of Periyar and in the shade of Perarignar Anna, it is not enough merely to conduct a Self-Respect marriage. One must understand its implications and encourage inter-caste marriages so that caste distinctions disappear and religious distinctions disappear. There are people who ask, “Will caste disappear because one inter-caste marriage takes place in one house?” But that is no reason to refrain from making this effort.

## PDF 253 / printed p.252

Among the marriages that have taken place in my own household so far, many have been love marriages and inter-caste marriages. I have confidence that such marriages will continue to take place in my home. But there are people who ask, “If inter-caste marriages take place in the homes of five or six people, will caste disappear from Tamil Nadu?”

Even today I saw an article in a weekly magazine asking: “If a hundred houses are built in Madurai or Tiruchi as a Samathuvapuram, will all of Tamil Nadu become a Samathuvapuram?” That question had been raised in an article in the weekly `Thuglak`.

I can ask in return: because Rajaji took one hundred Adi Dravidars and carried out temple entry, was caste abolished? No. But can one therefore say that carrying out temple entry was wrong? One cannot.

When Madurai Vaidyanathayyar took one hundred Adi Dravidars into the Madurai Meenakshi Amman Temple and carried out temple entry, did caste disappear? It did not. But can that effort, that action, be criticised for that reason?

Why stop there? In Puduvai, Subramania Bharathiyar brought in a Harijan boy named Kanakalingam, made all the Iyers stand around him, and conducted a sacred-thread ceremony for that Adi Dravida boy. “Look, I am making this Adi Dravida boy into an Iyer. If a sacred thread is put on him, he becomes an Iyer, does he not?” — saying this, he conducted the sacred-thread

## PDF 254 / printed p.253

ceremony. No one asked, “If Kanakalingam is made an Iyer, will all the Kanakalingams throughout the country become Iyers?”

But they ask only when I do something. If Bharathi does it, they accept it. If I follow Bharathi's path and do it, they refuse to accept it. If Rajaji does it, they raise it up and praise it. If Madurai Vaidyanatha Iyer does it, they applaud and praise him. But if we do it, they refuse to accept it. Those who reject it are only a few; those who will accept it in the future are many. It is with that confidence that this movement is marching forward today.

Thanthai Periyar said: “I run the `Kudi Arasu` newspaper. I do not care whether anyone buys it and reads it or does not read it. I do not care whether it sells a thousand copies or ten thousand copies. Even if no one reads it, I myself will write it, I myself will print it, and I myself will sit on the front platform of the `Kudi Arasu` office and read that newspaper alone. That is enough for me. That satisfaction is enough for me.” The movement that was begun with words like these has today spread throughout Tamil Nadu. Today this policy has spread wide like a banyan tree and is giving shade.

## PDF 255 / printed p.254

The family that belongs to such a policy — a steadfast family, a family that stands upright like a hill — is the family of Kanchi Manimozhiyar. In the home of Natarajan, the younger brother of Ilanchezhiyan, who shines as one of the distinguished members of that family, his son Ilavarasu and Thirunavukkarasu's daughter Kalaiyarasi are enjoying this happiness today. I wish that they may enjoy this same happiness forever, gain every kind of prosperity in life, shine as lights to their home and as workers in service of the nation.
