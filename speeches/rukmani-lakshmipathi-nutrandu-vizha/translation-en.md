# Mrs Rukmani Lakshmipathi Centenary Celebration

Karunanidhi speaks at the centenary celebration of Rukmani Lakshmipathi and says he is proud both to meet those gathered and to recall the achievements of a woman who has died but whose memory, in his formulation, has not faded.

He presents her as a figure who worked for the country, society, women and social reform, especially the eradication of caste. He praises the reformist tradition of her family and describes her as a jewel within that lineage.

Karunanidhi highlights an example offered at the event by Ponnammal: Rukmani Lakshmipathi did not merely speak of equality but practised it in her own household, replacing a Brahmin cook with an Adi-Dravidar woman named Poongavanam. He also praises her personal simplicity, including her decision not to wear jewellery, and defines sacrifice as voluntarily giving up what one actually possesses.

He recalls her participation in the 1930 Salt Satyagraha led in Tamil Nadu by Sardar Vedaratnam Pillai and Rajaji and the punishment she endured. He then notes her later public responsibilities: Deputy Speaker in 1937 and Health Minister in the Prakasam ministry in 1946. Despite these offices, he says, she remained simple, affectionate, beyond caste and religious divisions, and deeply committed to the country.

The speech also recounts the renaming of Marshall's Road as Rukmani Lakshmipathi Salai. Karunanidhi says that when the request was made at a function on the road, he accepted it immediately as an order to be implemented. He further recalls the later installation of her statue and the government's move to obtain approval from the Union Government for a commemorative postage stamp, which was being released at the centenary observance.

When Dr Ramamurthi introduces him not as Chief Minister but as “my friend Kalaignar Karunanidhi,” Karunanidhi says friendship is what endures: the office of Chief Minister comes and goes, but friendship is lasting.

He also speaks warmly of Sardar Vedaratnam Pillai and says he has written to the Union Government requesting a postage stamp in Vedaratnam's honour as well.

Karunanidhi closes by connecting the centenary event to the golden-jubilee year of independence. He says the histories of Tamil Nadu's freedom fighters should be written, but their poverty should also be relieved: society must preserve both the history of sacrifice and the dignity of the surviving sacrificers.
