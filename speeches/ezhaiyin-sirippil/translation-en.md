## In the Smile of the Poor

I am very happy to participate in the five-hundredth anniversary celebration of the revered Guru Nanak. Thiruvalluvar gave the people of Tamil Nadu the ethical work called the Thirukkural. It is noteworthy that in this year, when Thiruvalluvar's two-thousandth anniversary is being celebrated, Guru Nanak's five-hundredth anniversary is also being observed. There are many similarities between Punjab and Tamil Nadu.

I am not referring only to the similarity between me and your Chief Minister, Gurnam Singh. In Tamil Nadu, learned people standing in Valluvar's tradition have been saying that caste, religion and superstition must be abolished. In Punjab, standing in Guru Nanak's path, you proclaim the same idea.

In the Indian freedom struggle, Kattabomman was hanged in Tamil Nadu in an early phase. Punjab's hero Bhagat Singh too kissed the gallows. I also think of Oomaithurai, Periya Maruthu and Chinna Maruthu, who became victims of British murderous violence in Tamil Nadu. My whole body thrills; I bow to the soil on which those heroes shed their blood.

Sankaralinganar fasted demanding that our State be named Tamil Nadu.

## PDF 58 / printed p.57

He died during that fast — not now, but ten years ago.

Here too, an elder named Peruman died after undertaking a fast.

I do not wish to enter into that controversy. I kneel and pay homage to this soil in which that fighter was born.

Guru Nanak, born five hundred years ago, faced many hardships while striving to fulfil his principles. He was even imprisoned. That too became useful to him: he spread his ideas among the prisoners. For an idealist there is hardly a place more welcome than a prison. At times, the prison I used to visit so often in the past seems to me more attractive even than the post of Chief Minister!

Guru Nanak turned even a prison into a garden of thought. He had such strength from youth. The sun rises by driving away darkness; likewise, as Guru Nanak grew from childhood, he grew by driving away the darkness of superstition.

His family made all arrangements to invest the young Nanak with the sacred thread. He refused it.

“Twist love, humaneness and knowledge into a thread and wear that. That alone is the sacred thread; this one is unnecessary,” Nanak explained.

## PDF 59 / printed p.58

Once, exhausted, he fell asleep with his feet stretched toward a shrine. Religious men were shocked, woke him and rebuked him. He replied: “Turn my feet toward the direction where God is not.” Those who had scolded him asked forgiveness.

He brought together elements of Hinduism and Islam and created a new religion.

In Tamil Nadu there is a district called Salem. There, two mango plants are grafted together. The fruit is called a grafted mango, and it is very tasty. Sikhism is like that mango.

It is not enough merely to conduct festivals for great people. The Tamil Nadu government has encouraged an effort to establish a college in Guru Nanak's name in Tamil Nadu.

We have often happily participated in Nanak festivals held in Tamil Nadu.

We must not stop with festival, happiness and celebration. We must live by the paths laid down by great people and enable society to live by them too.

Many religious teachers have arisen in the country, yet the caste problem remains unresolved.

Many rationalists have arisen, yet superstition has not disappeared.

## PDF 60 / printed p.59

Why is this so? We must examine the reason. If a good medicine exists and yet the disease is not cured, what is the cause?

If the patient never takes the doctor's medicine, the disease will not be cured.

It is not enough for the principles laid down by great people to remain in books. We must read them, live according to them and spread them to others.

The sun gives light; those who sow must make use of it. The sky gives rain; those who cultivate the fields must use it.

Neither light nor rain takes a human being by the hand and pleads, “Lazy man, make use of me!” Great people similarly gave light and poured down a rain of ideas. If we fail to use them, the fault is ours, not theirs.

One who consumes love and humaneness and keeps them for himself sends God out like a belch.

One who generously gives love and humaneness contains God within himself. He himself becomes God.

When there is such an easy path to becoming divine, people ignore it and wander elsewhere searching for God.

In the smile of the poor —

## PDF 61 / printed p.60

in the prattle of a child —
in the sweat of the worker —
in the chastity of a woman —
in the character of a man —
in the eyes of the discerning —
in the knowledge of the scientist —

God is there. Yet some people, abandoning these, search for God as a blind man searches in a dark room for a black cat.

Guru Nanak was one who explained the true meaning of God to such people.

Social service changes from age to age. At one time people living like barbarians needed someone to teach them civilisation. Later, civilised people created a thousand divisions among themselves, and reformers were needed. When reformers themselves multiplied and conflicts arose among them, mediators were needed to reconcile them.

Human beings who once ate raw flesh were taught to cook. Once they learned to cook, freeing them from hunger became another task.

On one side we worship and celebrate children like gods.

On the other side we campaign for family planning, telling people not to have too many children.

To keep the dove of peace flying forever in the world,

## PDF 62 / printed p.61

it is amid such changing and contradictory conditions that jewels of humanity are born to serve people.

One such new light rose in the year 1469.

Guru Nanak taught:

“All religions are one!
All human beings are one!
Truth is above everything!
But there is something above even truth:
that is living by the path of truth.”

He proclaimed:

“There is one God;
religious rituals are futile;
caste differences must not exist.”

Five hundred years have rolled by. Rivers change course. Waterfalls change their paths. Mountains become valleys. Sea waves destroy cities. Deserts become gardens. Humanity, unable to summon the moon, has gone to the moon itself. Though nature has changed in countless ways over five centuries, there has been no change in the reforming message or the name of great people such as Guru Nanak. They remain.

Birds sing Guru Nanak's name.

Hearing that song, clouds shed tears of joy.

## PDF 63 / printed p.62

Nanak's song floats in the wind.

That wind embraces Tamil Nadu.

In that embrace I forget myself.

People of Punjab! I become one with you.

I stand before you.

My thanks to the Chief Minister of Punjab who placed me before you.

Long live the fame of Guru Nanak!
