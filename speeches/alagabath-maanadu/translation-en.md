Karunanidhi opens by linking Allahabad and Uttar Pradesh with major national figures and with the confluence of the Ganga and Yamuna. He uses that confluence as a metaphor for a gathering of backward and oppressed communities: linguistic identities may differ, but the demand for self-respect and social equality is shared.

He rejects caste hierarchy through a series of analogies. Whatever part of a divine body a person is imagined to have come from, all remain human; just as fruit on different parts of the same fig tree is still the same fruit, birth cannot create different grades of humanity.

The speech distinguishes political and economic justice from social justice. Constitutional equality, he argues, requires practical safeguards for people starting from historically unequal positions. He describes Tamil Nadu's reservation, educational assistance and employment measures, as well as the law opening priesthood to qualified people regardless of caste.

He discusses inter-caste and Self-Respect marriages, recalls the legal recognition given to Self-Respect marriages after the DMK came to office, and presents education as another instrument of social mobility. He asks the Union Government to extend representation in Central employment beyond Scheduled Castes to backward classes as well.

The closing sections stress that the advancement of a few individuals is not enough: those who rise should help lift the communities from which they came. He invokes Gandhi, Ambedkar, Periyar and Anna, reiterates his pledge to work for backward, oppressed and minority communities, and asks the conference to press the Union Government for full implementation of reserved posts and representation for backward classes.

## Separate source closing note

Special address delivered on 14 October 1973 at the representatives' conference of the Uttar Pradesh Harijan and Backward Classes gathering in Allahabad.
