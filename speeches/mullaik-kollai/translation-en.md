#### The Jasmine Grove

Beloved leaders! Comrades! Mothers! Members of other parties! I convey to all of you my greetings, mingled with gratitude.

The four of us who have come from Dravida to see you are not descendants of Appar, Sundarar, Manikkavasagar and Sambandar. Nor are we descendants of Rama, Lakshmana, Bharata and Shatrughna. We, the emissaries of the DMK, are four flowers—flowers that bloomed in Periyar's flower garden, within the mansion of principles built by Anna. Since yesterday I have felt elated to see you. I feel great joy. But sorrow too arises, friends! When I think that all of you were driven away, unable to live in the land filled with the pearl-sea and the jasmine grove, would tears not flow from my eyes—

I say these things because we who have come from Dravida have a duty to tell them. Today a wrong. incomprehensible notion is being spread among the people that we are a band of atheists. Who, truly, are the atheists in this country? Are the sinners who, in the name of God, have pushed and continue to push crores of people—society itself—into an abyss the atheists? Or are we, who labour with the rebirth of human society as our sole aim, the atheists? Which god, and in what nature, do you ask us to worship? Say that he is in the heart of the labouring worker. Call his labour God; call his strength God; there is meaning in that. But if, leaving that aside, you call what cannot be spoken of, what does not exist, “God,” how are we to believe it? What is God? Who is God? By way of explanation, one of our ancient Siddhars asked questions of these champions of orthodoxy: “Having called a planted stone a god, having placed four `puttam` upon it, circling around and muttering—what is this mantra?” We too ask only this. In the film `Melaikkari`, as Anna says, are we not asking for reform on the principle “One community, one God”? Even after seeing this, do you still lament that we are atheists? You say your God is the embodiment of knowledge and love. Are not all your gods, in truth, displayed as the very opposite—

In what way, in what sense, do you compare those days with these days? You board an aeroplane. You see Garuda flying beside you and slap your cheeks in reverence! You wear a wristwatch, and you consult Rahu Kalam! You make science a slave to Vedic orthodoxy! Is this honesty, is this good sense? And we who point this out are called an “atheist crowd,” comrades!

The DMK today continues to grow day by day, bearing many ideas and all manner of hardships. Even after the frenzy of domination, with its terrible maw, tasted the heroes Thalamuthu Natarajan, teacher Velayutham, Majeed and Pandian in an indelible manner, the DMK grows. Crushed by bullets, shells, cannons, police and armies, the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam still grows. Only after coming here was I able to see directly both how northern imperialism exploits the South and in what way Southerners have exploited the North. Our people from the South scrape and cure the hides of dead, rough goats here and work for traders for daily wages. But in the South, northern Marwari and Bania groups are skinning the people of the South alive. That is the heart-rending sight I have been able to see.

In streets overflowing with wealth—Thanjai or nanjai—there is the trade in children under Achariyar's rule! Of the beggars in northern Bombay, ninety-five out of every hundred are southern beggars. Are all these people hereditary beggars? No, no, comrades! The Tamil who lived a good life until before Swarajya rule now, because of northern domination and exploitation, has no means to live in his homeland and wastes away in the prosperous North as a beggar, as a pauper. When we see this, would our hearts not ache? It is a heart-breaking fact.

Our DMK is working so that such a miserable condition may disappear and a Dravidian federation may blossom. When we ask for Dravida to separate, it is not by showing the knife, showing the sword, or spilling blood; it is by peaceful means. By rational means, on the basis of `Bhoodan` and ethnic history, recalling our fallen great state, we ask to regain it. Just as Pakistan found a Jinnah, Dravida has our Anna. Under such leadership, stand in ranks by the thousands for the struggle. On the day the Dravidian republic blossoms, we must see the Dravidian autonomous-state flag raised in Bombay, comrades in Singapore let it fly, comrades in Africa make music for it, and comrades in Ceylon render a warrior's salute.

For such a liberation uprising, you comrades of Bombay, without party distinction, with the feeling that your own soil lies enslaved to another—
