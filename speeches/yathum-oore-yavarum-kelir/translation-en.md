The speech takes the Purananuru ideal “Every place is our town; everyone our kin” as the starting point for welcoming Auroville as an experiment in international fellowship.

Karunanidhi describes Auroville as a new settlement whose future cannot be judged from its early stage, comparing it to the seed of a banyan tree. He recalls local suspicion of unfamiliar innovations and a childhood memory of villagers fearing photography, using both to argue that new ideas are often resisted before becoming familiar.

He praises the growing cooperation between local villagers and residents from other countries, including foreigners learning colloquial Tamil. The projected Bharat Nivas and the separate State pavilions are treated as a model of “unity in diversity”: distinct identities are preserved while forming a larger union.

He announces Tamil Nadu Government support for the Tamil Nadu pavilion and promises cooperation with Auroville's development.

## Separate source closing note

Speech delivered on 28 October 1973 while laying the foundation stone for the Tamil Nadu pavilion at the international city of Auroville.
