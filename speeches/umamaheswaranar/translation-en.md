## Umamaheswaranar

Just as the great temple called Rajarajeswaram stands as a memorial to Rajaraja, the Karandai Tamil Sangam founded by Umamaheswaranar stands as a memorial to him.

When we say Thanjavur, the memory of Rajaraja Chola naturally comes to mind. In the same way, when Karandai is mentioned, one cannot fail to remember the late Tamilavel Umamaheswaranar. To unveil the statue of such a great Tamil scholar while the Tamil Sangam celebrates its diamond jubilee shows that, even if belatedly, the Tamil people do not fail to express their gratitude to him.

At a time when the people had forgotten the greatness, strength and antiquity of Tamil society, a group of young people with Tamil consciousness gathered in this Karandittaikudi area of Thanjavur more than sixty years ago. They sought to foster Tamil ethnic consciousness and protect the distinctiveness of the Tamil language. For nearly thirty years the heavy responsibility of preserving and nurturing that Sangam was borne by Tamilavel Umamaheswaranar, whose statue stands before us today.

## PDF 71 / printed p.70

At a time when speaking on public platforms in Tamil, writing in Tamil, and reporting world events in Tamil were regarded as signs of inferiority, as things unsuited to the modern world, while speaking and writing in other languages—particularly English—were treated as respectable and elevated, the late Tamilavel Umamaheswaranar brought about a major revolution through the Karandai Tamil College.

It was not merely the dominance of English. He also saw the intrusion of Sanskrit vocabulary reaching a point where one might fear that Tamils themselves would cease to cherish Tamil, and a condition in which a Tamil felt ashamed to speak in Tamil. Umamaheswaranar reflected on all this.

The young people of the Tamil Sangam deserve praise for the first steps by which they restored dignity to Tamil. Instead of the Sanskrit honorific “Sriman,” used for a respected person, Karandai Tamil Sangam first used the Tamil word “Thiruvalar.” It is no exaggeration to say that this distinction belongs to the Sangam and to Tamilavel Umamaheswaranar.

After Tamil consciousness began to rise in Tamil Nadu, many Sanskrit expressions that had enslaved common usage were replaced by Tamil forms.

Had this very platform been set up fifteen years earlier, when some began to speak from it,

## PDF 72 / printed p.71

they would not have addressed the chair as “vizha thalaivar” or “avai thalaivar.” They would have said, “Kanam Akkirasanathipathi avargale!”

If the tongue that once said “Akkirasanathipathi” can today say “avai thalaivar” with Tamil feeling, we cannot forget that this change came through the labour of people like Umamaheswaranar.

“Namaskaram” has today become “vanakkam.” Fifteen or twenty years ago, using “vanakkam” might have marked a person as belonging to a particular political movement and brought severe action against him. Such a period existed.

Today “vanakkam” has become an ordinary word in general use.

When we unveil statues of these people, we must remember at such functions what service they rendered to this country, what causes they worked for, and how selfless they were.

Umamaheswaranar spent his entire life labouring for the growth of this Tamil Sangam.

Even his death came while he was engaged in that work. He had gone to Calcutta. There he studied how institutions such as Santiniketan functioned; after that he set out for Banaras

## PDF 73 / printed p.72

Hindu University to learn how that university operated, intending to bring back what he had seen and the ideas he had gathered and use them to develop the Karandai Tamil Sangam.

On the journey from Calcutta to Banaras Hindu University he was struck by a severe fever and died.

He gave his life while performing his duty—and specifically while performing a duty for Tamil.

If we unveil the statue of that noble son today here in the Karandai area of Thanjavur, it is a matter of pride for all of us.

Standing before his statue on this good day, the pledge we must take is:

> We shall live with Tamil consciousness;  
> we shall live with the feeling, “We are Tamils”;  
> a Tamil will demean no one;  
> a Tamil will bow before no one.

Let us engrave that pledge in our hearts:

Long live Tamil.  
Let the Tamil people flourish.  
Let all of us labour so that our Tamil homeland may prosper.
