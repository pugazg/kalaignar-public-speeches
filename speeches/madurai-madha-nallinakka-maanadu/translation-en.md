### Madurai Religious Harmony Conference

I am extremely happy and proud to have received this good opportunity to see all of you, gathered here in your thousands at this conference, which activists belonging to five parties have run about and toiled to conduct with such distinction, dignity and enthusiasm — to establish religious harmony, to secure the rights of the States justly, and to extinguish the fire called untouchability. Five parties are on one platform today. If anyone asks, “Only five parties? Is that enough?”, the answer I give them is this: there are only five fingers on a hand. (Applause.)

If those five fingers are folded, they become a fist. A fist that strikes untouchability, religious fanaticism, and the forces that block the rights of the States. A fist that strikes and wins. When the five fingers fold, they become a fist. If more parties come one after another and the number becomes ten, what will we do? Ten fingers — two punches. These evils—

## PDF 271 / printed p.270

—to bring them down, to bring down untouchability, to bring down religious fanaticism, and to secure the rights of the States, this conference of these five parties is being held today in the city of Madurai as the arena for that purpose.

Everyone who spoke before me about religious harmony has explained it in great detail.

I wish to recall one thing. With the aim of fostering religious unity, a procession of flower-sellers called Phool Walon Ki Sair is held every year in New Delhi. To say the name of that procession in that language, it is Phool Walon Ki Sair. The flower-sellers' procession is now held every year in New Delhi. This is a festival that has been conducted since the Mughal period. This historically famous festival, held in the village of Mehrauli near Delhi, was inaugurated by Emperor Akbar (A.D. 1556–1605) in the interest of Hindu-Muslim unity and in order to emphasise national integration and religious harmony.

At this festival, Muslims and Hindus participate in large numbers with a feeling of brotherhood, without any difference among themselves, and in religious unity. In order to disrupt this festival in which Hindus and Muslims came together in such unity, in 1942 the British government, with its deceitful divide-and-rule—

## PDF 272 / printed p.271

—strategy, banned the festival itself. In 1942, the white rulers' government banned the festival jointly celebrated by Hindus and Muslims.

After independence, in 1961, Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru, wishing to foster Hindu-Muslim unity still further, breathed new life into that banned flower-sellers' procession.

This flower procession is the one festival in which Hindus and Muslims preserve their respective religious sentiments and, with those very religious sentiments, come together in unity and celebrate.

Without losing its ancient distinction, its traditions and practices continue to be observed in the same way even today.

On the day of the festival, Hindus and Muslims — the Muslims from the dargah and the Hindus from the Kali temple — form a procession, go together, pray to God and seek well-being.

At the head of this procession in which Hindus and Muslims go together, large fans (pankha), richly decorated with colourful flowers and beautiful velvet workmanship, are carried with splendour.

Following them, the cultural troupes proceed while presenting their artistic performances.

These flower fans are brought to the Mughal fort from Akbar's time at Mehrauli in New Delhi.

## PDF 273 / printed p.272

For this annual festival, various States of India send their pankhas, the corresponding fans and cultural troupes and take part. The festival lasts for one week, and cultural performances are held on the final day.

Since 1961, the Government of Tamil Nadu has sent pankhas and cultural troupes and made them participate in this festival. In most years, Tamil Nadu has won prizes for the specially designed pankha and for the cultural performance.

Even last year, two pankhas were carried in this flower-sellers' procession, and, in particular, an artistic performance was presented by the troupe of Dr. Somasundaram from Madurai.

Madurai Somasundaram received first prize for that artistic performance, and, in my capacity as Chief Minister, I received letters of appreciation from the Lieutenant Governor and the Chief Minister of Delhi for having sent that cultural troupe.

This year too, the flower-sellers' procession is to be held in New Delhi on October 11. We have received a message from there requesting us to send a cultural troupe this year also to take part in the festival. Accepting that request, the Government of Tamil Nadu has provided 55,000 rupees for sending the cultural troupe there and for Hindus and Muslims to come together and conduct that flower-sellers' procession. I wish to inform you of this as well.

## PDF 274 / printed p.273

The reason I mention this here is that, even in Akbar's time, an effort was made to ensure that Hindus and Muslims lived in unity; this flower festival was arranged for that purpose; it was banned for a period by the white rulers' government; and, when the great Nehru held the responsibility of Prime Minister of India, it began to be conducted again from 1961 and has continued ever since, and is still being held today.

But this festival is held only in Delhi. This flower festival should be held in the capital of every State. In Tamil Nadu, it should be held not only in the capital city of Chennai, but also in Madurai and Tiruchi. In this way, even though Hindus and Muslims belong to different religions, the feeling that they should live without religious division and associate with one another in unity of mind must grow.

Even today I saw a report in the newspapers. As our Communist Party of India leader Nallakannu pointed out, you may have heard that, when Vinayaka Chaturthi processions for Vigneswara were held in many places, at Kayalpattinam — a town where only Muslims live — something notable happened. I have visited that town many times. Thousands upon thousands of Muslim people live there. When the Vinayaka procession passed through that area today, Muslim men and women, elders and others welcomed the procession, honoured it with garlands, and expressed their support and welcome.

## PDF 275 / printed p.274

Not only at Kayalpattinam. At Palani, Muslims and Christians too joined that Vinayaka procession and added distinction to it. To be specific, when this Vinayaka procession reached Palladam near Coimbatore, we received news that Christians, Muslims, elders and young people of that area all gathered, welcomed the procession, mingled cordially with those who had come, praised them, rejoiced and celebrated.

For some, this may raise questions such as: Did they worship God? Did they worship the God of that religion? They may ask whether Karunanidhi can welcome and speak approvingly of two religious communities worshipping one God.

I put aside, as a secondary matter, whether I am an atheist or a theist — I rejoice in the fact that atheists or theists, both of them, are together. Muslims and Christians welcoming Vinayaka Chaturthi and rejoicing in it, and likewise, on another day, a situation arising in which Hindus take part in an Islamic festival — no one can say that these are contradictions.

What are we speaking about here? Religious harmony. I am duty-bound to cite this here as one path suited to that harmony. This religious harmony did not begin today or yesterday; Thiru. Vi. K., in an article written “To the youth...”, points out how, amid so many differences, it grew and became strong—

## PDF 276 / printed p.275

—among young people.

He says: “Who is a Tamil? One may say that those who from time immemorial have had Tamil as their mother tongue, and those who settled in Tamil Nadu and established their lives here, are Tamils. Among them there may be Saivites; there may be Vaishnavites; there may be Arugars; (Arugar means Jains), there may be Buddhists; there may be theists; there may be atheists. It must never be forgotten that all of them are Tamils. Here the condition of the Sangam age comes to mind. They were not all people of one doctrine. That is, the Tamil Sangam age, that Sangam period, comes to mind. In the Sangam period, everyone was not of one doctrine. Among them were Saivites, Vaishnavites, Arugars, Buddhists and others. All of them lived with brotherly affection. What was the reason? Their thought did not dwell upon religious difference or other differences. They thought of themselves as Tamils — brothers — and, with hearts of unity, served Mother Tamil. Were Cheran Senguttuvan, Ilango Adigal and Seethalai Sattanar, these three, followers of one religious doctrine? No. One was a Saivite; another was an Arugar; another was a Buddhist. What spiritual fellowship existed among them!

Tamil students! Think. Cheran Senguttuvan and Ilango Adigal were children born of the same mother's womb. Yet they lived as followers of different religious doctrines. Were there fanaticism, enmity or quarrels between them?

## PDF 277 / printed p.276

Was it not spiritual fellowship that permeated them? Before true love of Tamil, all small differences become like a veil of mist before the sun. Why should the Tamils of this age not live like them?” He thus asks the Tamils of this age today. From the Sangam age onward, Tamils lived in unity amid so many differences. How did they forget today to live that life? Tamil Thendral Thiru. Vi. K. asks.

It has been many years since he asked that question. Yet we are still asking it. On this platform, I and the leaders of four other movements are asking the same thing today. Untouchability is still running rampant in rural areas! Is this just? we ask. Even in urban areas described as regions of advancing civilisation, untouchability has still not disappeared. What is the reason? We want to know the reason. This conference is the effort we are making to bring it down. If someone asks, “If you hold one conference in Madurai, will religious harmony arrive? Will untouchability disappear? Will powers accumulate in the States?”, the question we must ask is whether, because of that, we should refrain from even beginning. Let us begin; let us begin. That is what my dear friend, Sankarayya, leader of the Marxist Communist Party, pointed out while speaking here.

“I met Karunanidhi and spoke with him. I met Thiru. Moopanar and spoke with him. They—

## PDF 278 / printed p.277

—are all in agreement,” Sankarayya pointed out. “Let all of us join together, travel on this issue, and win victory for your policy,” he said. I wish to state firmly here that the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam too is ready for that journey.

As he himself said here, this movement called the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam is one component of the Dravidian Movement. Thanthai Periyar took on the battle dress to bring down caste and eradicate religious fanaticism. With only slight changes in approach, our leader Perarignar Anna guided us. Therefore Sankarayya, our Nallakannu, our Vadivelu and Thiru. Moopanar all know very well the firmness of our policy on this issue. Therefore we are ready to walk firmly along with you.

Linked with this Religious Harmony Conference is the question of rights for the States, and our comrade Sankarayya has sounded the war drum here with great intensity and passion on that issue too. The slogan of State autonomy is not a cry that has arisen now merely because there are threats that the government of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam will be dismissed.

I wish to tell you this. After Arignar Anna passed away and I assumed responsibility for the government in 1969—

## PDF 279 / printed p.278

—within a few weeks I went to Delhi. In Delhi, on March 17, 1969, journalists from various newspapers in more than fifty Indian States met me and asked, “Your leader Arignar Anna used often to speak of greater powers for the States. What are you going to do about that?” The answer I gave them was: “I am considering the setting up of an expert committee to study Centre-State relations and recommend the powers that may be transferred from the Central Government to the States.”

I did not stop with saying it. Immediately after returning from Delhi, on August 19, 1969, on behalf of the government I constituted a three-member committee with Dr. P.V. Rajamannar as chairman, and Dr. Lakshmanasamy Mudaliar and Chandra Reddy as members.

That committee examined the question of granting greater powers to the States, including the ways in which those powers should be granted, and submitted a lengthy report. A committee functioning under the leadership of Murasoli Maran and Era. Sezhiyan examined that report; it was placed before the Cabinet and discussed; and, in 1974, in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly, a resolution was moved calling for greater powers for the States, for State autonomy, and for Article 356—

## PDF 280 / printed p.279

—to be removed entirely from the Constitution. When I moved that resolution, I say with pride that the party which supported it in the Legislative Assembly at that time was the Marxist Communist Party.

No one should forget one historical truth in this matter. Even before Indian independence, under the leadership of Comrade Ranadive — at a time when the Communist Party was one — the Communist Party of India was a party that passed a resolution insisting that greater powers should be given to the States and that there should be State autonomy.

Not only that. Before Indian independence, even the All India Congress party — the Congress party under the leadership of the great Nehru — was a party that accepted the policy that State autonomy should be granted.

After that, some changes occurred in the Congress party. Therefore there was a weakening in the policy of granting greater powers to the States. But from then until today, the Marxist Communist Party and the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, and the Tamil Maanila Congress party under our Moopanar that has emerged and grown — and has been growing — in Tamil Nadu today, as well as the Communist Party of India and the Janata Dal, are movements that have continued to insist that greater powers should be given to the States.

## PDF 281 / printed p.280

In 1974, on the basis of the report submitted by the Rajamannar Committee, I moved a resolution in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly and that resolution was passed by the Assembly. This was the resolution:-

“This House, having taken up for consideration the views of the Government of Tamil Nadu on State autonomy and on the recommendations of the Rajamannar Committee, together with the report of the Rajamannar Committee, resolves that, in order to preserve the unity of the Indian nation, which contains diverse languages, civilisations and cultures, to advance economic development, and to enable State administrations which are in close contact with the people to function without obstruction, the Central Government should accept the views placed by the Government of Tamil Nadu on State autonomy and on the recommendations of the Rajamannar Committee, and that the Constitution of India should be amended immediately on the basis of creating a true federal system with federation at the Centre and autonomy in the States.” I moved this resolution in the Legislative Assembly on 16.4.74, and it was passed.

I sent the resolution that had been passed to the then Prime Minister of India, Mother Indira Gandhi.

Even before that, when I sent the Rajamannar Committee report to Prime Minister Indira Gandhi in 1971, on receiving it—

## PDF 282 / printed p.281

—Prime Minister Indira Gandhi wrote to me on June 22, 1971:

Dear Thiru Karunanidhi,

I have received your letter of the 15th June with which you have sent me a copy of the report of the Centre-State Relations Inquiry Committee. Your Government will probably examine the recommendations of this report. As you know the Administrative Reforms Commission also went into the questions and has already submitted a report which is under our consideration. If the views of your Government on this matter are made available to us, they will also be taken into account. These are important issue and we intend to consult all the Chief Ministers.

With regards,

Yours Sincerely,

(Sd.) INDIRA GANDHI.

Yours affectionately,

(Sd.) Indira Gandhi.

That was the reply sent to me in 1971.

But after that, although a committee called the Sarkaria Commission was appointed to examine Centre-State relations, neither the Central governments that came in between nor the Central Government in office today has yet come forward to amend the Constitution and implement the policy of granting greater powers to the States. That is our grievance.

It is for that reason that here, along with resolutions on religious harmony and the abolition of untouchability, we have passed a very important resolution embodying the idea that greater powers should be given to the States — even if it does not bear the name “State autonomy”, it embodies that idea.

I wish to recall what Perarignar Anna said about the condition in which the States exist today.

“Our concern is that the rulers at the Centre desire a burden that they cannot carry.

## PDF 284 / printed p.283

If the strength of the Central Government means the total strength obtained by adding together the separate strength of each State, that is acceptable in argument, good for politics, and suitable in practice.

But if the Central Government alone is to hold all rights and strength, while the States are to possess only enough authority to toddle along, that is not fitting or acceptable.

By taking all the powers of the States and piling them up in the Central Government, not only will the States become weak, no new strength will thereby arise for the Central Government.

Let us think about all powers other than national defence. Let the States take the powers they need.

Then let the Central Government take the remaining powers that the States willingly give it.

It is the State Government, not the Central Government, that is intertwined with the people's joys and sorrows. Therefore it is wrong to say that only a strong Central Government can solve problems. If they hesitate to grant State autonomy, our doubt is whether, by bearing excessive powers again and again, they may lose the fort in great matters such as defence.

## PDF 285 / printed p.284

The doctrine of State autonomy was born precisely to change the condition in which all higher authority is in Delhi.”

That is what Anna pointed out.

It is on that basis that the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam proceeds. In keeping with that, an excellent resolution has been passed here at this conference.

We can no longer allow the Central Government to keep raising the sword, saying that governments may be dismissed whenever it thinks fit. We have twice been victims of such dismissal of governments. The first victim of such dismissal was the Communist Party in Kerala. So far, State governments have been dismissed more than ninety times using Article 356 of the Constitution. They seem determined somehow to score a century. We must take a firm resolve that we will no longer give room for that.

Likewise, they are thinking of using Article 355 as they please. Suddenly, they send a committee saying they must ascertain whether things are proper in the States and whether law and order are sound. Very well, the Central Government sends a committee to see whether law and order are proper in the States. Who is to send a committee to see whether law and order are proper at the Centre? Should there not be an answer to this question?

## PDF 286 / printed p.285

Therefore, this conference declares unequivocally that the responsibility for seeing whether government is being properly carried on in a State, and the responsibility for changing that government, belong to the people's forum and not to the government at the Centre.

There must be a change from the attitude that only those in the States are people who commit offences while everyone at the Centre is a pure self-radiant being. This resolution emphasises that point here.

Anyone and everyone says, “For that I will dismiss the State Government, I will dismiss it.” If that is consolation for them and a threat to us, we cannot go on enduring it. On coming to power at the Centre today, they proclaimed a “stable government”. Is the government now in office at the Centre a stable government? It is a government that staggers every day. This government came under the name “stable government”. The name this government has earned today is “a government that yields to pressures”. That is the name the Central Government of today has earned.

You may have read the story of the rat and the frog that tied their legs together and became companions. That story is today being enacted in the coalition-party government at the Centre—

## PDF 287 / printed p.286

—we must remain careful and vigilant. Let us take a solemn vow to protect the people from falling victim to religious fanaticism, to prevent the Central Government from raising its sword to destroy the rights of the States, and to make untouchability fall.
