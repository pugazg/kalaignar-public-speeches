# ‘We’ Means Democracy; ‘I’ Means Dictatorship — English translation

Karunanidhi begins by explaining that he has come to the Poompuhar College of Arts despite poor health and a pressing need to return to Chennai for the Assembly debate on the Governor's Address. He says his affection for the college and the students' persistent invitation made him determined to attend.

He praises the non-partisan spirit shown by students from different political affiliations and notes that the college had achieved cooperation among student groups even before similar political understandings emerged elsewhere.

The speech then turns to Poompuhar itself. Karunanidhi recalls the cultural structures and memorials created there—among them the seven-storeyed building, art gallery, Ilanji Mandram, Pavai Mandram and other historical and literary installations. He laments that some of these have been neglected after a change of government and argues that public institutions and cultural works should not be judged according to the individual who created them.

From that criticism he develops the speech's central idea. A society cannot progress through the arrogance of the individual “I.” Language, community, country and people's advancement rest on a broader sense of “we.”

Karunanidhi does not argue that the word “I” should disappear altogether. It is useful for identifying an individual action—“I came this morning,” for example. But when collective achievements are described as “I alone did this,” arrogance appears. Replacing that claim with “we did this” allows humility and collective responsibility to emerge.

He uses the college itself as an example: if every brick was carried by students and the cement was prepared through the contribution of teachers, its achievement belongs to “we,” not to any single person.

The conclusion states the thesis explicitly: **“We” is democracy; “I” is dictatorship, arbitrary rule.** The college should continue to grow through collective effort, discipline, dignity and cooperation, and he expresses the hope that it will earn the standing of a university.
