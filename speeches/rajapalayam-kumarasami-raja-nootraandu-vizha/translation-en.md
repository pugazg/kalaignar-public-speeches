### Kumarasami Raja Centenary Celebration at Rajapalayam

At a time when we are holding the centenary celebration of the great man who, though departed, has not disappeared but fills all our hearts — a patriot, a good and capable man who drew the hearts of the people of Tamil Nadu to a great extent — P.S. Kumarasami Raja, I take great pride that the present Government of Tamil Nadu has obtained the opportunity to take part in it, and that I am the Chief Minister of the Tamil Nadu Government that has received that opportunity.

Rajapalayam city wears a festive appearance today. All the roads leading to Rajapalayam are astir and stand decorated. In everyone's heart the name of Kumarasami Raja is being uttered today. As Moopanar pointed out, in order to honour his memory, it is not only this function that is taking place here; in ways that will endure permanently and be useful to the people,

## PDF 195 / printed p.194

a wedding hall, a library containing more than 30,000 fine books, and several other halls — many such useful undertakings are taking place today in connection with the centenary celebration.

When my dear friend and Member of the Legislative Assembly Azhagar Raja asked in the Assembly that this function should be held at Rajapalayam, I said, “It will be held.” I also said, “The government itself will take the lead in conducting it.” Even Azhagar Raja may have had a doubt. He may have wondered: he said it then, but will the government itself be able to conduct it today? The reason was the doubt whether the government itself would still be there by then. (Applause) But the government is here. And it is the government itself that is conducting this function.

In order to cooperate in conducting this function, the organising committee — and in particular our beloved Ramasubramania Raja and the other members working with him — have taken up their part in an excellent manner and created a situation in which this function can be distinguished in every way today. I convey to them my thanks, appreciation and good wishes.

When the literary scholar Kumari Ananthan spoke here, he observed that “Chief Minister Kalaignar has the nature of respecting national leaders and patriots.”

## PDF 196 / printed p.195

Not merely today or yesterday, but always, however many thousands of differences of opinion there may be, you all know very well that I have never lagged behind anyone in honouring and praising those who dedicated themselves and worked for the country, the language and the people, and in acting with the thought that their memory must endure forever.

They have listed here how many memorials have been established in Tamil Nadu under the DMK government. In particular, our literary scholar himself made what he called a request; and since I always accept such requests as commands, in fulfilment of that command a large memorial hall for Viswanath Das is being built today at Tirumangalam in Madurai.

Likewise, accepting what he said at a function in Chennai, a memorial hall for martyrs is being built in the Guindy area. Even yesterday I saw what he said on television. When television showed some pictures and said that portraits of the martyrs to be placed in that memorial hall are being prepared, I felt proud. I felt happy. The martyrs' memorial hall will be ready soon.

## PDF 197 / printed p.196

I wish to say one more thing.

Ramasubramania Raja mentioned in his welcome address, with overflowing gratitude, that he had requested that Kumarasami Raja's name be given to this district's Collector's office, and that I accepted that request in 89-90.

The Collector's office complex had already been named after Kumarasami Raja in 89. I wish to say one more thing. If, like Kumarasami Raja, I mention another name, it is a name all of you will remember. Though he did not govern Tamil Nadu for a long period, he governed for a short period in such a way as to occupy a place in people's hearts for a very long time. He was called “the beardless Periyar” by leaders of the Dravidian movement: Omandur Ramasamy Reddiar. Ramasamy Reddiar's centenary came in 1995. I was not in office then. His centenary came in 95. I went through old files to see whether the government then in office had made any effort to celebrate that centenary. An effort had been initiated, and the file recorded that three and a half lakh rupees would be spent for it, but the event was not held. The allotted three lakh rupees was not spent. I am one who can continue the good things some people have done. Somehow,

## PDF 198 / printed p.197

I can carry them through. That is not to display my greatness. It is to perform the duty we owe to great people. In that way, August 25 is the birth anniversary of the late Omandur Ramasamy Reddiar. On that day, the centenary celebration of Omandur Ramasamy Reddiar — the celebration that was intended to be held in 95 but was not held — will be conducted in a grand manner in Chennai city on August 25, 98.

I announce even now that our Moopanar and leaders of other parties will participate in it. For just as Kumarasami Raja governed with concern for ordinary, common people and took part in public life, Omandur Ramasamy Reddiar too lived simply and was pleasant in his dealings with everyone. At the same time, for the sake of the principle he held, he told not merely the leaders here but Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru, the Light of Asia himself, that it did not matter even if he lost office, and carried his principle into effect. For such an Omandur leader, the centenary in August — the centenary that was missed — will certainly take place even though it has been delayed by two or three years. I consider it appropriate to announce that at this function.

Because, at the centenary celebration being held for one former Chief Minister, another

## PDF 199 / printed p.198

former Chief Minister's centenary is being announced by me, the present Chief Minister; that is precisely why I say this is very appropriate. If you look at Kumarasami Raja's life and read it without leaving out a single line, as our Mullaivendhan pointed out while speaking here:

“Kumarasami Raja too had to lose his office for certain principles. Kalaignar too had to lose his in the same way.” This is not the only incident. Even in youth I see many incidents in his life that correspond with mine. Kumarasami Raja himself wrote in his biographical notes that when he was studying in high school he walked seven miles from Rajapalayam to Srivilliputhur to study. Moopanar knows this. There are 15 kal between Thirukkuvalai, where I was born, and Tiruvarur. I had to walk seven miles to the village of Tirunellikaval there in order to board the train. I too walked seven miles, boarded the train, and went to Tiruvarur to study.

So these seven miles form a very strong similarity between Kumarasami Raja and me. I even wonder whether that one similarity too may be a reason I came to take part in this function. Another thing: he too failed the S.S.L.C.

## PDF 200 / printed p.199

I did as well. (Applause) When he failed the S.S.L.C., his friends apparently felt sorry. Kumarasami Raja is said to have told them then, “Why are you feeling sorry? Is this something to feel sorry about?” I too said the same kind of thing when I failed. Not only did I not feel sorry; I went travelling with Periyar. In fact, it was because I travelled with Periyar that I even failed then.

Likewise, Kumarasami Raja too failed the S.S.L.C. because of his involvement in public life. He is said to have said that no one should feel sorry about it, and that he himself would not.

Another thing: he noted in the account of his youthful life that among sports he had the greatest liking for football, and that while playing it he fell and broke half a tooth.

I too had great involvement in football. While studying in Tiruvarur, I took part in many football matches. I did not break a tooth. But I have a wound on my forehead. It is still there today. It is a scar caused by falling while playing football.

In so many ways, in our youth, Kumarasami Raja's life and my

## PDF 201 / printed p.200

life correspond. I wonder whether Ramasubramania Raja, having read both our life histories together, insisted that I should come to this function for that reason.

I think of this district. I think of the surrounding region. Another incident Kumarasami Raja himself records from his life is that he had a student friend belonging to the Iyengar community. One day, when the two went to the market street, the student from the Iyengar community went into an Iyer's shop and bought and ate a vadai. The one who bought and ate the Iyer-shop vadai was an Iyengar — Kumarasami Raja's friend. His father came to know that the Iyengar boy had bought and eaten the vadai. He beat the Iyengar boy, saying, “You have done something improper. Can you eat a vadai from an Iyer's shop?” Not only did he beat him; to remove the ritual pollution, he mixed milk and water, added cow dung to it, added cow urine, and made him drink it, saying, “Drink this. Only then will the pollution be removed.” Even then there was caste hostility between Iyers and Iyengars — and its shadow remains even today.

Kumarasami

## PDF 202 / printed p.201

Raja is the one through whom we learn that a century ago, in this district and in other districts, Iyers and Iyengars themselves were divided by caste hostility. Such caste differences, religious differences, religious clashes and caste clashes have existed in Tamil Nadu for a century. There were many people who grieved over them and thought they must end, that they must be removed. When we think that people like Kumarasami Raja were among them, we can understand how great the task before us is.

If he had wished in his youth, he was born into a great royal family. He was wealthy, with lands and gardens in abundance. Yet instead of desiring that pleasant and comfortable life, he was imprisoned once for a year and another time for nine months, dedicating himself to the country and the people. That such an impulse arose in him in his youth is striking. Reading those memories of his youth makes me wonder with concern: will the young people of Tamil Nadu today reform themselves? Will they think, “We too should become like Kumarasami Raja”? For I am a person filled with great sorrow and concern.

My concern is about how today's young people are going to be of use in protecting this country, society, language and people in the future. Some fifty years ago, young people

## PDF 203 / printed p.202

in their student days, whatever movement they had committed themselves to — whether the Congress movement, the Dravidian movement or the Communist movement — would, in colleges and schools and at literary lectures, learn epics, read histories, critically discuss historical heroes, converse with one another about following their path, hold discussions and debates. These were habits of the young people of earlier times. But today's young people — I am not speaking of everyone. Even today some have that same drive.

There are young people who are like spearheads protecting this society, with a spirit of service and a desire to labour for it. But we are living in a time when many young people think it is enough to spend their time on entertainment, shows, cinema and extravagance, and make a pleasure-city itself the goal of their lives. This must change. I believe that the youthful life of people like Kumarasami Raja will serve as a lesson in bringing about that change.

The man who gave us such a lesson was former Chief Minister and former Governor of Orissa P.S. Kumarasami Raja. Those two English letters, “P.S.,” that appear before his name — I do not think of them as the initials of his family or his father's

## PDF 204 / printed p.203

names. I take P.S. to mean “Public Servant,” because he lived precisely as a public servant. He was not concerned about victory or defeat — not merely in examinations, not merely in office, but even in the politics in which he was involved. When victory and defeat came alternately, he regarded both alike and worked on the principle that the country was important and society was important. It is not enough for us merely to remember and rejoice in the fame of such a great man today. We are conducting this function with the hope that he should become a good lesson for future young people.

Our Ramasubramania Raja has placed a few requests here. There are two or three requests. Though I cannot immediately reply here to all of them, there is one important request: Greenways Road in Chennai, where ministers live. Our reception committee chairman has asked that the name of that road be changed to Kumarasami Raja Road. Accepting that request, the government will take steps within another day or two to have that name changed. (Loud applause) Expressing my confidence that the Chennai Corporation Council too will cooperate, I take leave saying, “Long live the fame of Kumarasami Raja.”
