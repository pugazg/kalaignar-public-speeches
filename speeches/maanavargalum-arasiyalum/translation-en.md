Karunanidhi addresses students at the concluding day of a Valluvar festival at Madras Christian College. His central advice is that students may understand politics and hold convictions, but during their student years they should first master their studies. He compares colleges and universities to places where swords, spear-points and other instruments are being forged: unless students are properly sharpened and formed through education, they cannot become effective instruments of public life later.

He invokes Anna's image of students as seedlings in a nursery. They should grow strongly in the nursery before being transplanted into the field. Political preferences may exist in their hearts, he says, but partisan commitment should wait until the student stage has been completed.

Karunanidhi then reflects on the fact that he himself entered politics as a student, joining Periyar on political tours while supposedly preparing for his SSLC examination. He immediately warns students not to treat exceptions as models: Kamaraj's lack of formal education does not mean others should deliberately remain uneducated. “Exceptions are different; guides are different.”

He identifies two exceptional kinds of struggles in which students may become unavoidable participants: defending the land from subjugation and defending a language that is as precious as one's eyes. Even here, he distinguishes such causes from ordinary partisan mobilisation.

The final section discusses the January 25–26 language-related agitation. He says the coming Republic Day is not being declared a day of mourning; rather, Tamil people's grievance over language policy is being expressed on that day. He also rejects speculation that the agitation is designed to provoke dismissal of the State government. If the State government were threatened merely for permitting the black-flag event, he says he would stand in support of that State government.
