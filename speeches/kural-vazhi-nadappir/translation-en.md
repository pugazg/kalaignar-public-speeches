Karunanidhi opens the Ilango forum at Thiagarajar College by paying tribute to Thiagarajar, whose work helped create the college as both an artistic temple and a centre of learning. He compares the students' spirited welcome to the martial energy associated with Cheran Senguttuvan, while noting that the gathering is formally in the name of the poet Ilango.

He asks students to think beyond immediate questions of examinations and employment. The past should not simply be recreated, but the intellectual and cultural resources of ancient Tamil society should help Tamil people compete with modern scientific and civilisational development. Old literature, he says, should be used as nourishment for thinking about the future.

The speech then moves through the landscapes of classical Tamil poetics and the religious references associated with them. Karunanidhi uses Tolkappiyam, Tirumular and the Tirukkural to argue against hierarchy by birth. The Kural line “பிறப்பொக்கும் எல்லா உயிர்க்கும்…” becomes a central statement that distinctions arise from work, not from birth.

He develops the argument through the familiar body-and-fig-tree analogy: even if people were imagined as born from different parts of a divine body, all remain human, just as fruit growing on different parts of the same fig tree remains the same fruit. Students, he says, are one community while they are together in the college, and should resist the caste identities that may divide them later.

Karunanidhi then turns to Sangam literature. He contrasts Kalidasa's image of a frog unknowingly taking shade beneath a cobra with a Tamil poetic image from the Palai tradition in which a stag deliberately gives its own shade to the suffering doe. His point is that Tamil literature binds literary beauty to mutual understanding and lived human relationships.

The Tirukkural is presented as an especially rich guide to life. He cites the couplets on a father's duty to make his son foremost in an assembly and a son's duty to live so well that others marvel at the merit of his father. Students should become such children and such social assets.

He urges them not merely to preserve Akananuru, Purananuru and other Sangam works, but to read, enjoy and reinterpret them so their meaning reaches others. Recalling his own youthful effort to render difficult Purananuru material in accessible Tamil under the title `கவிதையல்ல`, he asks the students to continue that work in their own generation.

The speech closes with a call for them to become courageous children of Tamil society, protect Tamil Nadu and Tamil society, and move forward with confidence.

## Separate source closing note

Speech delivered at a Thiagarajar College function in Madurai on 20 October 1980.
