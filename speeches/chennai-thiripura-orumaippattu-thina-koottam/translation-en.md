## Chennai - Tripura Solidarity Day Meeting

I stand before you to second the resolution proposed here by the chair of the function, Comrade Soundararajan. All of us on this stage have seconded this resolution. If I must put it specially, this is an alliance that does not keep one thing inside and speak another outside. (Applause) If this alliance seconds it, that means Tamil Nadu itself seconds it. (Loud applause)

Whenever democracy is endangered in any corner of India, Tamil Nadu is the first state to raise its voice. When the Emergency was proclaimed, it was leaders in the northern states who were arrested. Lok Nayak Jayaprakash Narayan, Morarji Desai and others were arrested. But you know very well that in Tamil Nadu, on seeing that and seething with anger, Perunthalaivar Kamaraj too,

## PDF 392 / printed p.391

the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, the Marxist Communist Party and the left parties raised their voices.

Likewise, recently in Bihar, when an attempt was made to dismiss the government, and the recommendation to use Article 356 reached the President, the first state to raise its voice in opposition was Tamil Nadu.

In Madhya Pradesh, a Congress-ruled state, when the news came that Christian women, nuns, had been raped, the state that sprang up, organised marches and expressed its protest was Tamil Nadu.

Therefore, whether injustice or wrongdoing takes place in any part of India, or whether it becomes clear that democracy is being harmed and degraded, Tamil Nadu is the state that will rise in response. It is in that spirit that Tamil Nadu has assembled here today to remove the evil that has arisen in the tiny region of Tripura.

Though this is a meeting being held in one hall, whether we realise it or not, the intelligence wing of the Central Government will know very well that this meeting collectively expresses the thoughts of all the people of the state.

I read this resolution not once or twice, but many times. This

## PDF 393 / printed p.392

resolution, even if Prime Minister Vajpayee himself were to read it, would not make him angry. If one thinks about what a resolution adopted by the Marxist Communist Party and supported by parties such as the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam would be like, or what the ministers at the Centre might expect, the heat they expect is not in this resolution. Even if there is no heat, there is sting; in that sense this resolution has been framed here and has come before us to be seconded.

I too read it again and again. I searched closely to see whether anywhere there was even one word saying that the way the Central Government was acting on the Tripura issue, the way the Vajpayee Government was acting, the way the BJP Government was acting, was condemnable. There is none. The reason is that the aim of the resolution is not to condemn the BJP Government; what we are giving primacy to here is the Tripura issue. That is why the conclusion of the resolution says only this: “Therefore, accepting the request of the Tripura State Government, this meeting requests the Prime Minister of India that the Central Government should immediately provide additional army units and financial assistance and come forward to safeguard the unity of the State of Tripura and the unity of the country.”

It does not even say that it “strongly urges” them. Perhaps they fear that if it becomes too strong it might

## PDF 394 / printed p.393

hurt! (Applause) The whole of our view is contained in another sentence here: “This problem of Tripura is not merely a problem of that state; it is also a problem connected with the security of the country. It is a problem of this nation.” From that alone, all of you can understand the good intention of this meeting and the good intention of this resolution. Whether it is understood there or not, I do not know. (Loud applause) I ask that it be understood.

What is happening in Tripura? Those words are set out in the resolution: “In the past six months, thirty people, including State Health Minister Bimal Sinha, leaders of the Marxist Communist Party, members of their families and tribal people, have been massacred. More than twenty-five people have been abducted.” That is what the resolution says. If we permit such abductions, support them and applaud them, then later we too will be abducted. (Applause) We must not forget that. They say one knows the pain of a headache and fever only when one gets them oneself. Today the Health Minister in Tripura is abducted. In the same constituency, Ranjithriyang, the son of Marxist Communist Party Member of Parliament Baju Banriyang, is abducted by extremists.

## PDF 395 / printed p.394

In the first week of August, five officers were brutally killed at the Narendarpur tea estate. Extremists suspected of belonging to the banned Tripura National Liberation Front movement abducted twenty people in the first week of August alone. Among them, Marxist supporter Rathishiyamsha and school student Deepak Majumdar are noteworthy. A businessman named Breeb Malagar was abducted by extremists from his house in the Sandhi Bazaar area.

At the end of that same August, extremists shot dead eleven people in two days in Tripura. At the end of September, three people were killed. Two students belonging to a hill-dwelling community were abducted, beaten and killed.

These extremists of the National Front even hijacked an entire bus. They ordered the male passengers on it to get down from the bus and run. Those passengers ran for four kilometres. Some of them fainted while running. The violent men beat and tortured them and also shot dead one man who fell down. Seeing all these atrocities, the Chief Minister of Tripura, Mr. Manik Sarkar of the left party, took a very accommodating position: even if the extremists in Tripura wished to hold direct talks with the Central Government,

## PDF 396 / printed p.395

the Tripura Government would not interfere. Without standing on pride or prestige and insisting that only we must conduct the talks, he has said that, with the good intention that peace must somehow come to Tripura, that security there must not be impaired, and that no damage of any kind must be caused to India's unity, the Tripura Government would not intervene.

He has said that if such talks were held between the Central Government and the extremists, he would be happy, and that if the extremists came forward for talks with the Central Government, he himself would arrange the meeting. When the State Chief Minister has come down with such broad-mindedness and said this, the message conveyed through this meeting is that it is not proper for the Central Government still to remain silent without listening to the State Government's request, the people's difficulties or the atrocities taking place.

The situation has become such that when the government at the Centre is asked to provide the army, even a part of the army already there is taken away. By explaining this at this meeting, we point out that Tripura is a tiny region with a population of about twenty-eight lakhs — Tripura has a smaller population than Chennai

## PDF 397 / printed p.396

does. That Tripura, after India became independent in 1947, has been functioning since 1949 as a free state out of a princely state. Maithili Sivaraman mentioned here that the Marxist Communist Party has assumed office there for the fourth time. I do not know whether their request is perhaps being rejected because they have come to office for the fourth time. For even in Tamil Nadu, the Central Government does not appear to be paying attention to the representations of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam government, which too has come to office for the fourth time. (Applause)

If our dear Member of Parliament Khadardas has come here to state his grievance, I am compelled to state our grievance through him. (Applause)

The Tamil Nadu Government asked the Central Government for certain assistance to suppress extremism in Tamil Nadu. It has been many months since we asked that a battalion of the Central Government's rapid-action force be permanently stationed in Coimbatore. No way has yet opened for it. It has been many months since I asked Union Home Minister Mr. Advani himself that the Central and Tamil Nadu governments jointly establish two Indian Reserve battalions in Tamil Nadu, sharing the expenditure. It has still not been fulfilled.

## PDF 398 / printed p.397

Because the Al-Umma and Jihad Committee movements operate from other states as well, a request was made to the Central Government on 18.3.98 that they be banned at the all-India level. It has still not been fulfilled.

Several months ago, I met the Home Minister in person and asked that the Central Government provide financial assistance of ten crore rupees every year to modernise the police. It has still not been fulfilled.

Since the Explosives Control Act is a Central law, I sent a letter on behalf of the State Government to the Central Government on 21.3.98 asking it to impose strict rules governing the possession and transport of explosives. This too has still not been fulfilled.

The reason I say all this is that we must not forget that this will remain the situation so long as there are Central Governments that think it beneath their dignity to heed the requests of State Governments immediately. It is to change that attitude that for a long time we have raised the demand that State Governments should function with autonomy and that the government formed at the Centre should function with a federal character.

The alliance of parties that agree with that demand is the alliance assembled on this stage. If the voice of this alliance

## PDF 399 / printed p.398

is raised today for Tripura, the Central Government must understand that it is a voice raised for the welfare of the people of Tripura and for the security of India, not for the advantage of the parties present here, and must act immediately. It must send the army. The army that had been there and was withdrawn must be sent there again. The strong protection that the State Government asks for must be provided to them.

We must remember that Tripura too is a part of India. I state that this meeting is precisely to give that reminder, and I take leave by saying that I second this resolution on behalf of all the people of Tamil Nadu.
