## A Temple for Valluvar

When Perarignar Anna formed the government in 1967, he assigned me the Public Works portfolio. We arranged for Thiruvalluvar's portrait to be placed in all Tamil Nadu government buses and for a Kural to be inscribed in them. At that time, Vinayagam, who sat in the opposition benches of the Legislative Assembly, looked at Anna and asked: “You have written ‘Whatever else you may fail to guard, guard your tongue’ in the buses. For whom is that Kural written — for the driver or for the conductor?”

Anna immediately replied: “For everyone who has a tongue.”

The Thirukkural contains such fine couplets.

Not only that: it was also decided that portraits of Thiruvalluvar should be placed in all tourist rest houses belonging to the Public Works Department — government tourist bungalows, municipal travellers' lodges and the like.

After Perarignar Anna passed away, it was decided that on his birthday medals should be given to outstanding members of the police force,

## PDF 30 / printed p.29

along with cash awards and certificates for distinguished service. When the question arose whose image should be engraved on such a medal, it was Thiruvalluvar's image that we chose.

Why? So that we should not forget the counsel: “Raise the rod swiftly, but let it fall gently.”

The government has taken steps of this kind so that Thiruvalluvar and the Thirukkural may become deeply fixed in the minds of the people.

When the World Tamil Conference was held in Chennai, it was decided to grant three lakh rupees each to Annamalai University, Madurai University and the University of Madras — nine lakh rupees in all — for Thirukkural research. A ceremony was held on the beach to hand the grants to the Vice-Chancellors, and a former President of India also participated.

Research is now proceeding with the concern, enthusiasm and support of Vice-Chancellors and Tamil scholars.

The government is engaged in various activities to fix the Thirukkural and Thiruvalluvar in people's hearts and to spread the greatness of the Kural throughout the world.

In that spirit, we wished to renovate this ancient Valluvar temple and make it a place of historical distinction.

## PDF 31 / printed p.30

When I wished to establish the Silappathikaram Art Gallery and the sculptural work for it was under way at Mamallapuram, I used to visit there at least twice a month.

The students of the sculpture college working there asked: “When this work is over, you must give us other work.”

I returned wondering anxiously, “What work can I give them?”

They said: “Send us to renovate all the temples that have fallen into disrepair.”

At Poompuhar, foundations have been laid not only for the Silappathikaram Art Gallery but for eight additional structures. Those eight buildings too will become places fragrant with art.

With the aim of fulfilling the promise made to the Mamallapuram students, creating an imperishable memorial to Thiruvalluvar, enabling the world to rejoice in Tamil Nadu's artistic wealth, developing tourism and at the same time helping the economy to grow, the Thiruvalluvar memorial is being created in the Nungambakkam area of Chennai.

We intend to design this memorial in the form of the Tiruvarur temple car. We called the committee together many times before reaching this decision — because this concerns Thiruvalluvar, and no one should be able to find fault!

This chariot-shaped memorial will be built entirely of black stone.

## PDF 32 / printed p.31

Our desire is to place all 1,330 Kurals there, together with sculptures illustrating each Kural. All this will cost many lakhs of rupees.

It was said here that the government should assist this effort. Such things are not being done because someone asked us to do them; the government itself has taken the initiative.

Certainly, very soon the Thiruvalluvar memorial will rise for the people of the world to see and enjoy.

I ask everyone to cooperate so that the temple may be established, rather than leaving it merely to the government.
