# Our Lady of Velankanni Church Silver Jubilee

Karunanidhi speaks at the Silver Jubilee of the Our Lady of Velankanni church in Besant Nagar. He opens by joking that the preceding speakers have spoken so poetically that he cannot tell whether the gathering is a silver-jubilee function or a poetry session. He recalls having come to the church roughly twenty-five years earlier to ring its bell and says he is grateful to return for the jubilee.

Responding to Peter Alphonse, he says his continued opportunities to serve as Chief Minister come less from personal strength than from the goodwill of the people before him. A discussion of “Mother Velankanni” becomes a broader tribute to motherhood. He says he reveres his own mother Anjugam, Sivagami because she gave birth to Kamaraj, Sathya because she gave birth to M. G. R., and Mother Velankanni because she gave birth to Jesus.

Karunanidhi then recalls the transformation of Besant Nagar and Elliot's Beach. He describes an earlier shoreline covered with refuse, thorny growth and wind-blown thorn balls, and says Anna's `சீரணிப்படை`, local residents and fishermen helped clean the area. In that setting, he presents the Velankanni church as a symbol of cleanliness and recalls Archbishop Arulappa asking him to ring the church bell when the institution was established.

The speech contains a characteristic wordplay about being a “festival Chief Minister.” Karunanidhi notes that he had been Chief Minister during several silver and golden jubilees, then jokes that the phrase does not mean “Festival Chief Minister” but a Chief Minister who does not fall — and that he remains standing because of the support of the people.

He turns to the church's Silver Jubilee souvenir, quoting passages that warn that blind religious practice can make faith an object of ridicule and damage both religion and humanity. Another passage praises Mary's motherhood not merely in giving birth to Jesus but in service, compassion and courage. Karunanidhi says these qualities — resolve, courage, motherhood and affection — should shape human life.

The address then explains why he attends functions of different religions. He says a Chief Minister must be common to all people. He will attend wherever people are invited as equals, regardless of caste or religion, and he recalls participating in communal meals at the Madurai Meenakshi temple and Kapaleeswarar temple alongside leaders of different parties and faiths. What he rejects, he says, are invitations from those whose conduct denies equality or threatens other religions.

Returning to a line from the Jubilee souvenir, he recalls his `Parasakthi` dialogue: the objection is not to temples themselves but to temples becoming the dens of the wicked. He links this to an anecdote about Anna, who said that he did not attend a religious function because of miracles but because of love, peace and the recognition of all people as human beings.

Karunanidhi connects that idea with Anna's popularisation of Tirumular's phrase **“ஒன்றே குலம், ஒருவனே தேவன்”** and with the egalitarian aspirations of Bharati, Vallalar Ramalinga Adigal, Bharathidasan and Gandhi.

He also interprets the Christian cross as more than a sign of sacrifice: visually it is a plus sign, a symbol of bringing people together. That, he says, expresses the value of a faith that joins human beings rather than divides them.

Karunanidhi closes by saying he does not claim to have completed all his duties. He asks the gathering for the strength, support and goodwill needed to continue serving during the rest of his life.
