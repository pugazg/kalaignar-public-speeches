### Function to Present Ex Gratia Grants for the Books of Thiru. Vi. Ka. and Kalki

When our beloved Saavi spoke here, he raised a question to such an extent that one might wonder whether he was giving the special address for this function or speaking in a debate: which is the finest among the districts — Thanjavur, North Arcot, or Chengalpattu? That was the argument he placed before us.

Finally, setting North Arcot and Thanjavur against each other, he asked: what does Thanjavur have? You say it has the Cauvery. But, he pointed out, there is no certainty that water will come in the Cauvery. Even if there is no certainty that water will come in the Cauvery, we are engaged in struggling, pursuing legal proceedings and trying to secure it. But I request Saavi not to forget what has happened to the Palar of your district. (Applause)

Saavi said, “Was Kalaivanar born in Thanjavur? Was he not born in Kanyakumari!” Though he was born in Kanyakumari,

## PDF 163 / printed p.162

I firmly believe Saavi has shown here that the person who established the ‘Kalaivanar Arangam’ in his name was a man of Thanjavur.

Such competition among districts is necessary. If that competition is about how to develop Tamil, how to work for Tamil and how to render service, I welcome it. I firmly believe that this is the kind of competition Saavi has presented here.

Today we are honouring the memory of two great men and offering the tribute that we owe them. We are expressing our gratitude here for the rare service they rendered to the country, to society and to the language. Not only we, but our future society too must remember them, follow their path and labour for the country and the language; it is with that purpose that we are conducting this function here.

Saavi expressed great surprise here that, by bringing these books under state ownership, an ex gratia grant of Rs. 20 lakh was given to these families. I do not regard giving Rs. 20 lakh to their families as something great. For the achievements they have given us, for the service they have rendered and for the work they have done for Tamil, this Rs. 20 lakh is a very, very

## PDF 164 / printed p.163

ordinary sum, in my view.

That is why we have described and given it as an ex gratia grant — out of regard for them, not with the thought that this is enough for them. Nor even, as Kalki Rajendran indicated here, merely as a recognition; since it is being given on behalf of the government, perhaps the word “recognition” used by Rajendran here may be appropriate. But he himself read out here the review written by Kalki about the dialogue I wrote for the film *Manohara*.

So the truth in it is that Kalki had given me recognition forty years ago itself. (Applause) Friend Rajendran has reminded me here of that recognition. I feel elated whenever I think of it. I too read that review at the time. When I had written the dialogue for *Manohara* and the film was running successfully, there could have been no limit to the joy I felt when that review by Kalki appeared.

Not only *Manohara*. Kalki also wrote a review of *Velaikkari*, written by Perarignar Anna, who shaped all of us among the writers of the Dravidian movement, and praised it greatly,

## PDF 165 / printed p.164

as well. If I am to remind you of one more thing: when Anna’s play *Or Iravu* was staged in Thanjavur, Kalki presided over it, and it was in the speech he delivered there that Professor Kalki referred to Arignar Anna as the “Bernard Shaw of South India.”

Such a Professor Kalki was not only broad-minded but magnanimous. Whoever was a capable writer, speaker or Tamil scholar, wherever they were and whatever movement they belonged to, he never failed or forgot to praise them. That is why, when he died, Perarignar Anna wrote as follows in the weekly *Dravida Nadu* dated 12-12-1954.

Its title was “Amarathara has gone.”

“He was a writer who introduced humour into Tamil prose and stirred an interest in historical events even in the kitchen. There are many differences of opinion about his writing, his activity and his views. But his talent and capacity for action certainly deserve praise.

“Even though the entire nationalist camp shot arrows of irritation, there were times when he praised our services. The renaissance our movement created in the field of writing produced irritation and anger among many nationalist writers. But Kalki welcomed it and rejoiced. In the same way, in an opponent

## PDF 166 / printed p.165

he never hesitated to praise certain good qualities! He was not afraid to offer congratulations! That is why his death causes grief to many today. To us in particular, his death truly brings pain!” Thus wrote Perarignar Anna in *Dravida Nadu* when Professor Kalki died. Kalki created not only historical novels such as *Ponniyin Selvan* and *Sivagamiyin Sabatham*, but novels that can stand forever in history. He had boundless affection not only for the Tamil language but also for Tamils and the Tamil people. In particular, because he was born in the Chola realm, we must not forget that, as a sign of the extent of his affection for the Chola kings, he named his own son Rajendran.

It was Professor Kalki who established a memorial hall for Bharathiyar at Ettayapuram. When our Tamilkudimagan spoke here about his distinctions, he pointed out that Kalki had taken part in the freedom struggle and had gone to prison many times.

This government has never failed to honour those who worked for freedom. Among those freedom fighters, one who stood out as an honoured warrior was Tamil Thendral Thiru. Vi. Ka. Speaking here about Tamil Thendral Thiru. Vi. Ka., our

## PDF 167 / printed p.166

function president Tamilkudimagan referred to the views he had expressed in support of communal representation. But even if that is true, we must not forget that Thiru. Vi. Ka. was also one who opposed the policy of communal representation. That is history.

Thanthai Periyar left the Congress. It was from the conference held at Kanchipuram in 1925 that Thanthai Periyar left the Congress. That conference was held under the chairmanship of Tamil Thendral Thiru. Vi. Ka. The resolution Periyar brought at that conference was the resolution insisting on communal rights. The conference did not accept it. Because the conference president, Thiru. Vi. Ka., too hesitated to accept it, it was then that Thanthai Periyar walked out of the Kanchipuram conference. He left the Congress. That was one period.

But if proof is needed of the victory of the Dravidian movement, the very Tamil Thendral Thiru. Vi. Ka. who had presided over that conference and had not accepted Periyar’s communal rights policy later accepted Periyar’s communal policy and came to the point of saying that it was something that should be joined with the “national song.” If that happened, that was Periyar’s victory. (Applause) I am duty-bound to point out that it was the victory of the Dravidian movement.

Not only that; Rajaji and Thiru. Vi. Ka. were among those who, in those days, did not accept the very expression “Dravida Nadu” put forward by Periyar and Arignar

## PDF 168 / printed p.167

Anna.

I believe Rajendran will remember. That same Rajaji wrote in *Kalki*: even if “Dravida Nadu” is obtained, I will be happy about it; I will fight for Dravida Nadu. The veteran Rajaji reached a position where he could write that. Thiru. Vi. Ka., who had not accepted the expression “Dravida Nadu,” later opened the Dravida Nadu picture at the Dravidar Kazhagam conference held in Cuddalore. The slogan that rang out when he spoke there still rings in my ears. “Dravida-Nadu, Dravida Nadu, Dravida Nadu,” he said three times. Thiru. Vi. Ka. would say everything three times in his speeches. He did the same that day and said that we would certainly secure it.

Thiru. Vi. Ka. is no longer among us today; the Dravida Nadu policy too is no longer with us today. You all know the event in which Perarignar Anna, considering the welfare of the country, India’s security, sovereignty and unity, convened the party general council, passed a resolution and gave up that policy.

Even so, there was a time when even those who had expressed views contrary to the policies put forward by the Dravidian movement later came to accept them. It is only to indicate that development in the country that I wished to mention this here.

## PDF 169 / printed p.168

Thiru. Vi. Ka. is praised as Tamil Thendral, the Tamil breeze. As those who spoke here pointed out, he also appeared as a storm. He was a leader of workers. The union of thirteen thousand workers employed in the Buckingham and Carnatic Mills functioned then under the leadership of Thiru. Vi. Ka. The first trade union in India was formed in Chennai. The man who headed it was Thiru. Vi. Kalyanasundaranar. When he led that trade union, the Governor of the Madras Presidency was Lord Wellington. At that time a major struggle was taking place at the Buckingham and Carnatic Mills. It was a workers’ struggle. Wellington unleashed various forms of repression to suppress that struggle. Without bowing his head to any of it, Thiru. Vi. Kalyanasundaranar stood with his shoulders held high, organised workers in contingent after contingent and stood in the field of struggle.

The then British lord Wellington, filled with uncontrollable anger, ordered the deportation of Thiru. Vi. Ka. At that time the Justice Party was in office. The Raja of Panagal was the Chief Minister of the Madras Presidency — that is, he was then called the Premier, the Prime Minister. When news came that an order had been issued to deport Thiru. Vi. Ka., the pioneers of the Dravidian movement, Theagaraya and Natesanar, joined together and approached the Raja of Panagal

## PDF 170 / printed p.169

and said: immediately go and meet the Governor and make him withdraw the deportation of Thiru. Vi. Ka. If Lord Wellington refuses to listen, all of you come out of the ministry. We do not want the responsibility of office. Theagaraya sent them with that instruction. Hearing it, the Raja of Panagal and the ministers in his cabinet immediately met Wellington and firmly told him that Thiru. Vi. Ka. must not be deported. Wellington angrily asked, “Who are you to say that Thiru. Vi. Ka. should not be deported? I will deport him.” They replied, “Then here are our resignation letters,” and threw down their letters of resignation. Wellington yielded to that and, with no other course, withdrew the deportation order that was to have been carried out that day. That is the history of Tamil Nadu. (Applause)

The Justice Party has been criticised as though it was a party that wagged its tail for the white man. It was the Justice Party that, through this very incident, shattered that criticism even then. That party is what is called the Dravidian movement. I can give any number of examples to show that this Dravidian movement has never failed to remember national leaders and freedom fighters.

What is now being observed is India’s golden jubilee of Independence. We are in the golden-jubilee year of Independence.

## PDF 171 / printed p.170

It is in this golden-jubilee year of Independence that a nuclear-bomb test has been conducted in India on a scale that the whole world is discussing today.

I do not wish to enter into an inquiry about that. If a nuclear bomb has been exploded today, and when we know that Indian scientists who prepared it — particularly two scientists from Tamil Nadu — had a place in it, we all feel happy. (Loud applause)

I do not know what its repercussions will be. But arrows of opposition are coming today from many countries on all sides. They are being fired at us.

If, in the final stage, we have to withstand all of them, then we will have no need for the debate over whether conducting the nuclear test was right or wrong.

At that point, the question that will remain in our minds will be: India, or the other foreign countries?

Whether what was done was right or wrong may be discussed now. If tomorrow India faces danger from other countries, I wish to remind you here that India will forget all political animosities and stand as one — that we will stand as one. (Loud applause)

Because this function is for Thiru. Vi. Ka. and Professor Kalki — people devoted to this country, patriots and

## PDF 172 / printed p.171

devotees and devotees of Tamil, people who worked for today’s society, people who laboured for world unity, and who wrote, spoke and rendered service for it — I consider it entirely appropriate to say this at this moment.

The name and fame of Thiru. Vi. Ka., and the name and fame of Professor Kalki, are not things that can endure merely through this function we conduct today. These are names that stand beyond all such things — names that have risen so high that they continue to shape us even today. Let us again and again praise the two great men to whom those names belong! Let us bow to them!! Let us resolve to walk in their path.
