# Hearts Must Not Become Machines! — English translation

Speaking at the University of Chicago, Karunanidhi thanks Lloyd and Susanne Rudolph, the faculty and students, and places his movement in a long Tamil tradition of thinking about public ethics and politics.

He describes the DMK's democratic rise as the result of political awareness among ordinary people. Periyar's Self-Respect movement, he says, opened people's eyes, while Anna's political work clarified democratic consciousness. The ballot in every citizen's hand is therefore the true pivot of government.

The speech lists social-welfare measures pursued in Tamil Nadu: drinking-water schemes, rehabilitation of beggars, replacement of huts with housing, house-site rights for agricultural labourers, land pattas for the landless, and support for Tamil language and culture. These achievements are not presented as an endpoint; public work remains unfinished while poverty and suffering remain.

Karunanidhi then argues for State autonomy within a strong Indian Union: powers concentrated at the Centre should be distributed so States can grow in strength and prosperity without weakening the Union.

He ends with the image that gives the speech its title. Industrial and technological progress may make the world increasingly mechanised, but human hearts must not become machines. Democracy must continue to be guided by compassion, empathy and human feeling.

## Separate source closing note

Special address delivered at a University of Chicago seminar on 2 November 1971.
