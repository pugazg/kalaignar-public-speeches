## PDF 50 / printed p.49

### Indian Evangelical Church Function

Our Bishop has taken up, at intervals, all the time in which I, People’s Leader Moopanar and the others were supposed to speak, delivering fine addresses and filling all of us with inward joy. Through his worthy and excellent effort, this fivefold great celebration is taking place here with enthusiasm and in a splendid manner.

While taking part in this function and speaking, the Bishop, with the feeling that he had somehow to reveal what lay in his heart, kept expressing it from time to time. In one way, that was a source of happiness to me, to Moopanar, to Peter Alphonse and to the others. Whatever else may be said, I cannot refrain from appreciating the way Kumari Ananthan spoke—searching for words in a manner that established that he is indeed a man of literary wealth, and demonstrating that he has a skilled “hand” in inserting them into the people’s forum as well.

It is arguments of this kind—debates of this kind, debates that ought to be conducted in a civilised manner—

## PDF 51 / printed p.50

that politics needs; I am one who very much desires that. In that sense, even though so many people are here, our man of literary wealth Kumari Ananthan remained firm that the policy he holds must be established. There is a `கை` (“kai”, hand/skill) even inside `கொள்கை` (“kolgai”, policy)! (loud laughter) I appreciate the firmness with which he sought to establish it.

For a principle—for the principle he held—Lord Jesus dared to give even his life. But in the country today, many people are ready to give up the principle itself in order to keep themselves alive. (loud laughter) We are holding this church celebration in remembrance of Jesus. As all those who spoke before me pointed out, as a sign that people of good heart at this good celebration will do whatever good deed ought to be done, houses for the poor to live in are being provided, bicycles needed by them are being provided, and dhotis and saris are being given free to the needy. I offer my heartfelt appreciation for joining such fine acts to this celebration.

If education has today become an area of great development in this subcontinent, I will never forget that the Christian community is a community that has rendered great service in the field of education.

Even amid British domination—and also in a sphere beyond that domination—

## PDF 52 / printed p.51

in the spiritual field, in the religious field, and beyond sectarian religious arguments, Christian people of distinction have dedicated themselves well to the conviction that knowledge must grow throughout the Indian land and that they must render whatever valuable service they can for that growth of knowledge.

As far as Tamil Nadu is concerned, the history of Tamil Nadu records that many Christian elders came forward to foster the Tamil language. As a sign that we must show gratitude to such Christian elders—to those Christian scholars who fostered Tamil—when the World Tamil Conference was held in Tamil Nadu, during the period when our leader Perarignar Anna was Chief Minister, he honoured Christian religious elders and scholars such as Caldwell, G.U. Pope and Veeramamunivar by having statues erected for them on the Chennai seafront, on what is today Kamarajar Salai. All of you know this very well.

There are religions called Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism and so on. Religion must unite people through love. That is religion. Something that divides people through conflict is not religion; it is religious fanaticism. None of us should give room to that fanaticism. We who are seated on this stage, whatever political parties we may belong to, stand beyond those affiliations in having resolved that religious fanaticism must not be allowed to enter Tamil Nadu—indeed, the sacred land of India itself—and that if it does enter,

## PDF 53 / printed p.52

we must remove that fanaticism without fail.

Our desire is that all of you should imprint that pledge in your hearts.

Today, at this fine and pleasant celebration of this good Church—at this stirring celebration where many thousands of people have gathered, and at this moment when people have come even from many countries to lend distinction to it—the vow that all of us must take is: **Let us build religious harmony; let us safeguard humaneness.** This is the pledge we must take at this great celebration conducted by the Christian community that follows the path of Jesus, praises the glory of Jesus, praises his grace, praises his love, and cherishes his memory. Saying this, I take leave of you at this point.
