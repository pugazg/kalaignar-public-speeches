### PDF page 6

#### Karunanidhi in the Field!

Beloved Chairman! Comrades! Elders! Friends of the opposition parties who are standing aside and listening to the speech through the loudspeaker! My greetings to all of you, with thanks.

This meeting, convened here today under the auspices of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, has been called in connection with the coming general election: to support Comrade **Albert Jesudhas**, who is contesting the Madras Legislative Assembly from the North Madras Washermenpet constituency after signing an agreement affirming the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam's life-blood question—the separation of Dravida Nadu, the fundamental demand that “Dravida Nadu belongs to the Dravidians”—and thereby receiving the official support and cooperation of our Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam; and, likewise, to support Comrade **James Bond**, who, after signing the same agreement and receiving our support, is contesting for Parliament, the People's House in Delhi, from the constituency covering Washermenpet, Ponneri Taluk, Sriperumbudur, Tiruvallur, Saidapet and other areas—

—and to ask every one of you to cast your vote for them in the coming election, so that through this the cry, the heart-song, “Dravida Nadu for the Dravidians,” may resound in both the Legislative Assembly and the People's House.

Many parties and independents are contesting the election. Among them, in particular, is the Congress Party, which today occupies the seat of government.

Congress has been governing the country for more than four years. It is contesting the election again in order to sit once more on the seat of power.

Many comrades who spoke before me described what the present rulers, the Congress government, have done in these four years. What have they achieved? What good have they done for the people? They set out all the cruelties, horrifying scenes, hunger, starvation and famine that have been heaped upon the people.

Anna, the General Secretary of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, at the recently held State Conference, clearly explained the Kazhagam's—

—decision, position and programme concerning the election.

With regard to this election, he explained the aim of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam: from the time the present rulers, the present Congress government, climbed onto the seat of power until today, throughout these four years, they have brought decline upon the country. How many kinds of harm have surrounded the country! He explained how many more forms of ruin and tangled problems would surround the country if the same Congress came to power again and again, and if the grip of government once more remained trapped in its hands.

Therefore, Congress must be defeated; there must not again be the miserable condition of suffering under its disorderly rule—nor is there any need for it. This must be prevented so that the country may prosper and its people may live well.

The country knows well the cruelties committed by this Congress in the past. Many have spoken about them and continue to do so. I will tell you some things that some may have forgotten to mention, that you may have missed reading in the newspapers, or that others were unable to say. Listen.

Five years ago, T. K. Pattammal sang of dancing and singing because joyous freedom had arrived, while Baby Kamala danced in expressive gesture. From the beginning of that “joyous freedom” until today, what have these rulers done? What have they actually achieved? They have flung repression upon repression. I wish to explain this particularly, with figures.

It was during these five years of rule by the apostles of nonviolence that firearms were used **1,982** times. Not only that: **3,784** people were shot dead; more than ten thousand were seriously wounded. More than about fifty thousand were imprisoned, as preventive detainees and political prisoners. And it did not stop there. Of the political prisoners confined in jail, **eighty-two** were shot dead within the prison bars themselves.

Where did all this happen? Who are these arrogant men who, within five years, used firearms one thousand nine hundred and eighty-two times? Hitler? No, no! Mussolini? Certainly not! Then who? The Tsar? No. They are all the heirs of nonviolence, Gandhi's foremost disciples, now enthroned on the seat of government—

—the Congressmen themselves. Within just a few years—not once, not twice, but more than a thousand firings; as a result, more than ten thousand people wounded; heaps of more than three thousand corpses. A horrific sight, a pitiable condition, never before seen or heard anywhere; the gift bestowed by the **sons of Bharata who came to rule**; everywhere famine, hunger, disease, armies of the starving—cruelty! Cruelty!!

There are so many problems in the country that should draw the attention of those who came to rule. The suffering of the worker. The starving multitudes crying “Hunger! Hunger!”; the anguish of the poor weaver who, without yarn and therefore without work, without food to eat and without a way to live, wastes away, despairs of life and hangs himself; the painful news of a mother unable to feed the children she has borne; the agonizing reports of parents, driven by hunger, throwing their own little children into a desolate well and then taking their own lives—these matters struck against the Legislative Assembly and the Constituent Assembly. Did the rulers listen? No—the politicians, the Congressmen governing the country, did not.
