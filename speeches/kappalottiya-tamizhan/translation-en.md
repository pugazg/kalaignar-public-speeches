## The Tamil Who Sailed Ships

Today marks the centenary of V. O. Chidambaranar, the freedom fighter who served the country and bore the wounds of the independence struggle. Across Tamil Nadu the day is being observed with special feeling.

Prime Minister Indira Gandhi has accepted our invitation and come to Tamil Nadu, participating in events on the soil where this great man was born. Her presence honours the history of Tamil sacrifice.

Here in Tuticorin, and in cities, district centres, villages, streets and homes across the State, people are celebrating his centenary with enthusiasm.

## PDF 200 / printed p.199

We unveil his statue not merely to pay respect to his sacrifice but because that sacrifice must continue to guide us.

V. O. C. showed that a Tamil could challenge British power through shipping and enterprise. His speeches and writings became storms of resistance. For this he was sentenced to two life terms—forty years in all—later reduced on appeal. In prison he was isolated, forced to labour at the oil press and to break stones.

His memory remains joined to the freedom struggle.

## PDF 201 / printed p.200

The statue stands in the port area of Tuticorin, a place that may soon be transformed by new buildings and industries.

I thank Prime Minister Indira Gandhi for coming to open it.

I also use the occasion to place before her a long-standing Tamil Nadu demand: rapid implementation of the Sethusamudram project, which can expand industry and commerce in Tamil Nadu and India.

## PDF 202 / printed p.201

In the Prime Minister's presence I also announce that the part of Chennai long called “George Town” will be renamed “Chidambaranar Nagar,” fulfilling a demand that has existed since 1947.

Names matter when they preserve ideals and memory.

V. O. C.'s initials themselves can be read symbolically: a lawyer, one who argued for rights, and one who went to prison for defending those rights.

## PDF 203 / printed p.202

Thus the name V. O. C. signifies more than V. O. Chidambaranar alone: it recalls a lawyer who argued for rights and was prepared to go to prison for them.

With the affectionate greetings of all gathered here, I invite Prime Minister Indira Gandhi to unveil his statue.
