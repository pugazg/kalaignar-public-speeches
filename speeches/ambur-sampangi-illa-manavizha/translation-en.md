### Speech at the Ambur Sampangi Family Wedding

I am happy to take part in this wedding function being held at the home of my dear friend Sampangi of Ambur. At this life-union ceremony of Sangeetha, Sampangi’s granddaughter and the daughter of our younger brother Manoharan, and Balaji, the son of Radhakrishnan, I convey my heartfelt wishes that the two of them may live for many years, render much service to the country and shine in a way that demonstrates the distinction of this family.

Our party treasurer Arcot Veerasamy pointed out here that I have taken part in all the wedding functions held in this family. I myself presided over Manoharan’s wedding and conducted that wedding function then. Today too, I myself am presiding over and conducting the wedding of Manoharan’s daughter. If things continue like this,

## PDF 174 / printed p.173

I wish to point out that I have such attachment and affection for this family that I may even take part in the weddings of Manoharan’s grandson and granddaughter.

I also took part in the wedding of Selvi, the sister of both Manoharan and Anandan, and blessed the couple. I took part in the wedding of Sampangi’s other son, our younger brother Raju, and blessed that couple as well. All these are evidence that my participation in functions held in this family is something natural.

When I think of Ambur, I cannot forget Sampangi and Iramu. Around 1950, nearly forty-eight years ago, I met them in Chennai. Before that I did not know that this was Sampangi or that this was Ramu. At the time I was writing screenplay and dialogue for the films *Manamagal* and *Parasakthi*.

Kalaivanar N.S.K. told me that if I wrote dialogue from home I would face disturbances. He sent his secretary, a man named Chellamuthu, with me and arranged a room in a hotel called Select Hotel in the North Chennai area of Chennai city, asking me to sit there in solitude and write the dialogue. Similarly, for *Parasakthi*, produced by Perumal, who was born in “Pootu Thaakku” in this same North Arcot district,

## PDF 175 / printed p.174

I was writing the dialogue for some scenes there. At that time, two young men staying in the room opposite mine sent word through a boy working at the hotel that they wished to meet me. The boy came to me and said, “Two people from somewhere in North Arcot are staying here. They want to meet you.” I said, “Ask them to come.”

Then both of them came and introduced themselves: “I am Sampangi; this is Iramu.” They explained to me their attachment to the Dravidian movement. You can all directly see how the meeting that took place that day at Select Hotel continued until Sampangi and Iramu passed away, and how it has continued even after them.

As friend Mullai Vadivelu illustrated, and as our younger brother Durai Murugan and others pointed out here, I know very, very well how much Sampangi and Ramu worked in this region to develop and strengthen the Dravidian movement and this political movement called the DMK. That is why, whenever Sampangi or Ramu invited me to any function connected with Ambur, I never hesitated to come. You all know that I even came to the opening of the hotel he built here,

## PDF 176 / printed p.175

and that I did so. His stubborn principle, his conviction, was that no function in this house should take place without me. I have therefore continued to come to the functions of this family, both then and now, in the belief that fulfilling that wish is my duty.

Our younger brother Anandan has taken on the responsibility of city secretary and is working with great attachment to the party. Durai Murugan said here that I have many friends in North Arcot district, comparing many districts, speaking of my party connections with many districts, and saying that beyond all of them I have acquired more friends here.

It is in this district that I gained more friends; and it is in this same district that I lost some friends in between and then got them back. I believe you will all understand whom I mean even without my naming them. What does that show? It points to the proverb, “Water struck by water does not separate.” In a district bound to me by such emotional and relational friendship, I feel limitless happiness and pride whenever I see all of you. I feel that pride today as well.

## PDF 177 / printed p.176

When the district party secretary, our younger brother Sakhi, spoke here, and when the party treasurer spoke, they said that this is not merely a wedding function: you have gathered here in such numbers that it looks like a district conference. Since some people’s conferences are only as large as this wedding, we are now conducting weddings themselves as conferences. Whether the DMK wins or loses the opportunity for victory, the fact that you have gathered here is sufficient proof that it is a force no one can bring down.

They spoke here of all the achievements of the party government in this district and this constituency. Friend Abdul Latheef in particular repeated all the schemes I announced in the Legislative Assembly, perhaps fearing that I might forget them or that what I said in the Assembly might not be fulfilled; he has reminded me of them again with all of you as witnesses. There is no need to remind me. Because Durai Murugan is the minister responsible for that department, I assure Abdul Latheef and all of you once again that our younger brother Durai Murugan will certainly have all of them implemented. If he does not, I will not simply leave it as a matter between Abdul Latheef and Durai Murugan. I too will take responsibility for it; I assure him firmly of that.

## PDF 178 / printed p.177

You know that this movement called the DMK was not first launched as a political movement. We were all trained in that camp called the Dravidar Kazhagam alongside Thanthai Periyar. Perarignar Anna, I, the party general secretary Professor, and many of the party’s field commanders seated here — all of us were people who, with Periyar’s blessings, with his advice and accepting his reprimands, dedicated ourselves to his movement and carried out social work in the Dravidar Kazhagam.

In our youth we committed ourselves to a great movement that worked for religious harmony in Tamil Nadu, for a condition in which caste difference should not exist, and for amity in society. The propaganda that movement carried out, the principles it explained, and the ideals it placed before the public forum — if all these were to be fulfilled, the thought arose that it was not enough merely to remain a social movement and that the DMK must function politically. At the DMK state conference held in Tiruchirappalli in 1956, party comrades and members of the public who had gathered there voted in a way that pressed Arignar Anna to enter elections. The public forum gave that order then; the volunteers of the democratic mass movement called the DMK gave that order.

## PDF 179 / printed p.178

Accepting that order, in 1957 the DMK entered the electoral field under the leadership of Perarignar Anna. At that time fifteen of us entered the Legislative Assembly under Anna’s leadership. In the next election there were fifty. Then, in the election held in 1967, a government itself was formed under Anna’s leadership. Anna — we can only call it our misfortune — parted from us within two years; after that we took on the responsibilities he had left behind and remained in governmental office for some time.

If, after having held office three times, the DMK is in office today for the fourth time, it is because, whatever trials may come to the party — whether in the form of repression, in the form of MISA, in any form at all, or even in the form of electoral defeat — there are still lakhs upon lakhs of brothers and sisters who bear them, disregard them and hold aloft the flag of this party. (Loud applause) They will always be there. The reason for this is the labour of people like Sampangi and Ramu, the party’s brave fighters in North Arcot district, and the field commanders throughout Tamil Nadu — the work rendered by the DMK brothers and sisters whom they created and who still remain everywhere today. That is the reason for all this.

## PDF 180 / printed p.179

There is no need to list all the achievements this government is carrying out. You know them very well. When we speak of these things through advertisements, it is not for you, nor for the general public. It is to show these achievements to those who are still dancing about saying the DMK government must be brought down and sent home, jumping about and engaging in conspiratorial work toward that end. It is with that purpose that we show the achievements of the DMK government and the extent to which this government is connected with the people.

You may think that our younger brother Durai Murugan came with me from Chennai to this wedding because he travelled here by train from Chennai. But the place Durai Murugan had been for the last three days was Thanjavur district. Why? Durai Murugan and the Local Administration Minister, our younger brother Ko. Si. Mani, had gone there and returned after inspecting and encouraging the work being carried out in Thanjavur district. If that work is being carried out there, it is not work undertaken only for Thanjavur district. It is work for the whole of Tamil Nadu. Last year, work costing twelve crore rupees was carried out. Continuing and further expanding it, work costing twenty crore rupees is being carried out in Thanjavur district this year.

## PDF 181 / printed p.180

You may have read what newspapers published in this area, such as *The Hindu* and *The Express*, wrote about that work. They described it as work not carried out even in the time of the Chola kings. They described it as work never before spoken of in history.

The Cauvery rises and flows from Karnataka into Tamil Nadu, spreading through Salem, Mettur, Tiruchirappalli and Thanjavur to such an extent that this whole region is called the Cauvery belt, sustaining farmers in Thanjavur, Tiruchirappalli and a part of South Arcot district. Yet its branch rivers and canals had remained without being properly restored for many years, and there were many obstructions here and there that prevented the incoming water from reaching the fields fully. Considering all this, efforts were undertaken last year to deepen those canals and small rivers. As a result, not only did the fields benefit last year; because a path was created for the floodwater to flow away, no one can forget that Thanjavur district itself was saved.

Continuing that work, this year work costing twenty crore rupees is proceeding very rapidly in Thanjavur district to classify those canals over many kilometres. The newspapers

## PDF 182 / printed p.181

have praised it. Once that work is completed, the incoming water will not be wasted but will reach the fields, enabling Thanjavur district farmers to obtain a greater yield. By enabling Tamil Nadu’s food needs to be met, whether it is North Arcot, Salem, Coimbatore or any other district, this work is being done today so that a prosperous Thanjavur district may benefit the people of every district.

Thus a government that thinks about and acts upon the work that must be done for the people every day is functioning here today. Some people are in a hurry; some are restless; some have the thought of Gandhari from the Bharata story. Because of that they are saying today that this government must be removed.

Whenever journalists meet me, they ask me about it. Wherever they meet me — when I go to a function and climb onto the stage, when I get off an aircraft in which I have travelled, when I board a train, when I get into a car or get out of a car — journalists surround me and keep asking, “What, sir, they say the government is going to be dismissed; what do you say?” I do not now go on listing all the reasons why it should not be dismissed. I simply say that I am not worried about it.

## PDF 183 / printed p.182

Governments come and go. As Anna said, office is like the towel placed over one’s upper body. Even if it falls, a person can still walk about. But principle is like the dhoti tied around one’s waist. A person cannot walk about after losing it.

So we live for principle; ours is a movement that exists for principle. Our thought is that it is enough if this movement remains. That is our goal. No one will ever separate themselves from that ideal or from the DMK. One of the great pillars of this firm movement in this region was my dear friend Sampangi. With the hope that the family lamps lit today in Sampangi’s home will blaze brightly and help spread the greatness of this family further and further, I bless the bride and groom.
