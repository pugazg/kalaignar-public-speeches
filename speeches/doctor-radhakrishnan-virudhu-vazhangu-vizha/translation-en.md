# Dr Radhakrishnan Award Presentation Function

Karunanidhi speaks at Valluvar Kottam on Teachers' Day and says he feels renewed enthusiasm at seeing the venue restored to dignity after years in which, in his view, it had been misused.

He explains why the State's former “Good Teacher Award” was renamed the **Dr Radhakrishnan Award**. Calling only a few recipients “good teachers,” he argues, could imply that the rest were not good teachers. Since the award is presented on Dr Radhakrishnan's birthday, the new name is both more appropriate and less hurtful to the teaching community. He expresses irritation that some newspapers and television reports still used the old name.

That discussion leads to a broader point about public usage and names. Karunanidhi recalls renaming Mount Road as Anna Salai and says even people in his own movement continued to use the old name. He similarly urges people to use “Chennai” rather than “Madras,” noting that foreign visitors themselves had already begun saying “Chennai.”

Dr Radhakrishnan is presented as an example of a teacher who rose through knowledge and ability to become Vice-President and then President of India. Karunanidhi expands the familiar image of teachers as ladders: they are not only ladders that help others rise, but can themselves become the finials of the national tower.

He points out that seven members of the ministry had teaching backgrounds and says the government is committed to protecting teachers' rights. In particular, he assures teachers that any future changes in the powers of village panchayats, unions or district panchayats will not take away rights already secured by teachers.

The speech emphasises teachers' role in shaping the future of students and society. Karunanidhi says teachers should receive the same kinds of rights and benefits as government employees and promises that teachers will not be forgotten when future pay-commission recommendations are implemented.

He cites literacy figures given in the speech — 52.11 per cent for India and 63.72 per cent for Tamil Nadu — but says even the higher Tamil Nadu figure is not enough, especially if population growth erodes gains. Education, he says, is a tool for social change, and he invokes a Kural on knowledge as an impregnable defence.

Karunanidhi links expanded educational opportunity to the history of the South Indian Liberal Federation/Justice movement and the Dravidian movement. He quotes the idea that India's future is decided in classrooms, attributes to Periyar the view that people are shaped by teachers, and recalls Anna's warning that if teachers suffer today, the country will suffer tomorrow.

A large section details teacher recruitment and appointments. The speech states that 13,140 intermediate teachers were appointed in one phase and 10,384 more were being appointed through employment-exchange seniority, contrasting this with 8,450 appointments in the preceding five-year period. It further gives figures for graduate, postgraduate and head-teacher appointments and argues that the new process reduced scope for corruption.

Karunanidhi also discusses transfers, particularly the hardship faced by women teachers sent far from their home districts, and recalls a special order allowing affected women teachers to return to their home areas.

He urges teachers themselves to remain students throughout their careers: a teacher who will teach a lesson the next morning should study it the previous night. He links this to the government's incentive increments for teachers who pursue higher education and notes the extension of vehicle-loan facilities to private-school teachers.

The closing section refers to prizes for top students in Plus Two and SSLC examinations and to government support for first-generation graduates entering professional education. Karunanidhi ends by congratulating the teachers receiving the Dr Radhakrishnan Award and the students receiving prizes and assistance.
