## PDF 89 / printed p.88

### Puthandu Isaivizha

At this function, S. P. Balasubrahmanyam sang the poet Vairamuthu's words, “Iniya maalai pozhuthu”—“a pleasant evening hour.” We are all experiencing in this hall the delight of that pleasant evening hour. Through Century Productions this function has been arranged to honour music maestros and senior figures of the world of art. I convey my thanks, respects and New Year greetings to those who have given me the opportunity to take part in it.

My New Year greetings are not only to them, but to the people of Tamil Nadu—and especially to all of you who are seated here, filling this hall to overflowing.

Something that is a matter of great wonder is taking place here as an event. It is natural that it is difficult for many people to hold a single common opinion. Two doctors will not hold the same opinion. Two poets will not hold the same opinion.

## PDF 90 / printed p.89

Beyond all that, if two music vidwans hold the same opinion, that is a great wonder of the world. But on this stage many musicians and music masters are seated together, and when I think that through this function they have bid farewell to the situation that used to prevail, I am greatly delighted.

Brother Viswanathan, one of the Mellisai Mannargal; Isaignani Ilaiyaraaja; Ramamoorthy, the other of the Mellisai Mannargal; and music directors—those who once shone with fame among music directors—are all seated here. Though today they may be in their later years, the songs they composed move about Tamil Nadu with youthfulness, and those who have had the pleasure of seeing and hearing that sight are all seated here.

Brother Viswanathan mentioned something at one function. He has also made sure to mention it in an article.

After Arignar Anna's death, when I was serving as Chief Minister before 1975, we felt that a Tamil Thai Vazhthu was needed to be sung at our government functions. We sought one out and chose the song by Manonmaniam Sundaranar beginning, “Neerarum kadaludutha nilamadanthaik kezhilozhugum — seerarum vadanamena thigazh Bharatha kandamathil,” and gave it to Viswanathan, asking him to set it to music. He composed twenty-six different pann—

## PDF 91 / printed p.90

that is, tunes—for it. Finally, this particular pann was selected as the best, and those who sang it were T. M. Soundararajan, seated here and forever reminding us of the late Thyagaraja Bhagavathar, and Susheela.

> **Source note:** The frozen Tamil uses the difficult phrase `தேர்வுத் தந்து` on PDF 90; E2 retains the conservative sense “selected … and gave it” without altering the frozen Tamil layer.

Even after their time, even after the time of Viswanathan and Ramamoorthy who set the tune, and even after my own time—the one who wished that song to become the Tamil Thai Vazhthu—whenever that song resounds in any age, through that song we will have attained immortality and will continue to sound in the hearts of the people of Tamil Nadu. Viswanathan and Ramamoorthy, who created such a wonderful pann, have taken an important responsibility in this function and are conducting this pleasant event honouring many artists.

It is especially significant that Naushad has been invited here. I am taking part in a function in which Naushad is participating.

When our Yesudas was about to sing a Hindi song here, our musicians unnecessarily asked my forgiveness beforehand. That apology was not necessary. I am an opponent only of the imposition of Hindi (loud applause), not of Hindi music.

## PDF 92 / printed p.91

Naushad is someone who greatly captivated the people of Tamil Nadu through Hindi music. Through those musical songs, Tamil Nadu has kept him in its heart without ever forgetting him; such a great person is taking part in this function.

A souvenir was released here. In your presence I presented that souvenir to Naushad and brother Ilaiyaraaja. They first gave the souvenir to me. I turned through a page or two. Many pages of brother Viswanathan's life story are included in it. By reading one page I understood just how clever brother Viswanathan was.

Viswanathan was three years old then. At that age his father died. In the grief of his father's death, his mother decided that she and her son Viswanathan could fall into a pond in Pudukkottai and die—that they could commit suicide. She carried Viswanathan to the pond and, when she tried to throw him in, Viswanathan refused and told his mother, “If you throw me in, you will go away. So you jump in first; then I will jump.” He has written that this is what he said. It is in his life story. I am not the one saying it.

Fortunately, if his mother had not listened to that talk and had thrown Viswanathan into the pond that day, today we would not have had a Mellisai Mannar—

## PDF 93 / printed p.92

we would not have had him. (applause) When I think that, as though for the very purpose of rendering musical service to all of us, brother Viswanathan refused that day and told his mother, “I will not fall into the pond,” I feel doubly happy. He and many musicians associated with him have come together on this stage and are continuing to give us a wonderful musical programme on this pleasant evening.

I too have taken part in this musical programme. They have remembered all those people and offered each of them their appreciation and affection. I wish to make clear here that I too am one who stands in the place of offering love and respect to all of them. Generally in the world, this is an age in which people forget their predecessors, those who developed our arts before us, those who shaped all of us. In such an age, and especially in the world of art, I offer my appreciation and good wishes to Visu and the musicians with him for having the spirit to remember all those predecessors and honour them. I also express my happiness at having received the opportunity to meet all of you at this pleasant time, and with this I take leave of you.
