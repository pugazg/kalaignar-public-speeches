### Muthamizh Peravai Function

Today this Muthamizh Peravai is conducting its 23rd anniversary function. For 23 years I too have been taking part in the Peravai's opening functions and receiving the opportunity to inaugurate them. But in these 23 years, how many great masters I have seen in this hall, and the sight of all of them seated in the front row still playing before my mind's eye—these are things that cannot be forgotten, things that cannot be left unspoken here.

One of the elders who used to sit in the front row to encourage this Muthamizh Peravai was Vazhuvur Ramaiah Pillai. Among the treasures we have lost during these 23 years is Vazhuvur Ramaiah Pillai. One dear to me who took determined efforts to start this Peravai was Kulithalai Pichaiyappa Pillai. Chidambaram C. S. Jayaraman and Thiruveezhimizhalaiyar were among those who continuously attended these functions and worked to see that they were held.

## PDF 81 / printed p.80

I have seen all of them seated here in this front row—Madurai Somu, Sirkazhi and the others. Even though today that front row is seen without all those who once occupied it, their successors, the masters and musicians trained by them, are continuing this artistic work from where they left it. Seeing that distinction, I feel a sense of fulfilment even amid sorrow.

During these 23 years, the music programmes, dance programmes and many other artistic events conducted by this Peravai at this Rasika Ranjani Sabha have, I must say, given new encouragement and enthusiasm to the field of music, particularly to Tamil music.

At this function of such a Peravai, being held today under the chairmanship of our beloved Moopanar, Pandanallur P. Mahalingam Pillai has received the Rajarathna Award; Solomon Pappiah, the Iyarselvam Award; Charumathi Ramachandran, the Isai Selvam Award; M. S. Ramasamy, the Nattiya Selvam Award; Thiruppanandal S. Balasubramaniam, the Nadaswara Selvam Award; and K. P. Singaram Pillai, the Mridanga Selvam Award.

## PDF 82 / printed p.81

You know very well how deserving each one of them is of these awards. Mahalingam Pillai is a nadaswaram maestro and is most fitting to receive the award given in memory of the late nadaswaram master T. N. Rajarathinam Pillai. He received vocal training from the great Pandanallur Bharatanatyam teacher Meenakshi Sundaram Pillai. He has developed a style of his own in this art and has been offering nadaswaram feasts in many music halls. Today the Rajarathna Award has been presented to him, like placing another crown upon his fame.

Before I expressed in this Muthamizh Peravai the idea that such an award should be given, in the year Rajarathinam Pillai passed away, a function unveiling his portrait was held in this very hall under the chairmanship of C. Subramaniam. At that time C. Subramaniam was one of the ministers of Tamil Nadu. I too took part in that function and spoke, praising the fame of the late Rajarathinam. At that time I placed this request before C. S.: that such a title, a Rajarathna Award, should be given to good nadaswaram vidwans. But for some reason it was not fulfilled. It seems it had to wait until I myself came, announced it, and it was to be

## PDF 83 / printed p.82

awarded. (applause) Therefore, after I myself became Chief Minister, I announced the Rajarathna Award at a Muthamizh Peravai function, and for many years now that award has been given to many vidwans. In that line, today Mahalingam Pillai has received the award. I congratulate him; I commend him.

The recipient of the Iyarselvam Award is Solomon Pappiah. This award should perhaps have been given to him much earlier. On behalf of this Peravai I express my regret to him for the delay, because there can hardly be anyone in Tamil Nadu who does not know Solomon Pappiah.

I want to tell you one thing. I get up at 5:30 in the morning, finish my morning walk—the thing they call “walking”—by 6:30, return home, and sit down to write the Udanpirappu letter for the Murasoli newspaper. At that time the Tirukkural is broadcast on Sun TV. Some writers say that when they are writing there must not be any sound beside them. I am not like that. Whatever sound may be heard, once I begin writing I continue without worrying about it. But some sounds become sounds that encourage my writing. In that way, every day, the sound of Solomon Pappiah's Tirukkural, while I am writing, gives encouragement to my writing. (applause) I am not saying this merely in order to honour him in this gathering

## PDF 84 / printed p.83

as a courtesy. My wife and members of my household are here. Solomon Pappiah may ask them and find out whether I have truly said this before or whether I am saying it merely out of courtesy.

To that extent, every day it is the Tirukkural. Many people have written commentaries on the Tirukkural. I too have written a commentary on all 1,330 Kural couplets. I have drawn Kural Oviyam. For two hundred or three hundred Kural couplets I have shaped little stories and written Kural Oviyam so that the Kural may be easily understood. But beyond all that, every day he presents the Tirukkural in a way that stays in the mind and can be understood even by small children. There may be a difference of opinion between him and me regarding the word-by-word meaning he gives. It is a difference, not a contradiction. A difference is one thing; a contradiction is another. Contradiction is like oil and water. Difference is like milk and water. Milk and water may be different, but when poured together they mingle as one. I will not speak in “Madras” slang. (loud laughter) In the same way, however much you try to mix oil and water, they will remain separate. Thus, even where there is some difference without contradiction, how sweetly he recites the Kural, how sweetly he gives its explanation—I keep writing while marvelling at it again and again.

## PDF 85 / printed p.84

He continues to give me such encouragement. Even after he finishes all 1,330 Kural couplets, he should begin again and continue them in sequence—for my writing at least, he should continue. (applause) That is my request.

Mrs. Charumathi Ramachandran has received the Isai Selvam Award. She is one of the finest students of the late music maestro M. L. Vasanthakumari. Thiruppanandal Balasubramanian, who receives the Nadaswara Selvam Award, is the son of Srinivasan, a tavil vidwan who served as an asthana artist of the Thiruppanandal Kasi Mutt. He has performed music programmes in countries such as Singapore and Malaysia. He now serves as an asthana vidwan of the Mylapore Kapaleeswarar Temple. My congratulations to him as well.

M. S. Ramasamy Pillai, who receives the Nattiya Selvam Award, lived and trained in the gurukula tradition under Vazhuvur Ramaiah Pillai and became renowned as an accomplished Bharatanatyam artist. Mrs. E. V. Saroja, Padma Subrahmanyam, Hema Malini and others have danced to his nattuvangam. He has worked as Bharatanatyam teacher for the films *Madurai Veeran*, *Bhagyavathi*, *Konjum Salangai*, *Manam Oru Kurangu* and *Thuli Visham*. I commend him too.

Kumbakonam Singaram Pillai, who receives the Mridanga Selvam Award, trained under the mridangam maestro Kudanthai

## PDF 86 / printed p.85

Azhaganambia Pillai in the gurukula tradition and learned the mridangam. He became famous for playing mridangam with tambura sruti for Thiruvavaduthurai Rajarathinam Pillai and Thiruvenkadu T. P. Subramania Pillai. Since every one of these recipients is worthy of the award, I am repeatedly duty-bound to congratulate and commend them.

At this function our friend Deccan Murthy has contributed one lakh rupees to the building fund. This is not a wealthy Peravai. It is an ordinary, modest Peravai. For 23 years, every year when I come, they have been telling me that a building is needed. I too have been saying, “We shall see, we shall see. You undertake the effort. I will do what I can and stand by you.” For 23 years they did not engage in any such effort. Somehow this year they have taken up the effort and have asked the government to allot a site for this Peravai. I have assured them that the necessary action will be taken. (applause)

That site will be allotted soon. (loud applause) To carry out the work of raising a building on it, our Deccan has given one lakh rupees here. I too thought—our Moopanar, who is to speak next, in competition with him (loud laughter)—Moopanar says that whatever

## PDF 87 / printed p.86

is needed, announce it on my behalf) Moopanar says to announce however much is needed. It is election time. I do not wish to say too much. (loud applause) I am happy to tell you that he too is giving one lakh rupees. (loud applause)

Since both of them are giving, I state here that one lakh rupees will be given from the Chief Minister's Fund as well. (loud applause)

Moopanar said one more thing. He pointed out that this is the centenary year of Rajarathinam and said that it should be celebrated in a grand manner. The person with every qualification to serve as chairman of the committee conducting that celebration is Moopanar himself. (applause) Therefore, on behalf of all of you, I request that Moopanar serve as chairman of the Rajarathinam centenary celebration committee, take up its leadership, and work so that the celebration is conducted pleasantly, with vigour and distinction, bringing fresh strength to the world of music.

After Kundrakudi Adigalar, we had wondered anxiously what would become of the sweet Tamil he spoke. As a consolation to all of us, the younger Kundrakudi Adigalar came here from the Kundrakudi Adheenam and delivered an address, demonstrating that the Tamil of Kundrakudi would not diminish, with a

## PDF 88 / printed p.87

wonderful speech here. Indeed, his Tamil played and delighted us here to such an extent that one might say no musical programme was needed at all. (applause) I convey to him my commendation and good wishes, express my desire that the Muthamizh Peravai should continue to excel in its service, and with that I conclude.
