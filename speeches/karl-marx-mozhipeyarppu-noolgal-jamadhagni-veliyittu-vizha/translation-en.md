### Release Function for Karl Marx's Translated Works (Jamadhagni)

For approximately two and a half hours here at Kalaivanar Arangam, all of us have had the opportunity to exchange various messages. I begin my speech by also assuring you that I will soon grant all of you release from this hall.

The revolutionary poet Bharathidasan raised the question: “Everything we see was made by the worker—is poverty what he deserves to see? Is patience what he deserves to wear?” For a century, many thinkers throughout the world searched and searched for the path that would provide an answer to this question, and among them Karl Marx arose as a great thinker. The distinction of rendering this `Capital` in full, for the first time in India, into one of our Indian languages—and that too our own Tamil language—is a distinction that we all enjoy. Even though that great translator, Jamadhagni, is not with us at this auspicious moment,

## PDF 146 / printed p.145

Tamil society, the ideal-driven community of youth, and the working class are forever ready to offer him their appreciation and gratitude.

I believe Professor Dr. Jagadeesan, who spoke here, referred to the efforts undertaken by our Naganathan to publish this book.

If Thiruvalluvar were alive today, he said, he would have changed the Kural,

The help a son renders his father is this:  
to make people say, “What penance did his father perform to beget him?”

and sung instead about “the help a son-in-law renders his father-in-law.” I am duty-bound to tell Jagadeesan that if Thiruvalluvar were here, he certainly would not compose a venpa with such a metrical fault. (Applause)

Valluvar himself has sung it correctly:

The help a son renders his father is this:  
to make people say, “What penance did his father perform to beget him?”

When the word “son” occurs in the Kural, it does not refer only to a male child; it refers to a daughter as well. Many scholars who have written commentaries on the Kural have made us understand this.

Therefore, instead of taking only “son” in “The help a son renders his father is this: what penance did his father perform to beget him?”, if we take it as “The help a daughter renders her father is this: what penance did her father perform to beget her?”, and Dr. Jagadeesan asks, “How can that be? Was it not Naganathan who

## PDF 147 / printed p.146

published this? How can the daughter receive this distinction?”, one truth was explained here.

It was explained that the marriage held here in 1975 was a love marriage. If this bridegroom had not been found for this daughter, this book would not have appeared today. So the daughter encouraged that son, encouraged that bridegroom, and as a result this book called `Capital` is shining in our hands today. Since what Valluvar sang applies to the daughter as well, it applies to Shanthi too, and I congratulate Shanthi. Naganathan will not be upset because I congratulate Shanthi, for I know Naganathan's nature very well.

I am not someone who came to know him only one day long ago. Every morning when I go walking at Anna Arivalayam, Naganathan accompanies me. Whether anyone else comes or not, Naganathan never fails to come. It was during such walks that Naganathan explained this book to me many times. He told me that there was hesitation and an obstacle: it could not be printed, and even if printed, it could not be released. Accordingly, I was told that the application he submitted was lying pending in the department of our Tamilkudimagan. When I learned that it was lying pending, I myself asked Tamilkudimagan

## PDF 148 / printed p.147

about it, cleared the blockage, and, to the extent of relieving everyone's longing, `Capital` has been released today.

Tamilkudimagan should not feel hurt and say, “Ah! Anna has told everyone the truth that it was lying pending in my department.” I am saying this here, with Tamilkudimagan present, precisely because nothing should lie pending in any minister's department. Good things should happen at once. Bad things, if necessary, may remain pending.

As soon as I told him, Tamilkudimagan acted immediately despite the intervening delay, made this recommendation, and arranged in the Budget for five lakh rupees to be granted by the government toward Naganathan's effort.

Everyone who spoke at this function repeatedly praised me for giving this financial assistance. Perhaps they praised me because they felt it was their duty to praise me; I do not know. I consider it unnecessary praise. They happily kept praising and congratulating me, saying, “He gave five lakh, he gave five lakh.”

I was only the person who provided medical care. It was these publishers who carried this fine child for ten months and brought it out. They are the ones who should be praised. I think you praised me as though praising the midwife who attended the delivery instead of praising the mother who bore the child.

## PDF 149 / printed p.148

By providing this funding, I have merely expressed my sense of duty that Tamil, Tamil literature, and new ideas of this kind that ought to circulate in Tamil Nadu should be given encouragement; nothing more.

The person who wrote the preface to this book called `Capital` at that time—the person who wrote the preface to `Capital` on 5 November 1886, the preface to the English edition—was Friedrich Engels.

He says:

“`Capital` (Das Capital) is called the Bible (Christian scripture) of the proletarian class on the continent of Europe. Not only in Germany and Switzerland; in France, Holland, Belgium and America—and even in Italy and Spain—the conclusions arrived at in this book are blossoming into the basic principles of the proletarian movement.”

Engels says this in the preface he wrote for the English edition in 1886.

Further, while speaking about that book `Capital`, Engels says:

“It is necessary to state what capital means from a Marxist point of view. In the ordinary view, everyone who possesses some kind of property is regarded as a capitalist or owner of capital.

On this basis, self-employed small traders—shopkeepers and small workshop owners—are all called capitalists.

## PDF 150 / printed p.149

It is true that they possess a small amount of property.

But they too engage in labour. Their property does not primarily function as capital that grows by exploiting others.

Likewise, money used in moneylending and kept locked away in a box does not become capital.

Ordinary interest-bearing loans are mainly borrowed for expenses that arise from time to time. None of these constitutes capital that exploits labour. Through banks and insurance companies, capitalists obtain a portion of capital. In short, capital means money employed in exploiting human labour.”

With the refined and rich idea that `capital` means money employed in exploiting human labour, Engels provides in a single line an explanation of the meaning of the entire book `Capital` and makes all of us think deeply.

Where was Karl Marx, who laboured so hard to give us such a fine philosophical work, born? In Russia, described as a garden of common ownership? Or in Red China? No. He was born in a town called Trier in Germany. That town was a haven for priests and a stronghold of orthodoxy.

## PDF 151 / printed p.150

It is said that the German poet Goethe was astonished by the Christian churches, monasteries, religious institutions, residences of priests, and such things that he saw in Karl Marx's birthplace.

Karl Marx was born in a town so full of religious clergy. The town of Trier, where religious dominance was so strong, also tells the world that Karl Marx's ancestors were Jews. Their hereditary occupation—the hereditary occupation of Karl Marx's ancestors, which our Anaimuthu referred to here—was priesthood. They even changed that very name.

But Karl Marx's father, Hirschel Marx, practised law. The Jews themselves prevented him from practising law in that way. Because he was a Jew, Karl Marx's father did not receive sufficient income from legal practice. For that reason, on 24 August 1821, Hirschel Marx, raising the question that it was because he was a Jew that he was barred from practising law and had no income, converted to Christianity.

A year after he converted, his wife too converted to Christianity. Karl Marx, who was born and grew up in such a town filled with religious feeling and between parents who had changed religion, was the one who later said, “Religion

## PDF 152 / printed p.151

is opium for the people,” historians say with admiration.

At the beginning, he was involved in journalism. The region in which he was born was called Rhineland. Taking the name of that region, he and his friends entered that profession and started a newspaper called the “Rhineland Gazette.” That newspaper was started on 1 January 1812.

After completing his studies, during a period when he was unemployed, the articles he wrote in this newspaper brought both the newspaper and the name of Karl Marx great fame.

But after the newspaper's standing rose, the German government began to keep an eye on him. The reason was that he wrote criticizing the government, criticizing its policies, and criticizing its programmes, and the German government's eye fell upon him. Therefore he had to face many repressive measures.

An idea he set forth in that newspaper is one that should remain forever engraved in all our minds.

“There must not be the idea that people exist for the government. What is needed is the idea that the government exists for the people,” he says.

After this he stopped the Rhineland newspaper, and because of the government's restrictions circumstances forced him to go elsewhere. About the newspaper's

## PDF 153 / printed p.152

policies, Marx also says another idea very beautifully:

“Subscribers must not determine the policy of the newspaper. The newspaper must determine the policies of the subscribers.”

Subscribers means readers. When Thanthai Periyar started and ran the journal `Kudi Arasu`, people asked: Will this sell? Will people accept it? Will people be able to digest this idea? Periyar is said to have replied:

“Whether it sells or not, I will print the `Kudi Arasu` newspaper. Whether anyone buys it or not, I do not care. I will print at least one copy of the paper, and I alone will sit on the veranda and read that one copy myself.” (Applause)

Karl Marx expresses this same idea. Readers must not determine a newspaper's policy; the newspaper must determine the readers' policies. In his book, Saminatha Sarma very beautifully points to this breadth of Marx's thought.

When our professors here spoke about Karl Marx, they showed as evidence the many speeches delivered and writings produced by Arignar Anna on why there is an accord between the Dravidian movement and Karl Marx's principles, and an accord between the communist movement and the Dravidian movement.

## PDF 154 / printed p.153

When Anna refers to Karl Marx, he says:

“Poverty beside prosperity; fear beside strength—why these things? This question shook not ordinary people alone, but all those who stood as guardians of the world of ideas. From the time of the Greek philosopher Plato, who lived in the fourth century BCE, until before the time of Karl Marx, who lived in the nineteenth century CE, all inquiries into the welfare of the people failed to yield a conclusion suited to reason.

Master – slave  
High – low  
Religious teacher – devotee  
Landholder – cultivator  
Lord – servant  
Capitalist – worker

Karl Marx carefully analysed the history of the struggles that arose between them. He showed that the tyranny of lords and capitalists would not last long, and that only through the efforts of workers would a workers' world blossom. His clear conclusion brought oppressed workers together; it united the downtrodden and oppressed workers.”

## PDF 155 / printed p.154

Thus Arignar Anna speaks about Karl Marx.

Saminatha Sarma, in his book, points to lines of verse describing what the life of the great Karl Marx was like.

He fixed his ideal among the clouds.

But he had to live his life in the dust of the earth. He received only what he needed from day to day.

“He was pressed in on all four sides. Poverty tormented him. Need consumed him. In life generally, he was refined in the furnace. He was put through the crucible.”

These are lines of verse found in a book written by Saminatha Sarma.

I wish to remind you of this. I also wish to associate myself with those sentiments.

The accord between the Dravidian movement and communist principles is not something of today or yesterday. It is a relationship formed many years ago. Thanthai Periyar explained the doctrine, the true philosophy, that to say “Dravidar” itself means workers, means the proletariat. Whenever May Day was celebrated, Arignar Anna, in his speeches, said that to say “Dravidar” itself means workers.

## PDF 156 / printed p.155

Our beloved Nallakannu, who spoke here, said that we can dispel and remove certain illusions that have arisen in Tamil Nadu today, but that they have now taken deep root.

But our friend Sankarayya, who spoke and left, said, “It is only temporary.” Whether temporary or permanent, we all have the great duty of protecting Tamils, workers, the labouring masses, and this society without allowing such illusions to bewilder the minds of Tamils.

He said, “The two of us have united.” Forgetting that we too are on this stage, he said, “The two of us (CPI, CPM) have united.” Then he himself remembered and extended an invitation that you too should come. We are already together, are we not? So I do not know how you can say that the two of you have united and then invite us. Everyone must join together and strive to defeat the illusion that has arisen, for the future advancement of this society.

This feeling in me is not of today or yesterday. I am not now coming forward to tell you who I am merely by making use of this opportunity to speak at the release of `Capital`.

When, thirty years ago, I was asked in writing for a May Day greeting, the greeting I wrote was:

## PDF 157 / printed p.156

“Driving away weariness through fear,  
turning blood into a pool of sweat,  
the suffering worker too  
dances, saying, ‘It is a festival day.’”

You must not forget that the person who wrote such a May Day greeting is the one standing before you.

“Small streams, bunded fields, and rivers,  
good channels that hold back water and are laid out in order,  
ploughing and ploughing so that paddy may gather and yield fruit—  
all those shoulders full of labour,  
whose shoulders are they?”

You must not forget that I, who received the love and support of the revolutionary poet Bharathidasan who asked this question, am standing before you.

From the time I became involved in this movement as a boy—just yesterday, while speaking at a function, brother Duraimurugan said, “Had Periyar and Anna alone not been there, Kalaignar would have become a communist leader in Tamil Nadu.”

He said that because Periyar's Dravidian movement principles, social principles and Self-Respect principles, and Anna's Tamil consciousness attracted me, I did not become a communist through and through. Even if I do not bear the name “communist,” I am one who believes in common ownership. I am a

## PDF 158 / printed p.157

communist. That is why, in 1983, nearly fifteen years ago, why was there a need for me to write about Karl Marx's life in the `Kural Oviyam` that I wrote?

`Kural Oviyam` was written and published in 1983. I will read only the lines I wrote there about Karl Marx.

One hundred and sixty-five years ago—I say 165 because this was written fifteen years ago. But in fact, one hundred and eighty years ago—at the present point, a hundred years ago, the sculptor of egalitarianism Karl Marx, whose sky-high thought had come to a stop as he became a grave beneath the earth—we have come to know his doctrines. A body of people still raises the flag of struggle in many countries of this world, labouring with the eagerness to see when the goal of putting his ideas of common ownership into practice will succeed.

That great man—born into an ordinary family, living a very simple life, repeatedly caught in the cruel grip of poverty, and performing his duty without rest until he closed his eyes at the age of sixty-five—had a close friend like Engels; yet many do not know the greatness of the queenly woman Jenny, the lamp of the family, who stayed by his side and conducted family life with him until the end.

Born into a wealthy family, she was exceptionally beautiful. Many longed to marry her. But she waited seven years, determined that she would marry only Karl Marx, who had been born into an impoverished family, was not a match for her beauty, and was four years younger than she was. She did marry him. Whenever her husband was exiled, she went with him. When he was arrested, she too was arrested.

## PDF 159 / printed p.158

That lady Jenny writes as follows in a letter to a family friend:

“I had unbearable pain in my breast—back pain—and there was not enough money in hand even to arrange another woman to nurse the child. Enduring my breast pain, I nursed the child myself. The child developed convulsions and bit my breast; it became a wound and began to bleed. As a result, the child had to drink milk mixed with blood. In that condition, creditors entered the house and took our clothes, the children's cradle, and all the toys to put them up for auction. The child, after struggling with death for three days, died. We covered its body in the back room, while we and the other children lay on the floor in the front room throughout the night. When morning came, we could not even prepare a coffin for that child.

I did not endure these sufferings all alone. In one way, I am fortunate. My husband is everything to me—the fact that he is by my side is itself my consolation. I regard being with him, standing by him,

## PDF 160 / printed p.159

as the remedy for all my sufferings. But of those who have benefited from my husband in many ways, only one comes forward to help. When I think of that, my heart burns like fire. Is it not my happiness to stand joined with him as his companion while he performs his duty even in a helpless situation?”

The sufferings and sorrows that arose in the family life of Karl Marx, the owner of that treasury of ideas which the eyes of the world read and throb over today, were not one or two but a thousand. Should we not, wholeheartedly and with full voice, praise Jenny—that sacred flame of womankind who endured all of them and sustained him—as the ideal of “the excellence of a life-companion”?

Here is the Kural that states the definition of the excellence of a life-companion:

She who possesses the virtues fit for a home and, having become so,  
lives according to the resources of the man who took her, is the life-companion.

If Karl Marx came to my mind when I drew my `Kural Oviyam` for the Kural meaning that the true life-companion is a woman who possesses the good qualities suited to domestic life and conducts life, without any grievance, in accordance with her husband's material resources, I leave it to Nallakannu's good judgement whether I am a communist or not.

But there are some reasons why communism, in the name of communism, has not spread further in Tamil Nadu. Communism, according to Anna's

## PDF 161 / printed p.160

principles, according to Periyar's principles, and as I see it, must be developed in a manner suited to this soil. I ask that we recognize and think about this and undertake our efforts in that manner.

In India there are many nationalities, with distinctions of language, art, culture, cultural tradition, ethnicity and so on. Those many national peoples have many cultures. They have many languages. Taking all these into account, I point out here that if we make the effort in a manner suited to this soil, we can spread communism here. Brother Naganathan deserves all the praise we can give him for providing all of us with this book, a treasury like a precious trove. Jamadhagni, who translated this book into Tamil, deserves all the praise we can give him. He spent many years in prison. He was a man of sacrifice. He dedicated not only himself but his entire family to the freedom of India. His dear daughter Shanthi and Shanthi's dear husband Naganathan have now come forward to publish the workers' literature that this man of sacrifice laboured over and wrote in abundance continuously for four or five years, thereby spreading his fame. On my own behalf and on behalf of all of you, I convey to them my thanks, appreciation and greetings, and take my leave.
