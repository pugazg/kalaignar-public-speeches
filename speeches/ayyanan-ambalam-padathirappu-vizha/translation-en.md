## Ayyanan Ambalam Portrait Unveiling Function

We all know very well, from the speeches delivered by the leaders of all parties, the depth of sorrow with which this function is taking place here today. As our dear friend Tindivanam Ramamurthi pointed out here, I have known Ayyanan Ambalam well since 1960, and for many years I had the opportunity to move closely with him. When I think that this too is a great loss to me, I am also overcome by the concern that this loss cannot be made good so quickly.

Not merely for Tamil Nadu, not merely for the districts in the southern part of Tamil Nadu, but at a time when he had risen to become a leader of the Forward Bloc party at the all-India level, we have

## PDF 401 / printed p.400

lost him. When we think of that, we must regard this as a great loss to all of India and to the ideal of an egalitarian society that must emerge in India.

As all our friends recently pointed out, we remember his participation in the all-party leaders' meeting conducted on behalf of the Tamil Nadu Government for religious harmony and social unity, and how calmly he spoke there. That memory remains impressed in everyone's heart. For even in a heated atmosphere, he acted there in a manner meant to cool the heat and offer luminous ideas.

At one meeting, our friend Santhanam became highly agitated and, because of speeches made by some people saying, “Come, let us go,” rose to leave. Ayyanan Ambalam, remaining there, looked at me. I gestured to him to remain seated. Immediately, after calming Santhanam too and making him sit in his seat, Ayyanan Ambalam remained seated and, as our friend Varadarajan mentioned, gave great cooperation throughout the two-day event so that the principle for which we had assembled and the decision we had gathered to take could be fulfilled.

He was not someone who could be viewed within the circle of one particular community. He was not confined within a social

## PDF 402 / printed p.401

circle; he carried in his heart the vast principle that every community is part of human society. That is why, instead of arguing and struggling for one particular community alone, he continually raised his voice for the whole of society that was oppressed, suppressed, downtrodden and demanding its rights.

It is easy to incite and stir up some people to take part in struggles for the rise of a community. Both young and old must forever keep in their hearts what Mahatma Gandhi taught about what the goal is and how it should be attained. More important than attaining the goal is the approach we take in order to attain it. The late great Ayyanan Ambalam learned that approach well and put what he had learned into practice. Rather than achieving a goal through violence, only if that goal is achieved through good methods — through constructive work that attracts everyone and gains everyone's support — will it endure.

When we think of the condition of Tamil Nadu today, someone at some time may have been responsible for creating caste divisions. But those who were accused of having caused caste differences are not the people who today stand in the field trying to inflame caste

## PDF 403 / printed p.402

hatred and turn Tamil Nadu into a field of violence. Rather, it is we, the people who were divided — divided along caste lines — who are today showing caste difference and religious difference toward one another and fighting among ourselves.

In those days, authors writing books would first describe the prosperity of a country. Only after doing so would they write a novel. In describing it they would begin by saying it was a land rich in water and soil, and to show the excellence of the rule in that country they would write that the tiger and the deer drank water at the same watering place.

I think about that. I have said once before, with anguish: “Let us not even ask for the tiger and deer to drink at the same watering place as a symbol of unity as in those days; in these days, should not at least deer and deer drink at the same watering place?” For it is those of us who are in the condition of being oppressed and suppressed who are clashing with one another today. Those of us who are backward, those of us who are most backward, and those of us who are downtrodden are the very people clashing with one another today.

## PDF 404 / printed p.403

Imaginations were created about birth — people born from the head, people born from the shoulders, people born from the thighs, people born from the feet. And it is we who are regarded as those born from the feet who are today clashing with one another. The head, shoulders and thighs are all peaceful and safe. It is the feet that are suffering every hardship today.

All leaders are striving so that we may free ourselves from these harmful acts and live in unity. One of the leaders who strove in that way was the late great Ayyanan Ambalam.

Following the path of Thevar Thirumagan Pasumpon Muthuramalinga Thevar, he continued his own journey. The path of Thevar Thirumagan Muthuramalinga Thevar was the path of Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose. When we returned to office in 1996, Santhanam and Vallarasu, speaking in the Legislative Assembly, said that in the coming golden-jubilee year of Independence, a statue of Netaji, who had fought greatly for freedom, should be unveiled in the capital of Tamil Nadu. I accepted it immediately and announced it in the Assembly itself. No sooner had that news appeared in the newspapers than Ayyanan Ambalam telephoned me from Madurai and thanked me. With that memory, in the golden-jubilee

## PDF 405 / printed p.404

year of Independence, I invited him to the function at which the statue of Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose was unveiled on Kamarajar Salai along the Chennai seafront. He came and spoke. The speech he delivered still echoes in my ears.

Even today, after his passing — and even before that, and indeed afterward too — the friends who spoke here referred to the speech he delivered. I too remember it. Ayyanan Ambalam proclaimed strongly that neither Dr. Ambedkar nor Pasumpon Muthuramalinga Thevar was a caste leader; both were national leaders and deserved to be honoured at the national level.

Those who today stir up caste feelings should think about what Tamil Nadu was like fifty years ago. If they think of the efforts made by Thanthai Periyar, Perarignar Anna, the respected Quaid-e-Millath and Perunthalaivar Kamaraj to dim caste feelings and labour for a condition in which all were one community and all one people, I believe today's younger generation will reshape its methods and impulses and adopt the approach taught by Mahatma Gandhi, so that all may be one community, all in Tamil Nadu may be Tamils, and all in the sacred land of India may be Indians.

## PDF 406 / printed p.405

Comrade Biswas, M.P., even referred in the condolence address he delivered for this function to what Pasumpon Thevar Thirumagan had said. In 1957, Thevar Thirumagan said these words: “Even if our enemies badly provoke us and drag us into a quarrel, if anyone responds to violence with violence instead of preserving peace, it is equal to tearing open my heart and drinking my blood.” When Thevar Thirumagan Pasumpon Muthuramalinga Thevar said that, can there be a place for violence in Tamil Nadu? Can that Thevar Thirumagan be accused? In the same way, can another section go about with the arrogance that some people must remain low, remain subdued, remain submissive, and that they were born only for us to dominate them? We must find answers to these questions.

The answer we find must be the condition in Tamil Nadu in which everyone is one community. Establishing that condition is the tribute we can offer to that departed great man.

The chair of the function, Ashok Ghosh, mentioned that when this great man passed away they had asked for a judicial inquiry and that I had accepted it; he also pointed out that some other friends here had made requests that should be accepted.

## PDF 407 / printed p.406

As all the friends who spoke here pointed out, more important than whether we accept the requests made at this function, how we accept them, and when we fulfil them, is the most important request placed before us by that great man, Ayyanan Ambalam, who is gone yet not gone. Let us fulfil that request. Ayyanan Ambalam left us this counsel: “We are all brothers — there are no quarrels or disputes among us, no religious animosities; we will not lean into caste and religion and ruin our sense of equality.” Let us accept that counsel and fulfil that request.

I convey my deep condolences, on my own behalf, on behalf of the Government of Tamil Nadu and on behalf of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, for his passing; for the passing along with him of Muthurama Lingam, leader of the Tamil State Forward Bloc party; and for the passing of Usilai Rajan, organising secretary of that party. With this, I conclude my speech.
