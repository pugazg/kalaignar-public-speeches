## PDF 70 / printed p.69

### Purusai Gopalarathinam House Wedding

With the warm good wishes of all of us, the wedding ceremony has been completed here between Malarvizhi, the daughter of Gopalarathinam, who is Parasuraman's brother, and Muralidharan, the son of Subramaniam of Ellaikkudaiyur, Mettur taluk, Salem district.

Those who spoke before me have all explained whose family function this is.

Our Parasuraman has always been a man with a heart free of guile. He is without deceit or duplicity. Village people are generally like that. So, while addressing us, after saying, “Kalaignar avargale,” without even knowing whether the Professor had come here or not, he said, “Professor avargale.” All of you heard that and kept peering at the stage, wondering whether, without our knowing it, the Professor had come and

## PDF 71 / printed p.70

was sitting somewhere! Such is Parasuraman, a man without guile. By taking part in this wedding taking place in his home, I have not merely come to bless the bride and groom; I also stand before you to remind you of your duty to send to Parliament people with such pure hearts, people with hearts free of deceit.

As far as this constituency is concerned, brother Murasoli Maran has been fielded as the DMK candidate in Central Chennai. Looking at all of you here, I cannot ask, “Vote only in the Central Chennai constituency!” You have come from Salem district. You have come from Chengalpattu. You have come from Chennai and North Chennai. I know very well who most of those who have come here are. I also know very well that most of you here are people who received the 20 per cent reservation under the DMK government. (loud applause)

That reservation could not be obtained despite struggle after struggle and even after many people were sacrificed to bullets. As soon as I assumed the responsibility of government in 1989, I called everyone who had to be called and spoke with them, and issued the order that “20 per cent reservation shall henceforth be provided to the Most Backward Classes.”

## PDF 72 / printed p.71

You know very well that the person who issued that order is the one standing before you.

The Dravidian Movement was begun even before 1916 for the welfare of the oppressed, backward and most backward people of Tamil Nadu.

The movement that has politically evolved as a successor to that Dravidian Movement and is working today is the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam.

Although the DMK assumed responsibility for government under Anna's leadership in 1967, before that—before the twenty years of Congress rule—you know that the Justice Party had once taken responsibility for governing the Madras Presidency, the Madras State, which included Tamil Nadu, some parts of Kerala, some parts of Andhra and some parts of Karnataka. That Justice Party's tradition is the DMK's tradition; the DMK is its political heir.

What was the policy of the Justice Party? Equality and social justice. That is why, in those days, the Justice Party placed the symbol of a balance scale on its flag. It placed that symbol because equality and social justice had to be given to everyone. That movement, called the “Justice Party” in English, has its political heir today in the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam; I am duty-bound to point that out to you.

## PDF 73 / printed p.72

Thanthai Periyar led this movement, and after him Perarignar Anna held that this movement should also be placed in charge of government and that it should win the people's support for that purpose. If the social-reform ideas advocated by Periyar were to be made into law, we had to enter government. For during the Congress period, even though Congress had won with Periyar's support, its Cabinet did not accept Periyar's social-reform policies, Periyar's demands, or the thoughts and views of the Dravidar Kazhagam.

Periyar struggled. We argued in the Legislative Assembly on Periyar's behalf. We even introduced a private member's resolution that Self-Respect marriages should be legally valid. None of that was accepted. As Maran pointed out, only after Anna became Chief Minister was a resolution brought forward and passed declaring that “Self-Respect marriages shall be legally valid.”

For this land to be named “Tamil Nadu,” martyrs such as Sankaralinga Nadar gave up their very lives, fasting unto death! Even then, this land was not given that name. You know that only after Arignar Anna assumed office as Chief Minister, under the DMK government, did this land receive the name “Tamil Nadu.”

## PDF 74 / printed p.73

Likewise, people had fought against the dominance of Hindi from 1937–38 onward and many lives had been sacrificed without securing that victory. Only after Anna became Chief Minister and the DMK government came to office was a firm order issued that “there will henceforth be no place for Hindi domination in Tamil Nadu,” and it was declared that “in Tamil Nadu's schools there will henceforth be only Tamil and English—there is no place for Hindi.” You all know that Tamil Nadu was the only state in India to make such a declaration, and that the reason was the DMK government and Arignar Anna.

Thus the Dravidian Movement, which had been a social-reform movement, also entered politics and succeeded in the great task of turning many social-reform ideas into law.

You know this: it was when I was Chief Minister for the third time, in 1989, that I brought in and enacted a law giving women equal rights in property, an equal half-share in their parents' property just like men. At that time I said in the Legislative Assembly: “This was a resolution moved by Thanthai Periyar at the Self-Respect Conference held in Chengalpattu in 1929—a resolution that women should have equal rights in property. Sixty years later, today, I have made that idea of Periyar into law.”

## PDF 75 / printed p.74

Not only equal rights for women in property. In the local-body elections conducted by this government six or seven months ago—in municipal corporations, municipalities, unions and various village panchayats—the only government to provide 33 per cent reservation for women was the DMK government; I am duty-bound to explain that to you.

There is a woman Mayor in Tiruchirappalli (just as there is a male Mayor here in Chennai) and a woman Mayor in Tirunelveli. Women were given rights to that extent. Not only that: under the Kazhagam government in 1989, it was announced that unmarried women need only have studied up to the eighth standard—not even that they must have passed, merely that they had studied—and those poor women would receive Rs. 5,000 in financial assistance for marriage. After I came to office for the fourth time, saying that Rs. 5,000 was not enough, I announced Rs. 10,000. In this year and a half alone, marriages have been conducted for 46,000 couples at an expenditure of Rs. 46 crore. This is this government.

You know what kind of government the previous one was. Our Deputy Speaker, brother Parithi Ilamvazhuthi, explained it clearly here. They said a hundred crore rupees was spent on the foster son's wedding. We did not even say

## PDF 76 / printed p.75

that. They themselves described it on their own television channel as a “wedding without precedent in history.” On their own television they broadcast, “There has never been a wedding like this anywhere in the world, and there never will be one again.” This is a fact known to the whole town.

But what happened? The next year that lady said, “He is not my foster son at all”! They printed invitations saying it was the wedding of foster son Sudhakaran, distributed them not merely to the ordinary people here but even to the Prime Minister of India and the President, conducted the wedding here with great pomp, and a year later said, “He is not my foster son.” In the whole world, there can be no one except Jayalalithaa clever enough to change in the next year what she said a year earlier. One day she would call someone a dear friend. The next day she would say, “Not a friend, but a sister not born of the same womb.” She would say, “I cannot live separated from her.” Some ministers went and asked, “Amma, is it not because of her that you have acquired a bad name? Could you not stay apart from her for a while?” That Amma asked in return, “Would you live apart from your wife?” A sudden question, a thunderbolt question! All the ministers were stunned. “Just as you cannot live apart from your wife, I cannot live apart from Sasikala,” she replied in a single

## PDF 77 / printed p.76

word and drove them all away—the former Chief Minister Jayalalithaa, that great lady.

You know all the corruption that took place under her government. I do not wish to list every item. I shall give just one example; you may understand from it.

That lady is very angry with me and with Chidambaram. She says she will come back to power. She says she will put me, Chidambaram and Moopanar in the dock. She says she will conduct inquiries and punish us. As though they themselves were the judges, they have already announced everything now.

Do you know why she is angry with Chidambaram? Three hundred thousand US dollars came to this lady from abroad. Three hundred thousand dollars meant one crore rupees in our Indian currency. Chidambaram discovered this. He told the officials to inquire: “Ask where the money came from!” The officials came and asked, “Did three hundred thousand dollars arrive?” “Yes, it did,” she said. She could not say “no,” because this lady had deposited it in a bank in her own name. So she could not deny it. “It came,” she said. “Who sent it?” they asked. “I do not know,” she said.

An unknown person in America sends one crore rupees to Madam Jayalalithaa—

## PDF 78 / printed p.77

an unknown person. A man whose address is unknown sends it. A man walking along the street sends one crore rupees!

When asked, “What did you do?” she said, “I deposited it in the bank.” They asked, “Why did you deposit it? When an unknown person sent it, should you not have sent it back to that person?” She said, “I did not know the sender's address. So I immediately called the auditor and asked, ‘What can I do?’ I told the auditor, ‘One crore rupees has come like this. I do not know who sent it; I do not know what to do.’ The auditor said, ‘Deposit it in the bank in your own name.’” Thus, says Jayalalithaa, after calling the auditor and finding out what to do, she deposited it in the bank in her own name. In the villages they say, “The little girl who knew nothing put the bolt on herself.” In the same way, this Amma supposedly deposited it in her own name.

Think about it for a moment. This is a wedding. AIADMK members may also have come here. Parasuraman may have invited them too. It is election time; he would have invited everyone. They too may have come.

The brothers belonging to the AIADMK should think about this. All right, one crore rupees came. She did not know what to do and called the auditor to ask. Instead, why could that one crore rupees not have been deposited in the party fund itself? Have you thought about that? Was not that lady the Chief Minister

## PDF 79 / printed p.78

when this money arrived? Why could she not have deposited that money in the Chief Minister's Relief Fund? The party fund did not come to mind. The Chief Minister's Relief Fund did not enter her head. She deposited that money privately, in a bank, in her own name.

Corrupt acts of this kind were not one or two; they took place to the tune of many crores of rupees during five years of rule. It was only after clearing all that away that, in the 1996 election, you gave your support to the DMK, the Tamil Maanila Congress and the other allied parties and made us victorious. It is only on your command that we are placing that lady and the other ministers before the courts and conducting the cases. Those inquiries must continue. At the Centre there had been a good government, but within a year and a half a situation arose in which that government had to fall. So that good government may bloom there again, give your finest blessings to the bride and groom, and give your votes to Maran—that is my request.
