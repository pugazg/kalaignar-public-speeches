# The Time When Youth Give the Orders! — English translation

At Tagore Arts College in Puducherry, Karunanidhi begins humorously by asking whether the occasion is a student-union inauguration or a festival of demands, because the student leader has used his welcome speech to press ministers for institutional improvements, relief from rising prices and a university for Puducherry.

He tells students that their concerns cannot stop at their own college or State. They must also understand national and international issues. The contemporary Iran–Iraq war becomes his example: events far away can affect India's economy, energy supply and future. The principle `யாதும் ஊரே; யாவரும் கேளிர்` therefore has practical meaning, although universal human concern need not erase one's own language, community or national identity.

The speech then turns to the anxieties facing young graduates: unemployment, inequality and an economy controlled by a small number of people. Invoking Tagore's image of God in the smile of the poor and Bharathidasan's poetry about labour, he asks how far society has actually succeeded in creating such a smile.

Karunanidhi says democracy gives people the power to change governments, but stable governments still face difficult social and economic problems. Young people must therefore issue not impulsive commands but informed commands born of mature thought.

He recalls his own youth and the orders he once gave in struggles for Tamil, rationalism and against superstition. The climax is his memory of carrying the Tamil flag in Tiruvarur during the 1938 anti-Hindi agitation when he was about fourteen.

He asks the younger generation to continue that commitment: to protect Tamil language, literature and history while holding together the identities “Tamil by language, Dravidian by race, Indian by nation, human by the world.”

## Separate source closing note

Speech delivered at a Tagore Arts College function in Puducherry on 5 October 1980.
