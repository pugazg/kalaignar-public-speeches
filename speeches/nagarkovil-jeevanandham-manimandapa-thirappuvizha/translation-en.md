### Nagarkovil Jeevanandham Memorial Hall Opening

Because this event taking place today has gone on for a long time, I am standing before you intending to take leave of you quickly. After taking leave, I must go through Nellai to Vagaikulam and reach there by 4:30; only then will I be able to depart on the special aircraft leaving Vagaikulam for Chennai. After that, I will not be able to leave.

The person who repeatedly urged, both in the Legislative Assembly and privately with me, that this memorial hall in the name of our elder brother Jeevanandham, which is being opened today, must somehow be established was Mani, who has been elected to the Legislative Assembly from this district on behalf of the Marxist Communist Party. Because of his great effort, because of the affection I have for elder brother Jeevanandham, and because I felt that his

## PDF 114 / printed p.113

life should serve as a lesson to the youth of today and to the youth of the future, and that this would stand as a good symbol of that, it has been built and is being opened today.

This memorial hall has cost fifteen lakh and fifty thousand rupees. At the same time, in this district, an eye-hospital building costing approximately 38 lakh rupees is also being opened today. A municipal reading room costing 10 lakh rupees is being opened. Seven vehicles purchased at a cost of 49 lakh rupees for drinking-water distribution in Nagarkovil city are being put into service. An integrated milk-production development scheme is being implemented at a cost of 91 lakh rupees. Thus, projects worth approximately 2 crore rupees are being completed and set in motion.

In addition, at the foundation-stone-laying function for projects that are to be begun, a foundation stone is being laid today for a district chest-disease building estimated at 30 lakh rupees. A foundation stone has been laid for a district Siddha medical division building to be constructed at an estimated cost of 20 lakh rupees. A foundation stone has been laid for the Keezhamanakudi–Melamanakudi connecting bridge to be built at an estimated cost of 4 crore rupees. A foundation stone has been laid for a Backward Class college-students' welfare hostel building to be constructed at an estimated cost of 43 lakh rupees. Approximately

## PDF 115 / printed p.114

5 crore rupees' worth of projects have had foundation stones laid.

Under the Moovalur Ramamirtham Ammaiyar Memorial Marriage Assistance Scheme, financial assistance of 36 lakh rupees is being given to 360 women. Free house-site pattas are being given to 300 beneficiaries at a cost of 40 lakh rupees. Under the Integrated Rural Development Scheme, loans of nearly 4 lakh rupees are being given to 37 beneficiaries. Under the Indira Housing Scheme, 25 cluster houses valued at six and three-quarter lakh rupees are being given today to 25 beneficiaries.

Under the Singaravelar Memorial Housing Scheme, 240 houses costing 77 lakh rupees are being given free of cost to fisherfolk today. Two farmers who achieved high yields have been given incentive prizes: the first prize is eight thousand rupees and the second prize four thousand rupees, a total of twelve thousand rupees.

Under the rehabilitation scheme for persons with disabilities, three-wheeled vehicles worth fifteen thousand rupees are being provided to five beneficiaries.

In this way, various welfare schemes worth approximately nine crore rupees are being provided today for the benefit of the people of this district. Even though so many schemes have been announced and implemented today, representatives of the various organizations who spoke here—whether they are representatives in Parliament's Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha, or representatives in the Legislative

## PDF 116 / printed p.115

Assembly, or representatives of local bodies—have placed many demands before us here.

When the Legislative Assembly is in session, it is the convention that no announcement should be made outside it. If you ask whether our Members of the Legislative Assembly do not know this convention, they do know it. Even so, they have placed some demands directly before you, as though saying: “You said I did not argue in the Assembly. I may have argued and it may not have appeared in the newspapers; I am placing these demands once again before you here only to prove that I will argue for them.” In that manner, they have put forward many views and many demands here.

I am not one who rejects those demands. I can only say that they will be fulfilled. If I were to announce exactly when they would be fulfilled, these very people might tomorrow incite someone in the Assembly and have them ask, “When the Assembly is in session, how could the Chief Minister announce all these things outside?” It is because of that concern that I have not stated categorically here whether and when their demands will be fulfilled. Even though I have not announced it, I am bound to tell you that all of them will certainly be fulfilled soon and, at the same time, at the appropriate time.

Everyone has spoken about the medical college. There is meaning in Velayudham's haste when he says, “Announce it this very year.” That is because he belongs to a party capable of calculating whether he will be in power next year or not. So if he says, “Announce it this year itself; next year it may not be possible to announce it,” then, if anything of that kind should happen, I declare here that next year I myself will come and announce it. (Loud applause)

When medical colleges were announced this year for Thoothukudi and Vellore, our Minister for People's Welfare, Arcot Veerasamy, while saying in the Legislative Assembly that there would be “two more colleges,” included Nagarkovil as well.

When speaking of Nagarkovil, he specified that “because facilities are not available this year, it will be announced next year.” If, in the meantime, the necessary facilities become available, I wish to state here that there is even the possibility of starting it this year, even without making a separate announcement.

Another matter is one general problem—and several problems. The water problem. The dam problem. Our Members of Parliament, Members of the Rajya Sabha and Members of the Legislative Assembly have all raised these problems. Our friend Kumaradas, Member of Parliament

## PDF 118 / printed p.117

Dennis, Moses, and all our friends who spoke have said that it would be most fitting for a memorial hall for the great leader Kamaraj to be established in the coastal area at Kanyakumari, at the place where his ashes were immersed.

I have no two views about its being situated in the coastal area. On one occasion, the then Prime Minister Indira Gandhi told me personally, and also announced publicly, that “there should not be a large number of buildings packed closely along the seashore.” After that announcement, even the Supreme Court expressed a view, and the principle that “there should not be buildings along the seashore” came to be adopted as a norm. Even so, you are not unaware of the respect we have had for the great leader Kamaraj despite our many differences of policy.

I wish to tell you one thing. Whatever party deceased persons may have belonged to, if they laboured for the country, for the language, for the people, for society—if they were people devoted to the public good—neither the party to which I belong nor my government has ever forgotten them.

When I assumed office as Chief Minister in 1969, the very first time, for Thillaiyadi Valliammai—who had taken part in the struggle in South Africa together with Mahatma Gandhi—

## PDF 119 / printed p.118

it was I who established, at Thillaiyadi in Thanjavur district, a settlement called “Thillaiyadi Valliammai Nagar.” I also wish to tell you that it was I who built houses there in the names of two Adi Dravida youths, Nagappan and Narayanasamy.

You know, and you have read, that Chidambaranar pulled the oil press for the freedom of this country, was imprisoned, and endured torture. But I ask you—I ask all the nationalists, including our friend Kumarasamy—during twenty years of Congress rule, did you ever search to find where the oil press he pulled was? No. During twenty years of Congress rule, no one searched to find where the oil press Chidambaranar pulled in the days of the British was. No one should forget that it was this Karunanidhi who had the thought that it must be found.

I searched for it, learned that the oil press was in Coimbatore prison, immediately had it brought out, had a simple separate pavilion built for it beside Rajaji Hall, placed the press there, wrote “The oil press pulled by Chidambaranar,” and turned it into an exhibit. That act was carried out by me under the D.M.K. government.

## PDF 120 / printed p.119

The memorial hall for the great leader Kamaraj is in Chennai, at Guindy, beside Raj Bhavan. Why was it placed there? Because that is where his body was cremated. Why was it cremated there? Because Gandhi Mandapam is there at Guindy, beside Raj Bhavan. We cremated Kamaraj's body there and built that memorial hall there because it was fitting that Kamaraj's memorial should stand beside Gandhi Mandapam. When the hall was nearing completion and the question arose as to what symbol should be placed on its facade, it was I who proposed that “the spinning-wheel symbol should be placed there.” It was I who returned home at midnight after having the spinning-wheel symbol installed. The next day the government was dismissed. I was also the one who learned that news.

The very next day after my government was dismissed, they removed that spinning-wheel symbol. Why did they remove it? Because it had been the election symbol of the Kamaraj Congress Party, they said that the spinning-wheel symbol should not be there and removed it. Later, after the Janata government came to power, we raised our voice over it and the spinning-wheel symbol was restored there.

Kamaraj and we may have had any number of differences of opinion. But we honoured the great leader Kamaraj, whom we accepted as a flame of sacrifice, as a noble embodiment of sacrifice. We honoured him because honouring him would be, as it were, honouring

## PDF 121 / printed p.120

ourselves.

Likewise, when the elder statesman Rajaji died, a demand arose that a memorial should be erected for him. Some people said, “There is already a large Rajaji Hall.” Even so, because there should be a memorial after his death, it was I who erected the Rajaji memorial at Guindy on the other side of Gandhi Mandapam—Kamaraj's memorial on one side and Rajaji's memorial on the other. When I was asked, “What symbol can be placed on it?” I said: “You know how far apart I and the Ramayana are. Even so, Rajaji was deeply attached to the Ramayana. He even wrote a serialized illustrated work called ‘Chakravarthi Thirumagan.’ Therefore, the facade of that memorial should be like Rama's crown.” I wish to tell you that it was I who drew and gave the plan for it.

In this way, whoever the national martyrs may be, even recently I built and inaugurated a memorial hall for the elder Bhaktavatsalam. Just as Jeeva's family members are taking part today, Bhaktavatsalam's family too took part in that function, remembered him with overflowing gratitude, and spoke.

You know the extent of the relationship between Bhaktavatsalam and me.

## PDF 122 / printed p.121

But his service, his sacrifice, and the days he spent in prison for the freedom of the country are all worthy of esteem and deserving of respect. That is why a memorial hall was erected for the elder Bhaktavatsalam.

I have known elder brother Jeevanandham from my youth. He was a Self-Respect activist in the path of Thanthai Periyar. In politics, although he was in the Congress movement and later worked in the communist movement, elder brother P. Jeevanandham lived as a Self-Respect activist until the end of his life. I was fortunate to have the opportunity to associate closely with him. Perarignar Anna held Jeevanandham in great esteem. To say more, his simple life should serve as a lesson to every politician engaged in public life.

When he finally died, he passed away at night in the hospital in circumstances in which there was not even anyone beside him. At dawn I received the news: “Jeeva has died. His body is in the Chennai hospital.” I immediately drove to Anna's house, took Anna with me, and Anna, N. V. Natarajan, and I went at once to the hospital, where that

## PDF 123 / printed p.122

simple man, that man of iron, that great man Jeevanandham who sang revolutionary songs so that an age of awakening might be seen, had been placed in the mortuary (place where bodies are kept). We saw his body brought out from the mortuary, placed a garland on it, attended to all the arrangements for the burial of his body, and conveyed our deep condolences that day to all the leaders of the Communist Party. This is an example of the extent to which, amid many differences of opinion, we held elder brother Jeevanandham in respect and affection.

Whether they were Congressmen, Communists, or belonged to any other movement, this movement has possessed the dignity and the culture to respect them. This is the movement created by Perarignar Anna. To put it even more plainly, it is a movement capable of respecting others. But, generally in our society, this culture should not be a one-way street. I say this to the leaders and friends in all parties.

Even today, on Kamaraj's birthday and memorial day, we go and place wreaths. We garland him. Even today, on the memorial

## PDF 124 / printed p.123

day of the elder statesman Rajaji, we go and place wreaths. We garland his statue. Everyone goes and garlands Ambedkar. But do some party members possess the disposition to go to Periyar's memorial on his birthday or on the day he died, or to garland his statue? No. Likewise, on Anna's birthday and on the day he died, do they follow the practice of remembering him, paying homage to him, or placing a flower garland on his statue? No.

That is why I say this. When a person dies, we should forget parties and preserve our culture with the feeling that he is someone who lives in the hearts of everyone. I wish only to say at this moment that it should not become a one-way street.

At this time, as I inaugurate Jeevanandham's memorial hall, his life is one that stands as a guide to all young people. When, at a young age, he was a boy and a student in the village of Boothapandi, it was apparently customary to distribute sundal at the Boothapandi temple. Even when that sundal was distributed, instead of eating and enjoying it by himself, he would bring many others—many students, boys, and poor

## PDF 125 / printed p.124

girls—and would eat only after sharing that sundal with all of them. This is an incident of historical significance that is highlighted even in the story of his youthful life.

“Sharing one's food and sustaining many lives is the foremost
of all the virtues that learned works have gathered.”

Thus said Valluvar. It is for that Valluvar that we are erecting a statue at the Kumari point. It was because elder brother Jeeva acquired, even in his youth, that practice of sharing what he received and eating together in the path of Valluvar, that he was able to join himself to the communist movement, whose principle was that there must be equality and social justice for everyone!

It was because of the conviction he held even in his youth that untouchability must be abolished that elder brother Jeevanandham took part even then in the struggle conducted at Vaikom by Thanthai Periyar to abolish untouchability.

All these are principles that the youth who are to become the future rulers of our society must read and follow. I do not have to tell the people of this district all these principles as though they were new. That is because Kanyakumari district is, among the various districts of Tamil Nadu, one of the few

## PDF 126 / printed p.125

districts distinguished for its knowledge. Kanyakumari district is a district capable of thinking clearly and arriving at firm decisions on anything. That is why, even in this election, Kanyakumari district took a decisive decision, as all the friends who spoke before me have pointed out. (Applause) They keep asking me, “Do something, do something for such a district!” I ask you: they are asking for a Kamaraj memorial hall to be built. In accordance with your wish, I give you here the assurance that the hall will be built on the seashore at the place where his ashes were immersed. (Loud applause)

There is no need to ask, “Is this not an assurance that should be announced in the Legislative Assembly?” This has nothing to do with the proceedings of the Assembly. Therefore, I give that assurance.

The Thiruvalluvar statue is being established there. In 1975, when I was Chief Minister, the decision taken at a Cabinet meeting was that a Thiruvalluvar statue should be erected there. After that, the function marking the start of its work took place on the seashore in Kanyakumari district after I returned as Chief Minister in 1989. It is a work spanning approximately twenty or twenty-five years. That is why the statue, which might have been completed at a cost of one crore or two crore rupees, now appears likely to cost more than 8 crore rupees. Whether it costs 8 crore or however many crore, the statue being erected there

## PDF 127 / printed p.126

will, I am happy to say, be a marvellous statue that people from all over the world and tourists will come to see. I tell you that the opening ceremony of that statue too will certainly take place either at the end of this year or at the beginning of the next year.

I wish to state that this government will engage in considering how to fulfil the various demands that have been placed concerning this district, and I thank all the friends who placed those demands.
