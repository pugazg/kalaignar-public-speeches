### Chennai - Chennai Suburban Traders' Association Conference

Many people are standing in line in this hall to drape shawls on me. You have also placed before me many demands. Only as a test, I have said, “The shawls may be draped after I finish speaking.” It is only to test whether you will still drape shawls after I finish speaking that I have said: drape them afterwards—if you have confidence in my speech, you may drape them after that. (applause)

This is not merely a conference of demands; at the beginning, Navamani and the others also said that it is a conference for expressing gratitude. The conference is taking place at Valluvar Kottam. When a conference is being held at the Kottam named after Valluvar, who wrote the Kural saying, “It is not good to forget gratitude,” no one would expect this conference to take place in forgetfulness of gratitude. In keeping with that, with a sense of gratitude, your

## PDF 55 / printed p.54

You have offered speeches of greeting and praise, and you have also placed a few representations before us.

Everyone who spoke said one thing. Madurai Rathinavelu in particular said, “It is precisely to fulfil all the demands that the Chief Minister has brought the entire Cabinet here.” Usually such demands are discussed calmly in a meeting and announcements are made according to the majority decision. I wondered whether I could announce them right here. Of the twenty-six ministers, only seven or eight have come. Therefore, I first wish to tell Rathinavelu and all of you that I am in no position here to take a decision by looking at the majority and announce it. (loud applause)

That does not mean I will disregard all your demands. (applause) I shall carry all of them in my heart and, at the appropriate time, convene the Cabinet, without looking at majority or minority, arrive at a good unanimous decision, and at the proper time announce it to you. I convey that good news here. (loud applause)

On the “badge” given to us at this conference, the symbol of a balance scale has been printed. The balance is the symbol of the traders' association. The very beginning of our movement was the balance scale. You know very well that the Justice Party is what has become the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam today. On the flag of that Justice Party,

## PDF 56 / printed p.55

the symbol was the balance. When our predecessors began this movement because justice was needed for the various sections of society, they placed the balance symbol on the flag that was flown for this movement. As people who have come along the path of that balance symbol, I wish to point out especially here that you and we are of the same caste.

The statistics showing the extent to which, under the Kazhagam government, the representations of the trading community have been heard and their demands fulfilled have already been explained here by my younger brothers who are ministers and who spoke before me.

The Traders' Welfare Board was mentioned. In 1975, when the Kazhagam government was in office, I announced that a Traders' Welfare Board would be formed. I also announced that a substantial sum would be allocated for it. But before the Board could be formally constituted and begin functioning, the Kazhagam government was dismissed. You know that those who came to power afterwards did not come forward either to continue the Traders' Welfare Board or to put it into operation. Later, only after I assumed office as Chief Minister for the third time in 1989 was the Traders' Welfare Board restarted, and a substantial sum—two crore rupees—was provided on behalf of the government for the Traders' Welfare Board, which is functioning today.

## PDF 57 / printed p.56

I am happy to inform you that, as of 31-12-97, the number of life members enrolled in this Traders' Welfare Board was 24,595. I ask that this figure of twenty-four thousand should soon rise to two lakh people, and I expect that it will.

I wish to tell you one thing. During the five-year period from 1991 to 1996, the amount paid by members of the Traders' Welfare Board was 35 lakh 48 thousand 575 rupees. One principle of the Traders' Welfare Board is that, when members pay an amount in this way, the government must contribute an equal amount. During those five years, the amount paid by members into the Traders' Welfare Board was 35 lakh 48 thousand 575 rupees. The matching amount from the government was never provided. Only after the Kazhagam government assumed office in 1996 were we informed that it had not been paid; and I am duty-bound to remind you that it was this government that provided the matching amount of 35 lakh 48 thousand 575 rupees which the previous government had failed to pay, forgotten to pay, or did not wish to pay.

We planned to make several important and significant changes in the Commercial Taxes Department, and we have made those changes. The ministers who spoke here have explained them in detail. I do not wish to repeat them

## PDF 58 / printed p.57

all again.

Many reforms were made in the system of sales-tax assessment. For that purpose, a Tax Reform Committee was constituted, with representatives of traders, members belonging to other parties in the Legislative Assembly, and members of the ruling party. This government has implemented, as far as possible, all the reforms they proposed. I do not wish to go into those details. Nor is there an occasion today for a long speech. You know the reason. All of you know well amid how many other tasks we have undertaken this work.

That is why I wish to tell you this: whatever your representation may be, this government has never failed to fulfil it. When you asked that there should be only a single-point tax on all goods, the beginning had already been made before 1976; later, in 1989, multi-point tax was removed on nearly sixty commodities and converted into single-point tax; and now it is this government that has created the rule of single-point tax for all commodities. You have experienced this in practice and praised it. Here today as well, you have expressed thanks for it.

For food items used daily by the people—pulses, chillies, coriander, turmeric, tamarind,

## PDF 59 / printed p.58

cumin, asafoetida, jaggery and `sukkai`—and also for silver toe-rings, anklets and waist-cords, hand-made matchsticks, paddy husk, broken rice and coconut husk, I granted complete tax exemption for 1996-97 in the financial statement that I placed before the House itself.

Likewise, among the goods on which sales tax was abolished in 1997-98 were fish feed, coconut shells and pieces, groundnut shells, curd, panchamirtham, educational charts, tourist guides and maps, heavy large sledgehammers, old silver toe-rings and anklets. The 11 per cent tax that had been in force on these goods was completely abolished. Not only that: bamboo screens, winnowing trays and baskets; thirty-nine categories comprising life-saving medicines for the treatment of cancer, leprosy and tuberculosis and indigenous medicines; solar cells; and footwear priced below fifty rupees were all made completely tax-free.

The 4 per cent tax that had been in force on cattle feed, leaf cups, sambrani, cycles for persons with disabilities, mineral earth used for making bricks and tiles, senna leaves, seed husks, leaves of the nilavagai plant, and tapioca `kappi` and `thippi` was completely abolished.

Minerals—metals, and gold sold to gold-jewellery exporters—

## PDF 60 / printed p.59

were subject to a 2 per cent sales tax; that tax has been completely abolished by this government.

When my younger brother Avadi Aruna spoke here, he mentioned that in 1993-94 the government's revenue through taxes was 3,397 crore rupees; in 1994-95 the revenue through these taxes was 4,164 crore rupees; and in 1995-96 the tax revenue was 5,023 crore rupees. You must think carefully about this. From 3,397 crore rupees in 1993-94, it rose by nearly a thousand crore rupees to 4,164 crore in 1994-95. In 1995-96 it rose by another roughly thousand crore rupees to 5,023 crore. But in 1996-97, because the Kazhagam government granted various tax concessions, it did not rise by another thousand crore. The 5,023 crore rupees rose only to 5,137 crore rupees. What does this show? It shows how many tax concessions this government has granted.

Rathinavelu said here that if tax concessions are given, revenue will increase. This is an example showing that it did not. Year after year the revenue had been rising by a thousand crore rupees. But because this government gave many tax concessions to traders, instead of rising by a thousand crore rupees it rose by only a hundred crore rupees. That means we have borne that degree of tax loss and revenue loss and granted concessions to the trading community. I wish to say this with pride,

## PDF 61 / printed p.60

Even if this loss has occurred, it does not matter. (loud applause) Your welfare is our welfare, and that is the welfare of the country. It is on that basis that this government has accepted such a loss and announced these concessions. I am duty-bound to point this out.

Not only that: it was this government that granted the concession allowing registration certificates to be renewed once in five years. Those engaged in trade had previously been required to register their businesses every year. Because the difficulties and official intimidation involved in that were pointed out, this government changed the procedure and ordered that they could renew their registrations once every five years. You will not have forgotten that.

Among the demands you have placed, one important demand concerns the Metropolitan Water Supply and Sewerage Board, which collects water tax from shops ranging from Rs.125 to Rs.345. You have requested that this be reviewed and reduced. I inform you that I shall discuss this with the Minister for Local Administration and the officials and that appropriate action will be taken. (applause)

The general public too are asking with distress why this election has come. They are asking anxiously whether an election is needed now. The same

## PDF 62 / printed p.61

feeling exists among you. You fixed the date for the conference even before the election came. But after the date was fixed, the election arrived. All your anguish is over why this election had to come along. Otherwise, you think, by now the Chief Minister would have accepted many of our demands while speaking at this Valluvar Kottam and would have given us answers accepting them all.

On fulfilling the demands during election time, Veerasamy said something here in a way that put me in a tight spot. He said, “Kalaignar is one who knew, during the Emergency, what had to be said and how it had to be said. Therefore he will tell us here how this should be said.” How is it to be said? Where there is a will, there is a way. (applause) The will is there. The way will become visible after the election. (loud applause) With that, I take leave of you.
