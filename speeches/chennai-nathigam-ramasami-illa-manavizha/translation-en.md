## Chennai - Nathigam Ramasami Family Wedding

With our warm good wishes, the wedding of Subash, the beloved son of our dear friend Nathigam Ramasami, and Malathi, the beloved daughter of Parameswaran, has been solemnized. When our dear friend Nathigam Ramasami welcomed all of us who have gathered at this function, it was not in the manner of a conventional welcome address at a wedding. As usual, he flung out the kind of words he writes in the *Nathigam* journal, poured forth impassioned expressions, and welcomed us in that fashion.

They say there should always be sweetness in a welcome address. But in the welcome he delivered, there was none of the sweetness of “vaa”; instead, we all noticed the severity of “vaa...r” (Applause). The reason is that it is the wave of a feeling that has been deeply embedded in the depths of his heart from those days until today. That is what all of us here have seen, heard and savoured.

## PDF 365 / printed p.364

As the Professor, Tamilkudimagan and Aladi Aruna have said here, from his youth until today he has remained steadfast in the principle he embraced. From childhood to this day, he has been a person who states what is in his mind without the slightest hesitation and without fear.

While speaking, he said that the Chief Minister had set aside so many duties today, accepted their invitation and come here. I would tell him, and all of you, that as far as our work is concerned, this work is our very first work. Only after this comes all our other work. Whether in those days when we were students learning from Father Periyar, or in this present period when, under the guidance of Arignar Anna, we entered the political field so that we might conduct ourselves according to a political path and continue to move within it today, we never forget that the work we first undertook was this very work.

If we forget that, all this other work would lose its meaning. Since this was the work we did at the beginning, as far as I am concerned, and indeed as far as our Professor is concerned too, we were all students of Father

## PDF 366 / printed p.365

Periyar, and during that period the platforms from which we mostly carried on the propaganda of our movement and explained Father Periyar's principles were wedding forums. Though those propaganda platforms, which in those days were wedding forums, later expanded into conferences and public meetings, that first work we did — work on wedding platforms — became at times the very platform that came to our aid in times of danger.

To be specific, when the Emergency had been proclaimed in India, there were prohibitions against holding meetings, conducting conferences and speaking. Whenever a prohibition is imposed — even now you can see this — if any government in any state imposes a Section 144 prohibition, there are certain exceptions. It will say, “except wedding functions and certain other religious ceremonies.” So, using that exception, during the Emergency all the propaganda we carried on was from these wedding platforms. Even there, loudspeakers were not permitted in many places. During the Emergency, in 1976, I spoke at wedding platforms in many places even with a megaphone held in my hand.

It is amid so much opposition, amid taunts and degrading speeches, that today we are continuing to build

## PDF 367 / printed p.366

this principle. The Professor pointed out here that Nathigam Ramasami has continued to retain the epithet “Nathigam” with his name. Whether it is that principle of atheism, the principle of Self-Respect, or any other principle, one must remain faithful to the principle one has accepted.

Today, in the spiritual sphere, there are devotees who accept God, worship God and have surrendered their hearts to God; at the same time, there are people in this country who disguise themselves as devotees and deceive that very God. Likewise, there are people who speak of rationalism. At the same time, there are also people who call themselves rationalists while engaging in activities that lie beyond rationalism.

Therefore, everyone is human. Whether they call themselves rationalists or devotees, what must be seen in both cases is whether they remain firm in the principle they have accepted. We must not forget that people who lack such firmness exist in both fields.

Father Periyar said that there is no God. Perarignar Anna formulated for the DMK its principle concerning God: “One community, one God.” When saying “One community, one God,”

## PDF 368 / printed p.367

the national poet — the emperor among poets, Subramania Bharathi — who had been a teacher to the Revolutionary Poet Bharathidasan, not Bharathidasan himself, counsels: “O ignorant ones who wander in search saying there are a thousand gods; will you not learn by reciting that knowledge alone is God?” Bharathi, who sang that knowledge is God, also said, “One must know for oneself that truth is God.”

Bharathi himself said that truth is God, and that to live in reverence of that truth is equivalent to worshipping God. It was that very idea that Perarignar Anna gave the DMK as a principle and philosophy in the words, “One community, one God.” That too is a saying of Thirumoolar. From then until today, this movement has grown through opposition, lived through opposition and kept swimming against the current. That is why our dear friend continues even today to say “Nathigam Ramasami” with firm emphasis.

We are carrying on this journey through immense hardship. Many of you may have heard of our elder brother Pattukkottai Azhagirisami, one of the leaders of this movement and the person who helped develop my ability to speak from a stage. Some may not know of him. In the agitation against Hindi domination, he undertook a march from Tiruchirappalli to Chennai. With him were Manavai Thirumalaisami, Moovalur Moothatti

## PDF 369 / printed p.368

Ramamirtham Ammaiyar and others, all taking part in that march. On the way, in one village, people had strung festoons made of footwear and even erected a welcome arch made of footwear, in an act intended to humiliate Pattukkottai Azhagirisami and the procession. When enraged cadres went to remove them, elder brother Azhagirisami told them that no one should take down the festoon. He placed a stool, climbed on it, stood there for an hour and explained the reasons and circumstances behind “Why I undertook this Tamilar Padai procession.” After that, it is history that the very people who had hung the footwear festoon took it down, gave a feast to those who had come with Azhagirisami, and sent them on their way to Chennai. (Applause) That is the history of this movement.

In 1957, when Perarignar Anna contested the election, a long board was put up right at the entrance to his house in Kanchipuram. On it they wrote about Anna in as degrading and insulting a manner as they possibly could. Party comrades became angry. Anna told them that no one should get angry. After it became dark, after 6 p.m., he asked for a Petromax light to be brought, had it hung beside the board, and wrote, “Lamp

## PDF 370 / printed p.369

courtesy: Annadurai.” He wrote that so the board would not become invisible to everyone at night. We are people who swam against the current in that way.

You may have seen a statue of Periyar near a bridge in Cuddalore. I myself unveiled it when I was Chief Minister in the past. That place has a history. Once, when Periyar was passing through that area in a procession and reached the vicinity of that bridge, footwear was thrown at him. Everyone wanted the statue to be placed at that very spot, in memory of the place where footwear had been thrown. Periyar himself proudly took part in the unveiling of that statue and spoke about it.

The reason I recount all this is that this movement has grown by enduring abuse, insults, humiliation and vilification. We have gone to prison as well. It is only after crossing such torments and all those attacks in which people spoke degradingly about our community, our families, our relatives and even our personal way of life that this movement has grown to what it is today.

For that kind of growth, as our Aruna pointed out — even though he may not have worked from a place in the limelight,

## PDF 371 / printed p.370

my dear brother Nathigam Ramasami too has contributed his labour and rendered service. He has given his thoughts and writings to this movement and helped it grow. It is therefore as a debt of gratitude to him — to appreciate that service, and to share in that honour — that we have taken part in this wedding and offer our wishes to the newlyweds Subash and Malathi.
