## Bharati Festival

Avvai T. K. Shanmugam, who used to lead the annual Bharati festival with great enthusiasm, survives this year only in his portrait. The true way to honour the dead, however, is to continue the work they left behind. I am pleased that the Bharati Sangam is doing that and is keeping Bharati's stirring poetry before the public.

Mahalingam mentioned that Bharati's centenary will fall in 1982 and invited me to participate. Whatever position I may occupy then, I will not forget the occasion.

## PDF 191 / printed p.190

Silambu Chelvar and I have been meeting almost daily at public functions. I joked that we keep giving concerts with the same accompanying artists, yet audiences continue to listen because what binds speakers and listeners is not novelty alone but a shared inner feeling.

Among those who can express Bharati's songs with the emotion in which they were written, Silambu Chelvar holds a distinguished place.

## PDF 192 / printed p.191

I recall visiting Bharati's birthplace at Ettayapuram after the Tamil Nadu Government purchased the house and turned it into a historical memorial. His writings and belongings were preserved there.

Many people can sing or speak of revolution without being revolutionaries. Bharati was different: he wrote revolutionary poetry and also stood in the field himself.

A poet such as Jayamkondar could create martial excitement through verse without personally entering battle. Bharati joined literary fire to lived courage.

## PDF 193 / printed p.192

He did not merely hand songs to others and tell them to march; he himself possessed the clarity to enter the struggle.

Some who initially refused to value his songs later praised them. That change demonstrates Bharati's strength.

His greatness also lies in the succession he created. Bharathidasan, born Kanakasubburathinam, consciously made himself Bharati's heir and praised him in memorable Tamil.

## PDF 194 / printed p.193

Later poets continued Bharati's style in language ordinary people could understand. Like the aerial roots that give a banyan its vastness, this poetic lineage enlarged Bharati's fame.

In *Panchali Sabatham*, Bharati gives Panchali a striking question: after Dharman had already gambled away himself, what right remained to wager her? It is an example of Bharati placing a revolutionary thought into the epic narrative.

## PDF 195 / printed p.194

Bharati's writing carried speed, courage and feeling, and his life also contained humour.

I recount an incident at a religious discourse in Puducherry. When devotees repeatedly answered a chant with “Govinda, Govinda,” Bharati whispered to a man nearby. At the next response, the man stood and said, “I am right here—did you call me?” His name was Govindan. It was one of Bharati's mischievous jokes.

## PDF 196 / printed p.195

Thus Bharati was a master of humour, fine Tamil, heroic passion and the yearning for freedom. In nine years we will celebrate his centenary.

Silambu Chelvar reminded us that those who sacrificed for national freedom must remain in public memory. He also suggested naming a street after Avvai Shanmugam. I will place that recommendation before the municipal council rather than treat it as an executive order.

He also raised the memory of Vanchinathan and the problem of honouring him in the name of Maniyachi railway station.

## PDF 197 / printed p.196

The Tamil Nadu Government had asked that Maniyachi station, where Vanchinathan shot Ashe and then himself, be renamed Veera Vanchi Junction. The reply treated the request as regional patriotism.

I regretted that, instead of supporting the proposal across party lines, some opposed it merely because this Government proposed it. Others suggested that the State simply rename the town itself.

## PDF 198 / printed p.197

But even renaming an existing town requires Central approval because postal and other Central systems are involved. The State cannot unilaterally change such names.

I present this too as another example in the argument for State autonomy.

On matters of this kind, parties should rise above partisanship and work together for worthwhile public purposes. Let that be a pledge taken at Bharati's festival.
