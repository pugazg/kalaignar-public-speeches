#### School Life

School life! A rich part of human life! A part that enriches human life and gives it form!

The human being is born into the world, grows, becomes accustomed to life, passes through its stages, and when childhood begins, school life too begins!

The good fortune of entering school life—yes! This must indeed be called a blessing, a piece of good fortune—is available in this country to only a very few: only a very few have the means, the opportunity, the standard of living, and the circumstances that make such a life possible.

### PDF page 7 — printed page 6

To enter school life, study many lessons, learn to read and come to know many books, plunge into the sea of poetry and the realm of epics, examine and understand many fields such as science, history, and geography, and emerge as a learned man, a college man, a poet, a story writer, an essayist, an upright man who instructs the town—the human children who receive this blessing, this good fortune, are very, very few! Extremely few! Why?

Many live, but those who study, those who are educated, are very few. Why? What is the reason?

Is it because studying is thought to be a sin? Is there some barrier to studying? What?

Why are the educated few? Only here? Only in this country!

Is this not the very land in which Saraswati Devi, the goddess of learning, is said to dwell? Is this not the land in which the proverb, “Kamban is the greatest in learning,” is current? Why, then, is there a famine of education here? Why are there so many people who never enter school life? What is the reason?

Is there a lack of regard for education? Are the people who live here devoid of the desire to honour education? Have they little respect for the learned?

### PDF page 8 — printed page 7

Or are they people whose very nature holds that studying is a sin? No, no. Then what is the reason for this condition of being unlettered?

If we look only superficially at **school life**, we cannot find an answer to this; nor will one be found!

School—a schoolhouse, a place to study, a life with the means to go there; at the age when one should go to school, not being born into a family line compelled by circumstances to do some other work—only those born into a prosperous life, comfortable circumstances, and a wealthy family line (apart from a few, and even those are very, very few) begin school life, begin it in this country!

The others, with the thought that “life itself is illusion; becoming dust is certain,” live as though saying, “Somehow we were born, somehow we lived,” with no hold upon life, struggling and circling through a barren existence, spending their whole lives as walking corpses!

Without flavour in life, without even a trace of the principle that life is meant to be lived, most of the people of this country lead an insipid existence with the weariness of merely saying, “Somehow, we are living.”

### PDF page 9 — printed page 8

Therefore, before we begin to speak about school life, we are under the necessity of knowing and properly understanding life, the standard of life, and the purpose of life!

Life! Human life in particular is different from other forms of life—entirely different, indeed!

All manner of things are born in the world, and they live too! Bird species such as quail, *kauthari*, myna, pigeon, parrot, eagle, and owl; animal kinds such as tiger, lion, bear, elephant, deer, rabbit, pig, dog, fox, cat, rat, and others; creatures living in water such as crocodiles, fish, frogs, and crabs; species such as snakes, snails, and tortoises that live both in water and on land; and so many other kinds—all these live.

Distinct and different from such other species, the human race has continued to live by subduing and controlling all other living creatures, changing and adapting them for its own use!

The difference, the distinction, between other species and living beings and the human being is immense, very, very great. Compared with other living beings, the human being is—

### PDF page 10 — printed page 9

—superior, a species possessing an additional faculty of knowledge—rational discernment.

Compared with other living beings, humanity possesses a higher quality: the capacity to distinguish and reason—to tell good from evil, what is needed from what is not needed, what is wanted from what is unwanted; to know that taking this path will bring danger, while taking that path will not bring suffering but will bring happiness; to examine and understand circumstances and surroundings. Humanity acquired rational discernment!

The human race is born with rational discernment everywhere! Once born, human beings shape and conduct their lives in many different ways and methods according to the surroundings, climate, and other conditions of the places into which they are born. Not only that: they change their surroundings and circumstances and advance. Using rational discernment, they even alter climatic conditions and arrange them in ways useful and favourable to themselves!
