### Veeran Sundaralingam Memorial Village Opening

A function that brings delight to the heart is taking place here with great dignity, distinction, fervour, and uplift. After 1972, I came to this region with Manali Kandasamy and Suppu and conducted the Veerapandiya Kattabomman festival. Accepting the demands placed then, I gave an assurance that I would rebuild Veerapandiya Kattabomman's Panchalankurichi fort, which had been lost, destroyed, and buried under the earth. Fulfilling that assurance, I built the fort and its bastions, and erected memorials for all the commanders who stood shoulder to shoulder with Veerapandiya Kattabomman and took part in the freedom struggle. As one of those memorials, it was I who even then erected the ceremonial gateway for Veeran Sundaralingam. In that capacity, I am very happy to take part in this event.

Dr Krishnasamy, our Member of the Legislative Assembly who has taken the leading place here, spoke about all the occasions on which I have remembered

## PDF 104 / printed p.103

Sundaralingam. He appears to have brought notes taken from a book I wrote; I too have brought notes on exactly the same passages. From this alone you can understand the unity between us. If I remind you that fourteen years ago Krishnasamy stood in the Pollachi parliamentary election on behalf of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, all of you can understand how this unity of thought came about.

About fifteen years ago, in the magazine “Kungumam,” I created a serialized illustrated narrative called “Paayum Puli Pandaraka Vanniyan.” The very first chapter of that serial was a chapter introducing Veeran Sundaralingam. Many of you may not have been able to read it. But as one who recalled him as long as fifteen years ago, I now remind you of it. That is what Krishnasamy also reminded you of here. I had written it there. While Krishnasamy was reading it, you became somewhat restless and, carried away by joy, forgot yourselves and raised slogans. Because of that, many among you did not listen carefully to what I had written and what he had read. Therefore, I shall read it myself:

“Sundaralingam, the close friend of Veerapandiya Kattabomman's beloved commander Vellaiyathevan and a warrior equal to him, was one of the lions on the battlefront of Panchalang-

## PDF 105 / printed p.104

kurichi. Born in an oppressed community, Sundaralingam was a great broad-shouldered warrior who rose in defiance so that foreigners should not occupy this Tamil soil.”

This appears on page 13 of the book “Paayum Puli Pandaraka Vanniyan.”

I say further: “Vellaiyathevan, born in the Devar clan, and this Sundaralinga Kudumban, born in the Devendra clan, lacked only one thing—that they were not born together; otherwise the two were inseparable brothers.”

I have written this too on page 36 of the book “Paayum Puli Pandaraka Vanniyan.”

I have also written on page 17 of that book: “This Sundaralinga Kudumban came in the lineage of Vettum Perumal, who fought heroically for the rights of the Pandya land.”

Veeran Sundaralingam was a commander and a pioneer in the freedom struggle against the British that took place two hundred years ago. The British described his native village as “the Gibraltar of the South.” Sundaralingam was one among those who possessed such distinction. Historical records continue to point out that he served as the commander of the intelligence corps of Panchalankurichi king Veerapandiya Kattabomman.

In those days the government gazette recorded the name of this village as “Kevunagiri.” How did it become Kevunagiri? I

## PDF 106 / printed p.105

do not look at that with surprise. Many names have undergone changes of language and changes of name in this way. Because the British Governor came and stayed here frequently, after some years it gradually became “Governagiri,” and later, in colloquial speech, it became “Kevunagiri.” I will give another example. In Chennai city, in the Mount area, there was a bridge named after a British man. During British rule, that bridge was called Hamilton Bridge. Little by little the name of that bridge wore down and finally became “Ambattan Bridge”—I ask pardon of those belonging to that community. I say this only to show the harshness of how that change occurred. It became “Ambattan Bridge,” and then, as though giving it a proper English translation, they translated it into English and made it “Barber Bridge.”

The name of the British gentleman Hamilton thus finally changed to the extent of becoming “Barber Bridge.” In the same way, because the Governor stayed here frequently, this was “Governagiri,” and in the course of time became “Kevunagiri.” Sundaralingam was the son of the couple Kattakaruppan and Muthu of this Governagiri. History says that because, even at a young age, he was a fertile field of valour and a man capable of completing whatever task he undertook, Kattabomman appointed Sundaralingam as the commander of his intelligence force.

## PDF 107 / printed p.106

British domination was gradually spreading. The British officers had decided to encircle Kattabomman's valour, his fort, and—as he had pointed out—the people of this region, and to seize the fort itself, and they were making preparations for that. The British possessed weapons and cannons that Kattabomman did not have. At the stage when they had reached a final decision to surround Kattabomman's fort and capture it, Vellaiyathevan had gone out of town for a wedding. Then the news reached Sundaralingam that the British had laid siege to the fort and were going to split it apart layer by layer.

Sundaralingam had a “muraippen,” a customary marriage-relative, named Vadivu. The two planned how to defeat the British scheme. Both disguised themselves as goat-herders. The British had stored explosives with which they planned to destroy Kattabomman's fort. Sundaralingam and his marriage-relative Vadivu reasoned that if those explosives could be rendered useless, the fort would be saved. They made their plan, went inside the explosives depot carrying a firebrand, caused all the explosives stored there to explode and be destroyed, and they too

## PDF 108 / printed p.107

became as explosives within those explosives and disappeared. Sundaralingam and that marriage-relative Vadivu became flames of sacrifice. Thus, offering themselves as prey to the fire of the explosives—even then, what today would be called a suicide squad, a suicide squad—Sundaralingam stood, under the leadership of Veerapandiya Kattabomman, as the commander of a suicide squad against the British. When we think of that, we feel proud.

But such suicide squads, all these acts of heroic daring, were necessary in those days. When the British came with cannons, when their army advanced, all these things were necessary to resist them. But with the change of times, to oppose the British, the noble Gandhiji took non-violence itself as his weapon. Taking non-violence itself as his weapon, he mobilized the people of India from Kumari to the Himalayan realm, and it was because of that freedom struggle under his leadership that the British left.

Even though the British left, and even though the country obtained independence, it was the soil that received freedom; the people did not receive complete freedom. We see that even today. It was to obtain that freedom that leaders such as Thanthai Periyar, Perarignar Anna, and Annal Ambedkar laboured. Even today we continue to labour in their path.

While we labour in that way, no one need think too highly of the 200 houses that have been built here today. But today, if you go to any district or any constituency in Tamil Nadu and find 200 or 300 similar houses, the reason for that—I do not say this boastfully; I do not say it with pride—is that, as Minister Ko. Si. Mani pointed out here, the scheme to build and provide free concrete houses for the Adi Dravidar people was first created, and first implemented in India, by Karunanidhi who is standing before you. I remind you of that.

Once, at an opening function for houses of this kind, Ammaiyar Sathyavani Muthu was then the minister for the Harijan Department. In those days that department was called the Harijan Welfare Department, and she was its minister. When 100 such houses had been built in one place and were being opened, she made a point. She too belonged to the Adi Dravidar community. Even so, she said with concern, “You have built all these houses. Will our people remain grateful?”

When I spoke then, I said: “Amma, I am a follower of Periyar. Do you know Periyar's golden saying? Do not perform your duty expect-

Therefore, whether they are backward people, most backward people, oppressed people, or people who today remain at the bottom, they are still pushed into darkness in circumstances in which they are unable to understand many things. We must light a lamp for all of them. We must bring light to all their hearts. We must create in them an awareness of who we are. When we create that awareness, we must create the feeling that all are one community and all are one people. Whether we identify some as a dominant community or identify others as people at the bottom, both sides, both sections, must come together as one, shoulder to shoulder and hand in hand, and live as comrades and as brothers.

## PDF 111 / printed p.110

What does this history tell us? What does Sundaralingam's history tell us? Vellaiyathevan and Sundaralingam were great warriors who, in the same army, clasped hands, put their arms over one another's shoulders, and led the fighting. Why has that condition changed today? The purpose is nothing other than to carry this changed condition through historical evidence and bring back that old condition once again.

In particular, reminding people of the old warriors and the martyrs of earlier times is one of our greatest responsibilities. When Krishnasamy spoke here, he said, “In the fifty years since independence, did anyone remember all these people?” When the man who asked whether anyone remembered them said that, I thought he would point to me and say, “This one man alone remembered them.” I understand that he left it without pointing me out because you would know it without being told.

Because at one time I was his leader. I have called him “younger brother” many times. Even today I am bound to call him that. It was when I was his leader that he stood as the DMK candidate in the Pollachi parliamentary constituency. I too had the opportunity to speak at many meetings and canvass votes for him. Therefore, if he spoke here with fury, if he roared like a storm, if he roared like a lion, I

Just as a mother, when her son wins fame on the battlefield, touches her own belly, I too touch my belly. I rejoice to the extent of thinking, “We have borne such a son.” In this happiness I too share a part with all of you.
