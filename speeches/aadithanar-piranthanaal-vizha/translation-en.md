# Adithanar Birthday Function

Karunanidhi begins by thanking Sivanthi Adithan, Governor Fathima Beevi and the organisers for presenting him with the **“Muthirai”** award at the birthday celebration of Adithanar. He also receives a purse of one lakh rupees. Recalling an earlier one-lakh-rupee award he received from Tamil University for his historical novel **“Thenpandi Singam,”** he explains that he had returned that money to the university to endow lectures in the names of Tamil scholars.

He contrasts that earlier situation with the present one. That morning, Ramachandra Adithan had contributed one lakh rupees to the government's poor-women marriage-assistance scheme named after Moovalur Ramamirtham. Karunanidhi says the one lakh rupees presented to him at the evening function will likewise be transferred to the Chief Minister's Relief Fund for that marriage-assistance scheme. He jokes that this does not mean Sivanthi's quota of giving has been exhausted.

Karunanidhi then remembers Adithanar as a teacher of the alphabet to ordinary people who had not even learned basic letters. Through `தினத்தந்தி`, he says, Adithanar used simple news writing to raise people who were intellectually “short” into “tall” people. He recalls Adithanar's slogan **“உடல் மண்ணுக்கு உயிர் தமிழுக்கு”** and his **“நாம் தமிழர்”** movement.

Karunanidhi recounts that at a 1958 organisational meeting in Chennai, Adithanar declared the Naam Tamilar movement to be a forward force for the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam. During a later flight to Tiruchirappalli, Adithanar pressed Karunanidhi, then the party treasurer, on the need to raise lawful funds for electoral work. Karunanidhi says this conversation helped spur his campaign to raise ten lakh rupees for the 1967 election; at the Virugambakkam state conference he ultimately handed Annadurai eleven lakh rupees.

He recalls Adithanar's political roles after the DMK came to power: Speaker of the Legislative Assembly under Annadurai and later Minister for Co-operation in Karunanidhi's cabinet. He also remembers Adithanar's agitations on behalf of palmyra workers in Tirunelveli and farmers in Chengalpattu, for which he was arrested and imprisoned.

Karunanidhi identifies another bond between them: during the 1965 anti-Hindi agitation, Adithanar was held in solitary confinement in Coimbatore while Karunanidhi was held in solitary confinement at Palayamkottai. He says they were the only two people in Tamil Nadu then detained in solitary confinement under the security law. After their release, they discussed constructive ideas for strengthening the DMK and conveyed them to Annadurai.

He credits Adithanar with revolutionising journalism. Repeating a formulation attributed to him, Karunanidhi says a dog biting a man is not news, but a man biting a dog is news. Though `தினத்தந்தி` became Adithanar's great success, an earlier paper, `தமிழன்`, had failed. He notes that Adithanar had even run that publication using handmade paper.

Karunanidhi then praises the newspaper family that continued Adithanar's legacy through Ramachandra Adithan, Sivanthi Adithan and succeeding generations. He names `தினத்தந்தி`, `மாலைமுரசு`, `மாலை மலர்`, `கதிரவன்` and `தினகரன்` as journalistic instruments arising from that family for sustaining Tamil and Tamil identity.

Turning to the speakers who praised him, Karunanidhi jokes about Justice M. M. Ismail's verdict that he is a **“முத்திரிஞர்”**, asking whether that is a judgment he can accept or appeal. He insists he is not a master of Tamil scholarship or grammar. Recalling his first chairing of a poetry gathering at Tiruchirappalli radio, he says he had openly admitted that he did not command the rules of **தளை, சீர், யாப்பு, தொடை**, and had written from emotion instead.

He recalls lines from that occasion: because he was singing about those who broke bonds, even his own verse might break metrical bonds; and even if metre were absent, what must never disappear was protection for the country and the language. He says this was his way of acknowledging that he had not formally mastered literary grammar.

Karunanidhi thanks former Tamil University vice-chancellor C. Balasubramaniam for nonetheless certifying him as a person of learning, and praises V. C. Kulandaisamy, another distinguished vice-chancellor, who through his speech had virtually elevated a man who never attended college into a university professor.

He then thanks poet Vairamuthu for repeatedly calling him his Tamil teacher. Karunanidhi says that when someone of Vairamuthu's poetic ability, learning, imagination and new thinking calls him “teacher,” it raises his standing among Tamil people and scholars. Whether or not Vairamuthu was literally his student, Karunanidhi says he is grateful for being treated as one in spirit.

The speakers, he says, associated the award with his attachment to language, his political positions, his identity as Annadurai's younger brother and Periyar's follower, his uncompromising rationalism, social justice, state autonomy, and the goal of making Tamil one of India's official languages.

He warmly praises Governor Fathima Beevi as a special opportunity for Tamil Nadu at a time when women's advancement and rights were being asserted: she was a woman governor, from a minority community, and a former judge.

Karunanidhi says Sivanthi Adithan deserves credit for bringing together the occasion. He then recalls that when he was born in 1924, thieves entered his home and stole even his mother's marriage-chain. Because the infant Karunanidhi did not cry, he says humorously, the thieves did not crush his throat to silence him. He jokes that he therefore owes his later life — its joys, sorrows and service to language and country — partly to the thieves' restraint.

He congratulates novelist Prabanjan, who is receiving an award alongside him, and thanks Sivanthi for honouring Prabanjan as well.

In the closing passage, Karunanidhi says he wishes to live many more years, not to remain in office or enjoy authority, but to continue serving Tamil until his eyes finally close. He invokes the image of carrying Annadurai's heart and one day returning it to Anna, and says he wishes to live long enough to perform more service and become worthy of the title **“முத்திரிஞர்”** bestowed on him.
