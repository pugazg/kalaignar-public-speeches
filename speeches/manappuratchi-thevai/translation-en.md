# A Revolution of the Mind Is Needed — English translation

Serving as moderator of a debate on whether social change comes through law or through a revolution of the mind, Karunanidhi argues that the two are interdependent.

He traces law to social development: reform laws become effective only when public attitudes have been prepared for them, citing temple entry, family planning and land-ceiling reform. At the same time, some basic prohibitions—murder, theft and similar harms—do not require lengthy public persuasion because moral disapproval is already deeply rooted.

He discusses Tamil worship in temples and Self-Respect marriages as examples in which sustained social reform preceded legal recognition. Using Anna's image that “law is a dark room; the lawyer's argument is a lamp,” he reframes law as the structure within which enlightened public understanding supplies light.

His verdict is that a revolution in thought creates and reshapes law, while law consolidates and secures social change. Therefore society needs both continuing mental transformation and legislation suited to changing conditions.

## Separate source closing note

Moderator's verdict delivered at Rajaji Hall on 5 September 1971.
