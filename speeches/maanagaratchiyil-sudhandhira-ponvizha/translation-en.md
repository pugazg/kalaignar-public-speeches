# Independence Golden Jubilee at the Municipal Corporation

Karunanidhi speaks at the Chennai Corporation's Independence Golden Jubilee function, which combines several commemorative activities: honouring elders and guides, laying the foundation for a Golden Jubilee memorial pillar, and inaugurating the Anna Renaissance Scheme.

He opens with humour about the ruling-side leader Malaiyan and opposition leader Vetrivel, playing on the images of a mountain and a spear. The larger point is that the Corporation is functioning with members from different parties under Mayor M. K. Stalin, supported by the Deputy Mayor, without treating party difference as an obstacle to civic work.

Karunanidhi expresses particular gratitude for the praise offered by veteran freedom fighter D. Chengalvarayan. He says that praise from such a figure is like receiving a golden medal from the freedom fighters themselves.

The speech then presents Tamil Nadu as a pioneer in several national developments. Karunanidhi recalls Chennai as a place where Gandhi and Rajaji discussed the freedom movement, identifies the Vellore uprising as an early freedom struggle, and notes Gandhi's encounter with poverty in Madurai. He again invokes Thillaiyadi Valliammai, Nagappan and Narayanasamy and their role alongside Gandhi in the South African anti-racial struggle.

He next turns to public welfare. Sir P. T. Theagaraya's introduction of a midday meal for schoolchildren in the Chennai Corporation is described as an early model later expanded under Kamaraj. Karunanidhi also refers to M. G. Ramachandran's nutritious-meal scheme and his own government's later addition of eggs, presenting these as stages in the development of school nutrition policy.

A similar argument is made about free concrete housing for Adi-Dravidar families. Karunanidhi recalls a 1974 Tamil Nadu scheme, its interruption after the dismissal of his government, its revival in 1989-90, and its later adoption nationally under the name Indira Gandhi Memorial Housing Scheme. He stresses that both State and Union expenditure ultimately comes from the people's tax money.

The speech then moves to local democracy. Karunanidhi notes that municipal elections had returned after roughly twenty-five years and says this fulfilled an election promise. He criticises the Panchayati Raj structure for creating disconnected tiers and contrasts it with Kamaraj's simpler local-government framework. His emphasis is on decentralising authority widely to village panchayats rather than concentrating it in a smaller number of district bodies.

He says power-sharing must be genuine, but also warns that decentralisation can be undermined by corruption. He recounts cases involving panchayat office-bearers accused of misusing cheque-signing authority to purchase household items and another local official accused of misappropriating a much larger amount. His message is that wrongdoing must be punished regardless of party affiliation.

Karunanidhi reinforces this by recalling his own 1974 decision to dissolve the Chennai Corporation after corruption allegations were raised in the legislature, even though some of those affected were his own political associates.

Near the close, he responds humorously to Chengalvarayan's remark about his name: `கருணை` has three letters and `நிதி` two, so compassion is greater than wealth. But, he adds, when wrongdoing is involved, `கருணை` can turn into `கடுமை` — compassion into firmness.

He concludes by congratulating the Chennai Corporation, Mayor, Deputy Mayor and councillors as the Anna Renaissance Scheme is inaugurated, and links honest local administration with the building of a new society.
