## Let the Well-Off Give Way!

Chancellor, Pro-Chancellors, Vice-Chancellors, respected professors, and dear friends receiving your degrees!

I stand before you in Madurai—the land of the Pandyan kings, celebrated in Tamil memory and in the climax of the Kannagi story in *Silappathikaram*. I did not have the privilege of a college education, but I stand here with confidence born of having inherited at least a little of the wealth of ancient Tamil. This is a land through which poets walked and from which Tamil's treasures were mined.

## PDF 147 / printed p.146

First accept my greetings; then take up the many responsibilities awaiting you and raise the flag of our success in every direction.

I have recently travelled through a small part of the world. Wherever I went, I found that the fame of Tamil had arrived before me. Scholars praised its musical quality and its sweetness. Even in Paris I heard Tamil spoken of as a distinguished classical language.

Yet that pride brought me pain too. I saw magnificent buildings abroad and asked myself when the children of Tamil Mother would enjoy such fullness of life. The poverty in which so many Tamils live prevented me from looking at those towers without tears.

## PDF 148 / printed p.147

Can the sweetness of life enjoyed abroad be created here by the effort of one person? Individual labour, sacrifice, ability and knowledge are not enough by themselves. Philosophies, doctrines and advice are cheap in the world market.

An individual must see himself as part of society, and society must understand that it is made of individuals. Clarity must be joined with courage and collective action.

When we call for labour, we do not mean that the already exhausted worker must simply labour still more. The duty of society is to work so that the crushing burden on that worker is reduced. That cannot be done by isolated individual effort; the whole country must become prosperous.

## PDF 149 / printed p.148

Everyone must therefore labour on different fronts. When labour is shared widely, the old belief that only a few are born to toil will disappear, and working people too will gain time for rest and recreation.

Graduates may ask: “We have studied for years, our parents have spent their resources, and now you speak to us of labour and sacrifice. Where are the jobs for which we qualified?”

I understand that question.

Twenty-three years have passed since independence. Three plans were implemented; the Fourth Plan was delayed and has only recently gained life. During the period without planning,

## PDF 150 / printed p.149

unemployment spread like fire and sowed poverty. The Government of India must confront this gigantic demon of unemployment. I am not asking you merely to wait patiently; at the State level we are framing practical schemes to reduce unemployment. Graduates should therefore retain hope.

Higher education should not mean only the immediate securing of a post. Education must enlarge knowledge and ability. Yet I do not deny the equally real question: must we not feed ourselves and our families? That is why I say State efforts must create avenues of employment.

For students from wealthy homes, education need not be only a means of subsistence. Their knowledge should be used to build industry and to undertake social and public work. If the affluent compete for every office post, poorer graduates who depend on such employment are pushed into deeper hardship.

## PDF 151 / printed p.150

I therefore ask graduates who possess financial security to cooperate with Government by making way for poorer graduates where ordinary salaried employment is concerned.

This alone cannot solve the whole problem. But even a limited measure matters when families have sacrificed to educate a son or daughter in the hope that education will rescue them from poverty. Within its powers, the State Government is trying to enlarge employment.

Those from prosperous families have many other fields before them: service to Tamil, science, research, industry and public work. Lighting a lamp of Tamil feeling in minds that have not yet awakened to it is itself a great task.

## PDF 152 / printed p.151

West Germany, once defeated and prostrate, rose rapidly through industrial growth. People who possess knowledge and capital can similarly join the Government in developing industry and turn perishable wealth into enduring wealth.

The unfinished work of social reform also remains. Bharati sang against caste; Bharathidasan sang of sweet Tamil. Their words should not remain on paper. Tamil must become not only a language of song but a language of study and instruction.

At this point the speech cites Gandhi's insistence that attachment to one's mother tongue should resemble attachment to mother's milk, and that replacing the mother tongue with another language as the medium of education damages children's full development and separates them from their home environment.

## PDF 153 / printed p.152

The Gandhi quotation continues by urging immediate change so that State languages obtain their rightful place in education, even if temporary confusion results.

Anna too had stressed that no qualification can substitute for the qualification of being one's mother tongue. English is indispensable for access to the world's knowledge and may serve as a link language within India and internationally, but that does not remove the necessity of Tamil as the medium of instruction.

I have seen countries that teach scientific and medical terminology through their mother tongues and prosper. Tamil must be developed not merely as a literary vehicle of *akam* and *puram*, but as a modern language capable of explaining the secrets of the atom

## PDF 154 / printed p.153

and the journeys of the moon.

Must every graduate turn only toward the direction in which a job may be found? Among you may be future thinkers, poets, reformers and public leaders. Even if none equals the great figures of history, I see before me people capable of continuing their work.

I ask you for strength and loving cooperation so that the country may prosper. Just as we ask the sun for light, the moon for coolness, the jasmine for fragrance and the breeze for sweetness, I ask you for the service that is yours to give.

The speech closes by recalling the old Tamil teaching that learning is good even when gained through labour and material sacrifice. You are the shade-giving trees growing in the soil of Nedunchezhiyan. Tamil Nadu asks you for shade. Go forth with determination, remove injustice wherever you find it, and grow into trees that give shelter.
