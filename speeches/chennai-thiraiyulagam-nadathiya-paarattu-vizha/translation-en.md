## Chennai - Film Industry Appreciation Function

My heartfelt thanks, first of all, to the distinguished people of the art world — brother E.V.M. Saravanan, K.R.G., Chintamani Murugesan, the affectionate friends who are part of the organising committee, and brother Kaviyarasu Vairamuthu, who stood with them and cooperated.

While speaking, Sivaji asked, when there are many elders, why did you invite me to preside over this function? There may be elders. But there is only one person suited to preside over a function of the art world. (Loud applause) That is why Sivaji has presided over this function.

Director Sikaram Balachander, who delivered the welcome address, and Bharathiraja, who offered felicitations, both referred to the various obstacles that arose in between. They pointed out that this function is taking place after many obstacles.

## PDF 332 / printed p.331

I wish to recall one thing. I am speaking of a time when Arignar Anna was alive. In one agitation I was imprisoned, and I came out of Tiruchi prison. Perarignar Anna waited at the gate of Tiruchi prison to welcome me. That day there was an appreciation meeting for me in Tiruchi. The next day, there was an appreciation meeting in Chennai under Anna's leadership.

Anna said, “After you speak in Tiruchi, come to Kanchipuram tomorrow. There will be a meal for you at home. The two of us can go together to the Chennai appreciation function.” Accordingly, after taking part in the Tiruchi function, I went to Kanchipuram the next morning, had the midday meal at Anna's house, and in the evening the two of us set out for the function in Chennai. On the way, a procession set out to welcome us from Anbagam, beside the present Anna Arivalayam. Anna and I were seated in a chariot, and the procession went to the beach. A grand function took place on the beach. Anna spoke in appreciation. At ten that night, Anna and I returned home.

Then my mother, Anjugam Ammayar, welcomed us and seated us in the hall. Looking at my mother, Anna asked, “How are you, Amma?” My mother then looked at Anna and said, “My younger brother, yesterday you were released from Vellore prison—

## PDF 333 / printed p.332

After being released, you came to Kanchipuram and saw your mother. Yes! After seeing your mother, you went back again to Vellore and spoke at the meeting, did you not?” “Yes, Amma,” Anna said. “It seems your mother suffered for one day to give birth to you. I suffered for 26 days to give birth to my son. That is why my son, after being released in Tiruchi and speaking at the Tiruchi meeting, came to Kanchipuram, came to Chennai, and without even coming home, spoke with you at the beach meeting, and has only now arrived,” she said. Anna then laughed heartily.

Why do I say this? Even for me to be born there were 26 days of suffering. Birth itself was a struggle. I am one who came out after waging a struggle even to be born. If I am to take part in a function, how many obstacles must I cross to get there? It is because life itself has taken the form of a struggle that I pointed to this incident.

They have organised this function and presented me with many things — a silver likeness, a gold chain, a watch, and so on. On many stages, in many places, such gold chains have been placed on me. I have never spoken on a stage while wearing them. But my dearest friend's command is that I must not remove it now. Sitting beside me, he would not allow me to take it off—

## PDF 334 / printed p.333

He kept watch. That is why I am standing before you wearing it.

As far as the distinguished people of the art world are concerned — whether it is brother Super Star Rajini seated here, Supreme Star brother Sarathkumar, Kalaignani brother Kamal, whom I have praised, Ezhuchikkalaignar brother Vijayakanth, or many other artists like them — I have not accomplished anything particularly great for any of them. Rather, because I am one who grew along with them from '45-'46; because I was involved in the art world even at a time when many of them had not been born, and even when many of them were children; because my 75th year is now under way; and because, as far as the film world and the art world are concerned, I have crossed 50 years, they are conducting both this golden-jubilee and pavala-vizha celebration here. I am the one indebted to their fine hearts, broad hearts and generous hearts; none of them is indebted to me.

As far as the film world is concerned, there has been great growth, to an extent that gives me happiness today. Whether that industry itself has experienced such growth is a matter for debate. All the difficulties it has faced—

## PDF 335 / printed p.334

They have brought them to me, and although I have accepted the responsibility of providing whatever assistance a government can provide, it cannot be said that the Tamil film world has today come completely into the light and that darkness has disappeared completely. Many reasons could be given. I do not wish to explain all of them here; it is already late.

I wish to state one thing with happiness. What gives me that happiness is that through this function the art world is standing united. (Loud applause) To those who expected, “Will differences not arise in the art world? Will the differences that arose not remain immortal as they are?”, I take great pride in seeing all of you together on this stage as an answer. More than the silver statue you gave me, more than all the gifts you presented, more than all the appreciation and felicitations you offered, it is the sight of all of you seated together in unity on this stage that cools my eyes. My heart rejoices. I want this sight to continue.

Balu Mahendra, who spoke here, expressed one thought. He said that film art should be made a subject in school lessons. Though he said cinema should be made a subject—

## PDF 336 / printed p.335

what that means is that film art should be made a subject. The Education Minister, the Professor, is here; regarding how efforts toward that may be undertaken, I think it would be good to discuss it with him and make that too a subject. Because students do not now come to school eagerly to study all the subjects that already exist. If only that subject were introduced, (loud laughter, applause) would not all the students come eagerly to school? With that hope, I say that we shall consider Balu Mahendra's suggestion; it is a good suggestion.

It has become very late. As far as this place is concerned, friends from Gujarat, belonging to North India but living in Tamil Nadu, are conducting a function here. For our sake, understanding our difficulty, they themselves came forward. Though they are from the North, with a concern that those in the South did not show (loud applause), as though advising me, “Do not speak of North and South any more,” they generously gave us this place and said they would conduct their own programme after this event. Not only that, they have also presented here a cheque for more than one lakh rupees for the Chief Minister's Relief Fund. I unfolded that cheque and counted how much it was. Because—

## PDF 337 / printed p.336

it was all ones: one lakh eleven thousand one hundred and eleven.

When I looked closely at the cheque, Sivaji turned his face and asked with his eyes, “Has that treasurer's habit still not left you?” (Laughter) Because, as he mentioned here, he and I have not merely known each other for ten years or twenty years. As he pointed out, there was a time when we wandered the streets of Thanjavur, the streets of Thiruvarur, the streets of Kumbakonam and the streets of Chennai. We were playful boys. At the time when Arignar Anna brought all of us together and accepted us as younger brothers, and when we decided to carry out ideological propaganda through the arts and worked for it, Sivaji and I took part together in movement propaganda and in several plays.

There were many plays such as *Thookku Medai* and *Vazha Mudiyathavargal*. It was while acting in those plays that, when a play was staged at Sankarankovil in Tirunelveli district to raise cyclone-relief funds for Thanjavur district, Sivaji and I went there. We arrived in the morning. Those who organised the play had not set up make-up rooms beside the stage—

## PDF 338 / printed p.337

Not knowing what to do, we put on our make-up at the place where we were staying, set out from there, and when we reached the place where the play was to be staged, there was no way to reach the back of the stage. We had to pass through the people who had come to watch the play. Sivaji, I, and the other actors covered our heads and crossed the area where the people had gathered to reach the stage. We acted.

After we had acted, the people who conducted the play truly made us cover our heads in shame. Taking away the surplus amount of 4,000 rupees that had been obtained from the play — the remaining amount from the play conducted for the cyclone-relief fund — the organiser disappeared. We did not know how we were to return. We who had staged *Vazha Mudiyathavargal* — “Those Unable to Live” — became people unable to return home.

It was then that friends Thangappazham, K.V.K. Sami and Sivasami, who had come to see the play, took us with them and said, “At least so that you can return to Chennai, stage a play in Thoothukudi.” We advertised it in the morning, staged the play in the evening, and with the amount left over from it, returned to Chennai. In such a testing time, such a painful time, cooperating with me in this movement, as Anna's—

## PDF 339 / printed p.338

younger brother and as a student of Periyar, stood my dearest friend Sivaji. That love and affection have not dried up. They will not dry up. You saw here his open-hearted speech as proof that they will never dry up at any time.

I had intended to come here and speak happily. But he made me shed tears and then asked me to speak. All of you know with what surge of emotion I came here, and that it was brother Prabhu who, supporting me by the hand, was able to bring me before this microphone. I had become that much a mass of emotion. He asked me to take his years.

As Kamal pointed out, rationalists cannot accept that, because no one can take another person's years. If you bless both me and Sivaji to live for many years, then all of you seated here too are people who have received such blessings. You too are people who have received such gifts and appreciation. You too have sat as the central figures at functions like this. Therefore, to bless someone, “May you live for many years,” means that even after a person's natural end, that person's name should remain in the minds of the people for many years. (Loud applause) My dearest friend!

## PDF 340 / printed p.339

My friend from the early days! Sugar candy bestowed upon the art world, treasure-house, Sivaji! Many people do not know whether you and I meet often or not. Because no report of a “meeting” appears in the newspapers, interpretations keep circulating in the country that there are all sorts of differences between us. Beyond all those interpretations, you and I are one. Two forms, one heart. We are, in that sense, one life. This very morning I happened to see a commemorative volume issued in memory of my mother Anjugathammal. In it, in an article Sivaji wrote about my mother, he says: “Is Anjugathai different? Is my mother different? For both me and Kalaignar M.K., there is only one mother. That mother is Anjugathai.” In the same way, Sivaji's mother is my mother; my mother is his mother. We are both children of Mother Tamil. We are your brothers.

At this sweet, rousing function, Karnataka Chief Minister J.H. Patel, who has come here from Karnataka, remarked that the statue looks older than I do. But whenever he writes me a letter, he writes, “My Dear Old Man, How are you.” That is how he writes in English. I do not know whether someone saw that letter wrongly and made the statue like this. (Loud laughter) Why did we invite the Karnataka Chief Minister here? Why has he been invited—

## PDF 341 / printed p.340

After the one thing that we kept calling and calling for, but which would not come, has finally come, (loud applause) should we not invite the person who gave it? That is why we invited him. On behalf of this organising committee, I convey my thanks to him and to Minister Anand, who came with him.

I know very well what kinds of difficulties the organising committee took on to conduct this function. I know very well how hard they worked. But their effort was not wasted. I had given the date saying that this function could be held on the 20th itself. When K.R.G., E.V.M. Saravanan, Chintamani Murugesan, the others, and Bharathiraja met me, they said the 27th would be convenient. I told them then, “The monsoon season is approaching. Therefore, hold it on the 20th itself.” They insisted, “No, it must be this date.” I wish to point out that at least from this they should understand that sometimes they must listen to what I say too.

But if this function had been held at the originally specified place, in the open air at Nehru Stadium, the function could not have taken place today. Because of nature's fury, because of the falling rain, the function could not have been held.

To those who helped this function take place here instead of there, my—

## PDF 342 / printed p.341

heartfelt thanks. (Loud applause) Whatever obstacles may arise, Revolutionary Poet Bharathidasan said:

> “Even if there are stones of obstruction, smile saying, ‘There are broad shoulders.’”

Likewise, however many obstructing stones may arise, our friends in the art world have proved that they possess broad shoulders capable of crossing over them. To them, and to the distinguished people of the art world who felicitated me, if they are younger than I am, I hold their hands and pour out my tears of joy. At the feet of those older than I am, I shed the pearls of my tears, bow to them all, and offer my thanks.
