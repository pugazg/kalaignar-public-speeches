## O Woman of the Press!

Only those who have run a journal truly know how difficult it is—the problems, obstacles and pressures involved. The DMK's growth in Tamil Nadu came despite newspapers that tried to keep us in darkness.

Bharathidasan called the newspaper a woman born in the heart of enlightened people and a ray of light in darkness. I once wondered why he chose the image of a woman.

## PDF 186 / printed p.185

The answer, I suggest, lies in Valluvar's description of womanly virtue: self-protection, loyalty, worthy speech and freedom from weariness.

A newspaper too must first preserve itself. That requires sound management, understanding readers, presenting ideas effectively, attracting circulation and avoiding the delays and weaknesses that can destroy a publication.

## PDF 187 / printed p.186

It must also preserve the principles for which it exists. “Guarding worthy speech” means not merely defending whatever words one happens to print, but maintaining standards in what one says.

A newspaper needs restraint, dignity and beauty as well as energy and intelligence. Vulgar language or verbal ugliness cannot be excused merely by claiming boldness.

## PDF 188 / printed p.187

A newspaper must also avoid discouragement. Sales may fall; attacks may come; but weariness cannot be allowed to defeat the publication.

I recall the early days of Periyar's *Kudiyarasu*, once known as the green-cover paper. Some readers were so afraid of being seen with it that they hid it inside another newspaper.

Periyar would say that even if nobody bought it, he would write it, print it and read it himself.

## PDF 189 / printed p.188

That determination helped *Kudiyarasu* grow to thousands of readers, spread Self-Respect ideas and carry Periyar's thinking across the country. It contributed to creating the rationalist political climate we see today.

So there can be no surrender to fatigue. A journal that gives in to discouragement cannot march boldly in the field of journalism.
