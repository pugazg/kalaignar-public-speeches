## Ilango Adigal (4)

Some may feel that politics enters speeches at an Ilango Adigal festival. Since Ilango himself wrote about public and political life, I do not consider such discussion improper.

Speakers here linked present political conditions with Ilango's literature. I also welcome the Muthamizh festival conducted by friends from the old Congress, though I wish such occasions would devote more attention to Tamil, *Silappathikaram*, Valluvar and Sangam literature.

## PDF 173 / printed p.172

I fully agree that nurturing Tamil is not the monopoly of the DMK. Congress too has produced writers, poets and speakers who served Tamil, and our movement has honoured such figures.

That same generosity should exist across parties: wherever a person serves Tamil, literary merit should be respected irrespective of party.

## PDF 174 / printed p.173

We honour Bharati each year and also honoured Bharathidasan when he died. Language must not become a field for party jealousy.

It is wrong to claim Tamil belongs only to one movement, but it is equally wrong to invent that accusation merely as a pretext to attack another movement at a Tamil festival.

Those who ask whether the DMK or Tamil Arasu Kazhagam has the qualification to speak of Muthamizh should first ask the same of themselves.

## PDF 175 / printed p.174

I use humour to expose shallow claims to literary authority. If one reads Ilango's invocation mechanically, one might absurdly answer that after praising the sun and moon he next praised Mars. Likewise, Ilango's poetic image of fish, bow and dark cloud could be misunderstood as though the word “car” referred to an automobile.

Such jokes point to the danger of criticising others' Tamil without understanding the literature.

The annual festival should not merely repeat praise of Ilango; it should lead us to commitments.

## PDF 176 / printed p.175

Those commitments should include ending caste, removing bitterness and building genuine unity.

*Silappathikaram* is rich enough that different people can claim Ilango as speaking to their own concerns. I even joke that, at a reform marriage, one could point to the elaborate rites of Kovalan and Kannagi and ask whether ritual alone guaranteed a happy life.

When the epic says Senguttuvan went north “to inscribe the goddess of chastity,” Ilango's language contains the deeper image of making the goddess endure as an inscription in stone.

## PDF 177 / printed p.176

The epic holds together the wealth of Tamil, the land, Tamil society and the heroic traditions of the Chera, Chola and Pandya realms.

We celebrate Ilango not simply to praise a great author but to gain emotion and awakening from the work.

## PDF 178 / printed p.177

Those emotions should not remain within one hall. Each person should carry them to others until the whole Tamil population understands its literature, history and self-respect.

Silambu Chelvar asked whether Tamil lives because the Tamil lives, or the Tamil lives because Tamil lives. I say the two are inseparable. Language and people must protect one another.

## PDF 179 / printed p.178

I compare it to milk and the milk-pot: the pot is needed to preserve the milk, but an empty pot has no purpose. So we must protect both Tamil and the Tamil people.

I recall political resistance to Tamil as a medium of instruction and the irony of some later celebrating Tamil publicly.

I also remember Sankaralinganar, who died after a long fast demanding the name Tamil Nadu, and the refusal of authorities even to allow proper mourning.

## PDF 180 / printed p.179

When we cited *Silappathikaram* for the word “Tamilakam,” opponents still pretended the literary evidence did not exist.

Today this land bears the name Tamil Nadu.

And yet they ask us, “What have you done for Tamil?” Tamil Mother, forgive them.
