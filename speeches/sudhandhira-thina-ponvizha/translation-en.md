# Independence Day Golden Jubilee

Karunanidhi opens by recalling that in 1974 he had argued that elected State Chief Ministers should have the right to hoist the national flag on Independence Day. Twenty-three years later, during the Golden Jubilee year of independence, he thanks the people of Tamil Nadu for giving him the opportunity once again to hoist the flag at Fort St George.

He cautions that fifty years after independence, India should not assume that complete freedom has already been achieved. Quoting Bharati's lines on slavery, caste and religious conflict, he asks whether the poet's challenge remains painfully relevant even in the Golden Jubilee year.

Karunanidhi invokes the sacrifices behind independence, including Thillaiyadi Valliammai and Gandhi, and asks what reward the country has given those who won freedom. He says Gandhi's final appeal might be understood as a plea not to worry about him but to protect the freedom that had been won.

The speech then turns to poverty and social inequality. Karunanidhi contrasts the nutritional advice available in wealthier societies with the reality of poor families in India struggling merely to silence hunger. He describes the hardship of agricultural labourers, weavers, industrial workers, fishers, miners, washermen, barbers and many other working people whose lives have not yet felt the “breeze of happiness.”

He argues that economic development cannot succeed if population growth overwhelms planned resources. Alongside development, the government and public must also confront black money, smuggling, tax evasion, adulteration, hoarding, corruption, violence, caste hatred and religious fanaticism, which he describes as social cancers.

Karunanidhi praises the survival of Indian democracy through crises and recalls older democratic traditions in Tamil society, including evidence from the Uthiramerur inscriptions. He says local democracy has been revived and local bodies are gradually receiving funds and powers. Government policy, he adds, is directed toward basic needs such as safe drinking water, roads, electricity, medical care, food, clothing and shelter.

He then announces measures for freedom fighters. Tamil Nadu already provides a monthly pension of 1,500 rupees, and the State will match the benefit announced by the Union Government for freedom fighters during the Golden Jubilee year. He also refers to memorial measures associated with V. O. Chidambaranar, Kakkan, Viswanatha Das, Kattabomman, the Maruthu brothers and Sundaralingam.

A substantial part of the address announces sentence remissions for prisoners: one year for those serving more than ten years; one year for those serving between five and ten years; four and a half months for those serving between one and five years; release in specified cases after half a sentence for those serving between three months and one year; and shorter remissions for other categories. Karunanidhi states that 554 prisoners will be released immediately and around 3,300 will benefit from sentence reductions.

He closes by saying these announcements are only drops of honey, not jars of honey. The government, he says, must revolve around the problems of oppressed, backward, most-backward and middle-class people, employees and workers. The final pledge is to protect unity, sovereignty and social justice, following the ideals associated with Gandhi, Anna, Periyar and Kamaraj.
